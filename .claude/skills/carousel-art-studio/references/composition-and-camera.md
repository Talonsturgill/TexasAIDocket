# Composition and camera

## Compose the thumbnail first

At 432 pixels wide, detail collapses before structure. Begin with three flat values and ask:

- Is the main silhouette recognizable with type hidden?
- Is one region dominant, one subordinate and one quiet?
- Does the eye enter at a deliberate point?
- Is the crop active, or is the subject politely centered?
- Does negative space create pressure, scale or anticipation?

Make six postage-stamp grayboxes before polishing one. A successful graybox should still carry
the visual thesis in grayscale.

## Name the camera

Every dimensional frame needs a camera note:

```text
Vantage:
Lens / projection:
Camera height and tilt:
Target:
Horizon:
Near / middle / far planes:
Crop and intended offscreen continuation:
Why this camera tells the claim:
```

Useful choices have meaning:

- low wide: monumentality, exposure, instability, distance;
- long lens: compression, congestion, stacked relation;
- macro: material evidence, hidden mechanism, fragility;
- orthographic: authority, comparison, system legibility;
- plan: territory, allocation, pattern;
- cutaway: concealed structure or process;
- tilted/oblique: tension, transition, competing planes.

Do not pick a dramatic lens because it looks dramatic. Pick it because perspective changes the
reader's understanding.

## Build depth before texture

Use at least three legible spatial layers where depth matters:

1. foreground occluder or scale cue;
2. subject plane;
3. ground/environment;
4. atmosphere or distant field when useful.

Separate layers by overlap, scale, focus, value, haze and motion direction. A stack of same-
value translucent rectangles is layering, not depth.

## Type has a spatial role

Choose one, deliberately:

- **monument:** type is a massive form the image negotiates;
- **aperture:** type or its reserve reveals/conceals the world;
- **instrument:** type belongs to a measurement or machine layer;
- **caption:** quiet type observes an image that already works;
- **counterforce:** type pushes against the subject mass;
- **evidence:** exact record language is materially embedded.

Avoid placing a self-contained text box into leftover space. Type and art can remain separate
for legibility while still sharing proportion, axis, scale or rhythm.

## Deck rhythm

Map the sequence before coding:

| Beat | Typical job | Spatial move |
|---|---|---|
| Stop | Singular hook | Extreme silhouette/crop, shortest copy |
| Orient | Establish world | Wider camera, literal anchor |
| Reveal | Expose mechanism | Cutaway, macro or layer separation |
| Measure | Make evidence felt | Data-bound form, comparison or scale shift |
| Turn | Change understanding | Reversal of value, vantage or behavior |
| Consequence | Human/institutional meaning | Specific object, place or absence |
| Resolve | Useful takeaway | Clearer space, decisive final image |

Not every deck needs seven beats and a nine-slide deck may split them. It does need contrast.
Alternate full/empty, near/far, dense/quiet, orthographic/perspectival and dark/light without
abandoning the same world.
