# Carousel craft — the platform science

What is measurable about how a LinkedIn carousel is actually consumed, separated from what is
merely believed about it. Where a claim here is a measurement, it says so. Where it is a
working assumption, it says that too, because a doctrine that cannot tell its evidence from its
habits will defend both equally.

## The frame

**1080 x 1350, four by five.** The tallest aspect the feed will render without cropping. Going
taller does not buy more attention, it buys a crop somebody else chose.

**A slide is read at about 390px wide on a phone**, which is 0.36 of what you are drawing. This
one number drives most of what follows. Design at full size, judge at 432px, and if a decision
only survives at full size it has not survived.

**The first frame is a thumbnail before it is a slide.** It competes in a feed against faces,
video and outrage. What wins there is not cleverness, it is contrast and one legible idea.

## Type at feed scale

| role | size | why |
|---|---|---|
| hook display | 120 to 170px | the only thing readable in a scrolling feed |
| headline | 60 to 110px | the claim |
| body | 32px and up | the sentence under the claim |
| floor | 24px | below this it is texture, not text, and QA warns |

**80px safe margins**, and keep roughly 150px clear top and bottom where platform UI sits.

**Contrast is judged at the worst point, never the mean.** Type over a gradient can average
10:1 and still fall to 2:1 somewhere along its run, and the eye finds that spot immediately.
This is a measurement the QA harness makes, not a matter of taste.

## The swipe

A reader swipes for one of two reasons: the frame promised something it has not delivered yet,
or the last frame changed what they thought. Everything else is momentum they brought with them
and will spend.

- **Nine slides is the working length.** Long enough for a turn, short enough to finish.
- **A deck needs one turn**, the frame where understanding changes. A deck without one is a
  list, however well drawn.
- **Density must vary.** Nine slides at the same weight reads as flat no matter how good each
  is. Where does it breathe?
- **The last frame lands or it stops.** These are different, and a reader can tell.

## What this product's decks are for

Not engagement. A record. The reader's question is the one the site is built around:

> Is anything being decided near me, and can I still say something about it?

A deck that answers that beats a deck that performs insight. If a comment window is open, the
date belongs on a slide, in house style, and probably in the caption too.

## The evidence is the art

The strongest slide in a record deck is almost always the document, read closely: the actual
figure, the actual map, the actual filing, drawn so a reader sees what it means. A stock
illustration of "artificial intelligence" is worth less than one honest measurement charted
well.

This is also the only kind of art that cannot be produced by a machine that has not done the
work, which is exactly why it is worth doing.

## Working assumptions, labelled as such

These are not measured here. They are what the craft literature and general practice suggest,
and they are held loosely until this product's own corpus can test them:

- A cover that states a number outperforms one that states a topic.
- A caption's first two lines matter more than the rest, because the platform truncates there.
- Decks posted with the source block as a first comment keep the caption clean without losing
  the citation.

**When this product has shipped enough decks to measure any of these, the measurement replaces
the assumption and this section shrinks.** A doctrine that only grows is a doctrine nobody is
checking.
