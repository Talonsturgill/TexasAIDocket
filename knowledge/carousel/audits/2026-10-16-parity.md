## Differences that explain the rework

Alaska has a multi-material height-field renderer in `assets/js/akbench.js` and lets a treatment
carry a measured scale or evolving motif through different physical representations. Texas
required at least six GPU scenes, five skies and a single recurring modeled hero. That rule
was broader than the defect it originally prevented. In Texas no. 52 the judges repeatedly
charged the kit car, unreadable water and repetition of no. 50's San Antonio night flood world.
Alaska no. 87 instead built its energy figures directly into a material ruler at one scale.

Texas no. 52 named frame 3 as its hardest subject, but the accepted audition covered 1, 4 and 9.
The audition was then reviewed six more times as shared sources and copy changed. Its record
did not preserve a separate bound judgement for each unchanged image. The new plan selects
the hardest frame and the review retains only identical source, image, asset and plan inputs.
An independent art director reads the actual native and feed images before six more frames
depend on the treatment.

Texas no. 52 used three pixel passes, two flow passes and three three-judge panels. Alaska's
record counts its revisions across the creative passes and used one scorer before and after
its craft cycle. Texas retains the final panel because its documented August 19th experiment
found fabricated claims missed by a single scorer. The new upstream sequence uses two pixel
polish passes and one flow pass after the harder audition. Hard factual, render and legibility
failures still require repair and fresh verification. The three scoring rounds, score weights,
craft floor and release ladder are unchanged.

Texas's compressed calendar passed its PSNR floor but lost a layout component at feed size.
CI first exposed that after the PR opened. The shipper now checks the WebPs against the same
layout gate while the reviewed originals still exist, then selects lossless WebP for the
affected frame. Its regression test replays the real October 16th calendar and verifies exact
pixel preservation.

## Implementation and activation

`txbench.js` adapts only Alaska's renderer from commit
`03da46445a18974351cc6e74835679b3de2c89c8`. It inherits the Texas chassis light, refuses conflicting
light and invalid geometry, reports measured height, materials, cast coverage and render area,
and avoids applying a second tone curve. Equal-height carrier pixels cannot shadow themselves.
The print and depth checks accept this physical route while retaining their spatial-scene
checks. A flat field, an import without a render and a small decorative relief do not qualify.

The daily master, director and critic briefs, engine skill, rubric descriptions, illustration
doctrine and CI all point to the same studio contract. `studio_clock.py` records real phase
timestamps across compaction. The routine archives those measurements before opening its PR.
No schedule, external routine setting, model pin, published deck, ledger history or generated
site file is changed by this repair.

The active trigger reads `prompts/daily_routine.md` from GitHub `main`. Source changes become
active only after their PR passes the checks on its current head and merges. The three native
render studies demonstrate the new renderer; they are not a newly shipped daily carousel or
proof of a faster complete run. Compare the next three ordinary runs using their archived
phase clocks, PR intervals and artwork scores. Report weekly upgrades and source outages
separately.
