## 0. THE RULE THIS DOCUMENT IS WRITTEN UNDER

The strongest material here is **chronology**, and chronology is where credibility is won or lost.

A man appointed to the PUCT by the Governor in 2021 is registered for Google in 2026. The state's
former statutory ratepayer advocate is registered for a data center land company. An energy
analyst widely quoted as an independent critic of the PUCT registered as a Google lobbyist in
June.

**Every one of those is a documented sequence and none of them is a transaction.** Publish the
dates, the filings and the URLs, and let the reader do the arithmetic. A publication that asserts
the exchange loses the story to a lawyer. A publication that publishes the sequence keeps it
forever.

## 1. How Texas lobby disclosure actually works, and where it goes blind

- Registration is required at either 2026 threshold: compensation over **$2,290**, or covered
  expenditures over **$990**, due within five days of the triggering communication `[DOC]`.
- **Compensation is a bracket, not a number.** An exact figure is required only above
  **$1,144,330** `[DOC]`.
- **7 of 8,183 registrations report an exact dollar amount. The other 8,176 report a bracket**
  `[DOC, computed]`.
- **95.5 percent (7,812) are filed as "PROSPECTIVE"**, meaning anticipated compensation under a
  contract, filed that way expressly so the registrant never has to amend `[DOC]`.
- **Hospitality recipients are hidden below a daily floor.** No naming required unless spending
  over **$132.60 on one person in one day**, or over **$110** on a gift. Events to which all
  legislators are invited need no attendee list `[REP]`.
- One 2025 lobby team spent **more than $46,000 on food and drink for officials, staff, family and
  guests and named no recipient**, implying every charge stayed under the floor `[REP]`.
- A former TEC chair described the mechanism on the record: **"They have 15 different credit cards
  in the deal to make sure that it's all below the limit"** `[REP]`.
- **In 2025 the Legislature reduced the fine for former lawmakers who lobby illegally** `[REP]`.

**The registry tells you who is hired by whom. It tells you almost nothing about how much.** That
is a finding, not a limitation to apologise for.

## 5. The absence findings, each with what was checked

**These are the best material in this document.** Each is a checked absence, not an assumption.

**A. No hyperscaler or data center developer operates a Texas corporate PAC, with one exception.**
All 2,451 registered PACs were searched for Google, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle,
OpenAI, Anthropic, Apple, Nvidia, Tesla, SpaceX, X Corp and every developer in the registry. The
exception: **"Forge the Future Project with Meta," ID 00090684, registered January 23rd, 2026 at a
Fremont, California address.** Meanwhile **Vistra, NRG, Calpine, CenterPoint, Oncor, El Paso
Electric, Constellation, Energy Transfer, Exxon, Chevron, Occidental, Devon, Diamondback and
ConocoPhillips all maintain Texas PACs**, several for two decades.

**The incumbent power industry built PAC infrastructure over twenty years. The AI industry arrived
through the lobby door in a single January.** `[DOC]`

**B. ERCOT has zero registered lobbyists, and the agency influence layer is essentially
invisible.** Searching 3,946 clients for "ERCOT" or "Electric Reliability Council" returns
nothing. Worse: **the entire 2026 lobby record contains only two agency docket designations,
statewide**, both filed by one person. The annual figure ran 13 to 38 in recent years. **The PUCT
and ERCOT proceedings where interconnection and cost allocation are actually decided leave almost
no lobby paper trail at all.** `[DOC]`

**C. The local-government lobby is outgunned, and its own hired guns are shared with the other
side.** Data center developers plus hyperscalers plus AI labs field **83 distinct registered
lobbyists**. TML, the Texas Association of Counties, Farm Bureau, Cattle Raisers and the Texas
Wildlife Association field **35 combined**. That is **2.37 to 1**. Against generators and utilities
(**205 distinct**) the local side is outnumbered **5.9 to 1**. And **Will McAdams, Michael Toomey,
Janiece Williams and Brian Yarbrough are registered for the Texas Association of Counties and for
Google, Lancium, Tesla or Oracle at the same time.** `[DOC, computed]`

**D. Texas has no data center trade group of its own.** No Texas-specific association appears among
3,946 registered clients. **The industry did not build a Texas institution. It rented an Austin
shop that also carries FedEx, Ford and Uber.** `[DOC]`

**E. The $4.2 million in AI-aligned super PAC spending in the 2026 primary cycle does not map to
any AI-named Texas PAC.** No PAC with "artificial intelligence" in its name is registered. Four
2026-registered candidates for that money, to be tested against the campaign finance file:
**American Power PAC** (Austin, Feb 6th), **Invest in Tomorrow Coalition PAC** (Burbank, Feb 3rd),
**Tech4TX** (Austin, Apr 29th), and Forge the Future Project with Meta. `[DOC + REP]`

**F. Texas Competitive Power Advocates, the generator trade group most cited in grid coverage,
registers exactly one lobbyist.** Its members lobby individually and at scale instead. Vistra alone
fields 37. `[DOC]`

**G. The progressive policy counterweight is four people.** Every Texan registers 4 against Texas
2036's 19 and TPPF's 15. **Combined conservative and business-aligned think tank lobby: 34.
Progressive: 4.** `[DOC]`

## 6. The influence path for a 500 MW data center in an unincorporated county

Grounded in the real 2026 fights.

**Step 0, before anyone local knows.** Land is optioned through an LLC. **Neighbors find out from
surveyors and property records, not from an agenda. NDAs bind the local officials doing the
negotiating.** Documented, not inferred `[REP]`.

**Step 1, the grid, where the real decision is.** Interconnection goes to ERCOT and the
transmission provider. As of May 2026 the large-load queue stood at **439 GW, about five times
peak, roughly 89 percent data centers** `[REP]`. **This step generates almost no public influence
trail** (finding B).

**Step 2, the county.** A Chapter 312 abatement goes to commissioners court. **The county has no
zoning and effectively no power to say no.** Brazoria County voted an abatement down and told
residents to call their legislators. **Hill County passed a moratorium and rescinded it after a
developer sued for $100 million** `[REP]`.

**Step 3, the school district.** The abatement value sits in the school M&O line. **Texas is losing
about $3.2 billion in sales tax revenue over two years to the data center exemption alone**, and
Abbott has now proposed repealing it `[REP]`.

**Step 4, water.** Groundwater conservation districts are the only permitting body with real
teeth. **93 water-related clients and 145 registrations in 2026, most of them individual districts
acting alone. There is no unified water counterweight** `[DOC]`.

**Step 5, the fight moves to Austin within days.** A county or city acts, and a state senator
requests an AG opinion or threatens preemption litigation citing HB 2559 and the Death Star law.
**San Marcos is the test case, and it is a home-rule city, one of 352 — the only category of Texas
local government with a plausible legal path** `[REP]`.

**Step 6, who is retained.** For the project: the developer's lobbyist, the hyperscaler tenant's
team of 6 to 10, the Data Center Coalition's five, the generator's bench of up to 37, the county's
own EDC, and a property-tax firm on the abatement. Against or for conditions: TAC and TML at 10
each **whose registrants include people simultaneously registered for Google, Lancium and Tesla**,
Farm Bureau at 3, the local groundwater district, and unpaid volunteer coalitions.

**Step 7, where money enters.** **Not mostly as corporate PAC money** (finding A). Texas has no
contribution limits for state office, so the **individual-donor channel dwarfs the PAC channel**
and stays invisible until the 1.04 GB campaign finance file is parsed.
