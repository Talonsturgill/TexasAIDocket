# Texas power families — who actually holds power, and how to write about them safely

Compiled 2026-08-11 from ProPublica (including Nonprofit Explorer 990 data), the Texas State
Historical Association, the Texas Ethics Commission, Texas Legislature Online, the Land Report
100, and Wikipedia used as a finding aid rather than a source of record.

**The research pass could not reach Texas Monthly, the Texas Tribune, Transparency USA or
Forbes.** Those are the four outlets that matter most on this subject, so several figures below
arrive secondhand. Treat this as **a structured research map with verified anchors**, not a
publication-ready reference.

## 2. Names on buildings versus actual power

**Say it plainly.** These are historical: **Murchison** (personal bankruptcy 1985, one of the
largest in US history), **Nelson Bunker Hunt's branch** (destroyed by the silver corner —
$10m CFTC fine, trading ban, Chapter 11 in 1988), **Wyatt** (Oscar Wyatt died October 2025 at
101; Coastal was dismembered; he pleaded guilty in 2007 to Oil-for-Food payments and served a
year), **Bush** (over in Texas — George P. Bush lost the 2022 AG primary to Paxton and no Bush
holds statewide office), and **Bass** (extremely rich, owns Fort Worth's civic core, **not a
factor in state politics**).

**The nuanced case is Cullen.** The name is on half the University of Houston, which makes it
look historical. **The Cullen heirs are the tenth-largest landowners in the United States and
control the second-largest private US coal reserves.** Do not let the buildings fool you.

## 3. Old money and new money want different things

| | **Old money** (Bass, Hunt, Kinder, Perot, Moody) | **New money** (Dunn, Wilks) |
|---|---|---|
| Target | general elections, governors, institutions | **Republican primaries** |
| Vehicle | direct giving, chambers, university and museum boards | 501(c)(4)s, scorecards, **owned media** |
| Goal | stability, access, permission to operate | **ideological conformity of the legislature** |
| Party relationship | funds the party's nominees | **funds challenges to the party's incumbents** |
| Legacy | a building, a park, a museum | a legislature |

**The asymmetry:** old money needs the establishment's cooperation; new money does not. Dunn does
not need a seat on a museum board. He needs 51% of a low-turnout March primary electorate, and
Texas's absence of contribution limits makes that purchasable by one person.

**This is not new.** H.L. Hunt bought radio in 1951 (*Facts Forum*, *Life Line*). Harold Simmons
funded Swift Boat in 2004. The Dunn and Wilks model is the third generation of the same Texas idea.

**But note the exception that matters most to us: SB 6 and TRAIGA — the bills that actually
govern our beat — came from the institutional energy and business wing (King, Schwertner,
Capriglione), not the movement wing.** The movement wins primaries; the establishment still
writes the energy and technology code.

## 4. The split estate — the most important structural fact on our beat

**In Texas the surface estate and the mineral estate are routinely severed, and the mineral
estate is DOMINANT.** The mineral owner has the implied right to use as much of the surface as is
reasonably necessary to develop the minerals, **regardless of who owns the surface and without
their consent** **[DOC]**. The accommodation doctrine is the surface owner's only real limit, and
it is narrow.

Why this decides stories:
- **Wind, solar and data centers are all surface uses.** A developer who leases the surface can
  have a drilling rig placed in the middle of the project by a mineral owner who was never a party
  to the lease and owes them nothing.
- **A $2 billion campus cannot be relocated around a drilling permit.** SB 6 now requires large
  loads to demonstrate **site control** — which means very little if the mineral estate underneath
  is severed and hostile.
- **The families who benefit most hold surface and minerals unsevered**, which is
  disproportionately the old ranching families who never sold.

**Practical rule: whenever a Texas data center or renewables project is announced, the first two
questions are who owns the surface and who owns the minerals. They are frequently different
people, and county clerk deed records answer both.**

## 5. Land does not vote the way politics does

- **Kenedy Ranch** leased 9,600 acres for **Gulf Wind — 84 turbines in phase one, 2009** **[DOC]**
- **Waggoner Ranch under Kroenke** runs **14,000 cattle, 1,100 producing oil wells and a 367 MW
  wind farm** simultaneously **[DOC]**
- **King Ranch objected** to its neighbor's wind project on migratory bird grounds **[DOC]**

**Do not assume large landowners are uniformly pro-renewable.** The biggest one in Texas
litigated the other way. The politics is ideological; the land economics is not.

And the Texas renewable buildout was launched by **SB 7 in 1999, signed by Governor George W.
Bush**. **SB 20 (2005) set a 10,000 MW target for 2025. Texas hit it in 2010, fifteen years
early** **[DOC]**. Any framing of "Texas versus renewables" that ignores this is wrong.

## 6. The universities are energy counterparties, not observers

**The Permanent University Fund holds about 2.1 million acres across 21 West Texas counties**,
endowed by the 1876 constitution with land considered worthless. The **Santa Rita No. 1
discovery on May 28th, 1923** changed that. The Available University Fund distributes **two-thirds
to the UT System and one-third to Texas A&M** **[DOC]**.

**UT and Texas A&M are themselves oil and gas landlords in the Permian Basin.** When a UT or A&M
institute publishes on energy policy, the PUF is in the background.

**The open question worth a public information request:** University Lands controls **surface as
well as minerals** across 2.1 million acres of exactly the geography where cheap land, stranded
gas, wind, solar and transmission converge. **Whether it is doing surface leasing for renewables,
water or data centers could not be verified** **[NV, HIGH PRIORITY]**. It is subject to the Texas
Public Information Act.

## 7. Andrews County — the wildcard

**Waste Control Specialists** holds **the only privately owned US license to dispose of Class A,
B and C low-level radioactive waste**, on a 14,000-acre site **[DOC]**. Founded by Harold Simmons,
sold to J.F. Lehman in January 2018.

**In *NRC v. Texas*, decided June 18th, 2025, 6-3, the Supreme Court held that only parties to the
NRC's licensing proceeding may seek judicial review.** Texas and **Fasken Land and Minerals** — a
284,000-acre Midland family — lost on that procedural ground **[DOC]**.

Cheap land, Permian gas, a permissive local politics toward nuclear-adjacent industry, and a
Supreme Court ruling clearing the procedural path. **If small modular reactors plus data centers
happen anywhere in Texas first, Andrews County is a strong candidate.**

**And there is no Wikipedia article on Fasken.** A West Texas land-and-minerals family that took a
nuclear siting fight to the Supreme Court and lost on standing, with almost no coverage. That is a
story sitting on the table.

## 8. Campaign finance — the data is public and machine-readable

**Texas has no contribution limits for most state races.** That is the structural fact behind
everything above.

The Texas Ethics Commission publishes a **bulk CSV database**:
`https://prd.tecprd.ethicsefile.com/public/cf/public/TEC_CF_CSV.zip` — **last updated August 10th,
2026** **[DOC]**. Two different record layouts apply depending on report ID range; both are
documented.

**Caveats to state whenever we use it:** only reports filed *with the TEC* are there — **local
filers (city, county, school board) file locally and are not in the bulk data**, which means much
of the school-board activity ProPublica documented is absent. Judicial reports only go back to 2016.

**And the enforcement reality: as of March 2026 roughly 726 individuals, including district
attorneys and judges, owe more than $800,000 in unpaid TEC fines, and the Attorney General has
declined to pursue enforcement since 2020** **[DOC]**. **Texas has disclosure without
enforcement.** That is documented, not editorial.

## 9. Handle with care — claims that circulate and are not well sourced

- **"Tim Dunn sold CrownQuest to Occidental for $12.4 billion in December 2023."** Wrong on
  entity, date and implication. The entity sold was **CrownRock L.P.**, a joint venture with Lime
  Rock Partners; **CrownQuest Operating is separate and continues**. Announced December 2023,
  **closed August 2024**. **Read Occidental's 8-K on EDGAR before writing anything.**
- **H.L. Hunt and the Kennedy assassination.** A large conspiracy literature exists. **None of it
  is adequately sourced.** Hunt's documented record — funding *Facts Forum* and *Life Line*,
  financing segregationist candidates — is the story.
- **"Tim Dunn is a Christian nationalist."** Asserted by Texas Monthly and by scholars quoted in
  ProPublica; **Dunn publicly rejected the label in an April 2024 op-ed**. Always attribute both.
  Never assert it in our own voice.
- **Any causal link between a donation and an official act.** Dan Patrick's campaign received
  $3 million from Defend Texas Liberty and he presided over Paxton's acquittal. Both are
  documented. **The inference is the reader's. Do not write it as a finding.**
- **Wilks net worth "$1.5 billion each"** — a 2013 Texas Monthly figure, thirteen years stale.
- **Autry Stephens "worth $25.9 billion"** — appears to conflate deal value with net worth.
- **"The Four Sixes was won in a poker game"** — **explicitly denied by Burnett and his
  descendants.** The name is the cattle brand.
- **Foundation "revenue" on a 990 is not grantmaking and not assets.** The Moody Foundation's
  **$1.43 billion FY2024 revenue** is almost certainly an investment-gain year. Do not report it
  as money given away.
- **Net worth figures generally.** Always write "Forbes estimated X at $Y as of [month, year]."

## 10. Legal exposure to think about before publishing

**Most of these people are living private citizens**, and public-figure status is not automatic.
Tim Dunn is arguably a limited-purpose public figure for political-spending coverage; that does
not extend to his family, his church, or his business conduct.

**Attributing motive is the highest-risk act on this beat.** "Dunn gave $X to Y" is safe. "Dunn
gave $X to Y **in order to** obtain Z" is an assertion about someone's mental state we cannot prove.

**And a fact about our operating environment: Energy Transfer obtained a $666.8 million jury
verdict against Greenpeace in 2025**, including a defamation finding **[DOC]**. That is not a
reason to soften true reporting. It is a reason to have every Energy Transfer sentence sourced to
a filing, a transcript or an on-record statement.

Texas's anti-SLAPP statute, the Texas Citizens Participation Act, generally protects journalism
but **has been narrowed since 2019**. Have counsel confirm the current scope.
