# Greg Abbott — the record this beat needs

Compiled 2026-08-11. Evidence labels: **[DOC]** primary source or official filing, **[REP]**
credible journalism, **[CON]** sources disagree, **[UNV]** could not confirm.

**Nothing marked [UNV] may be published.** Several widely repeated claims about Abbott are

## 1. The three things a reader should take

1. **The data center intervention is real in effect and vague in text.** The August 3rd, 2026
   audit order stopped ERCOT's Batch Zero study cold, and contains **no completion deadline, no
   pass or fail criteria, and no cited statutory authority** `[DOC]`.
2. **He is being attacked from both directions at once.** Trump called the Texas posture a
   "mistake" (August 7th to 10th); the San Antonio Express-News editorial board called it
   "busywork" that "shuns action" (August 6th). **That crossfire is the best available evidence of
   where he actually sits.**
3. **He is doing it in the tightest race of his career.** No non-partisan public poll has shown him
   ahead by double digits since June.

## 2. The 2026 data center sequence, with what each document does and does not say

### June 10th, 2026 — the one with deadlines `[DOC]`

A directive **letter** to PUC Chairman Thomas Gleeson and ERCOT CEO Pablo Vegas. Core requirement:
the PUC must require data centers to **"fully fund the costs of electric infrastructure needed to
serve their operations, preventing those costs from being passed on to residential ratepayers."**

Two hard dates: **July 17th** for a joint PUC and ERCOT memorandum, **July 31st** for PUC action to
reduce residential transmission costs. He pledged to seek codification next session, which means
the 90th Legislature convening January 2027.

### July 24th, 2026 — seven claimed actions, zero measurables `[DOC]`

**Verified: the release contains no deadlines, no numerical targets, no dollar figures and no
measurable commitments.** Every item is a process description, and the verb tenses give it away:
"requiring", "adopting", "begun", "developing". Item one promises data centers will "contribute
toward meaningfully reducing residential electric bills" **with no quantification of
"meaningfully"**.

**Fairness point that must travel with the criticism:** several of these describe genuine dated
regulatory actions that had already happened. **ERCOT's board endorsed the Batch Zero rule changes
on June 4th and the PUCT approved on June 18th**, and the process began in January and February
2026, months before the June 10th letter. Whether the letter caused the actions or claimed credit
for them is a **genuine open reporting question**, not a settled one.

### August 3rd, 2026 — the audit order `[DOC]`

Five audit categories: incentives received; on-site generation versus grid dependence; water
consumption, sources and cooling technology; community protections (noise, lighting, setbacks,
traffic); ownership and controlling interests. Abbott: projects failing requirements **"must be
denied connection to the Texas grid."**

**What it does NOT contain: no completion deadline, no pass or fail criteria, no cited statutory
authority, and the word "moratorium" never appears.**

**The operational effect was immediate and total.** ERCOT halted its Batch Zero transmission
planning study, suspending review of interconnection requests of 75 MW or larger.

**The timing nobody should miss: ERCOT began Batch Zero with 205 GW of load on August 3rd, 2026,
the same day the order stopped it** `[REP]`. Context in the release: roughly **474 GW** of
interconnection requests, more than five times Texas peak demand, about **90 percent from data
centers**.

### August 10th to 11th — the compliance campaign `[DOC]`

Rebranded as **"Governor Abbott's Data Center Standards"**: pay for infrastructure, reuse water,
reduce electricity costs, protect neighborhoods, eliminate taxpayer reliance.

Companies announced as committing: QTS (Aug 7), Galaxy (Aug 8), **Meta, OpenAI and Oracle**
(Aug 10), Edge ConneX, Stream Data Centers, Equinix and Sailfish Investors (Aug 11).

**The caveat a fact-checked publication must carry: the releases contain no statement from the
companies themselves, no project details, no megawatt figures and no dollar figures.** They are
issued by his office, not jointly. **The governor's office controls the framing of a corporate
commitment entirely. Ask for the company's own language every time.** Blockspace Media put it
precisely on August 11th: "Meta backs Texas data center standards but leaves infrastructure
commitment unpriced."

### The cost of the pause `[REP]`

BNEF: the audit could delay **49.8 GW** and cost projects **up to $15 billion**, putting
**20 percent of the entire US data center pipeline** at risk of delay. Oncor is weighing impacts
on a roughly 300 GW pipeline.

## 3. The date that actually decides who pays, and it is not his

**SB 6 (89R, signed June 20th, 2025, effective immediately)** is the statutory backbone `[DOC]`.
Authors King and Schwertner, with Democratic coauthors, committee votes 10-0 and 12-0.

- New PURA 37.0561: standardized large-load interconnection criteria, **PUC threshold 75 MW** at a
  single site.
- 39.170: transmission-voltage large loads must install **curtailment-capable equipment**; ERCOT
  may direct curtailment after exhausting market services, with 24-hour notice.
- 39.169: behind-the-meter net-metering arrangements require ERCOT notice and **PUC review within
  60 days**.
- **Cost allocation: the PUC must evaluate whether the four-coincident-peak (4CP) methodology
  appropriately allocates wholesale transmission costs, and must amend its rules by
  DECEMBER 31st, 2026.**

**That is the hardest date on the Texas data center calendar. It is statutory, it predates all
three of his 2026 directives, and it lands eight weeks after the election.**

**The pattern this reveals, and it is the through-line of the whole beat: Abbott's binding water
and energy commitments come from the Legislature. His data center commitments come from letters.**

## 4. The AI councils — three bodies, and the confusion is why this was unverifiable

This resolves the open item carried in the plan since the first research pass.

**Body 1 — AI Advisory Council (HB 2060, 2023).** Four Abbott appointees named February 23rd,
2024 `[DOC]`: John Bash (Quinn Emanuel), Mark Stone (CIO, Texas A&M System), Dean Teffer (IronNet),
**Angela Wilkins (Executive Director, Rice's Ken Kennedy Institute)**.

**Body 2 — Public Sector AI Systems Advisory Board.** Eight appointees named November 6th, 2025,
terms expiring September 1st, 2027 `[DOC]`. **Six are state agency CIOs and CFOs. Two are vendors:
a CGI VP and a Snowflake regional VP.**

**Body 3 — the Texas Artificial Intelligence Council under TRAIGA (HB 149, 2025).** Seven members
appointed **jointly by the Governor, Lieutenant Governor and Speaker**, so he does not control it
alone. **Advisory and non-binding: it cannot create binding regulations.**

**No evidence was found that the TRAIGA council has been seated** `[UNV]`. No appointment release,
no coverage of named members. **TRAIGA took effect January 1st, 2026, and seven months later the
council meant to advise on it appears unnamed. The absence is the story.** Note a source
discrepancy on size, seven versus ten; **trust the statute and read HB 149 directly.**

### What TRAIGA actually does `[DOC]`

**He signed it; he did not champion it, and there is no evidence he shaped it.** It is
**Capriglione's bill**, not an Abbott emergency item, passed committees 13-0 and 11-0, signed
20 days after transmittal with no documented ceremony.

Four prohibitions on private actors, **all requiring intent**: behavioral manipulation toward harm,
systems intended *solely* to infringe constitutional rights, **intentional** unlawful
discrimination (**disparate impact alone is expressly insufficient**), and CSAM or unlawful
deepfakes. Government entities face more, including limits on social scoring and biometric
identification. **The original bill was substantially narrowed**: impact assessments were cut back
to apply only to governmental entities.

Safe harbors for adherence to the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework**. A **DIR-administered
regulatory sandbox**, 36-month terms, shielded from AG enforcement while participating.
**Enforcement is the Attorney General's, exclusively**, with a 60-day cure period and penalties
from $10,000 to $200,000, up to $40,000 a day continuing.

**Assessment: a light-touch, intent-based, AG-enforced regime with a sandbox and a NIST safe
harbor. Closer to an innovation statute than a regulatory one.**

**"TRAIGA 2.0" appears in a May 27th, 2026 client alert, indicating amendment. What changed could
not be determined** `[UNV]`. **This may be the most important Texas AI statutory change of 2026 and
it is an open assignment.**

## 5. How he exercises power, which explains the method

Texas is constitutionally a **weak-governor state** by 1876 design. **Abbott's power is
accumulated, not inherited**, and ProPublica documented the accumulation in detail in October 2022.

- **Emergency powers as ordinary governance.** The clearest tell: he used a **2017 Hurricane
  Harvey declaration, renewed for nearly four years, to keep the state plumbing board alive after
  the Legislature abolished it** `[DOC]`.
- **Regulatory pre-clearance (2018).** He directed agencies to submit proposed regulations to the
  **governor's office** before public release. **This is the least-covered and arguably most
  important lever on this beat: utility and environmental rulemaking passes through his office
  before it is public** `[DOC]`.
- **The courts.** He has appointed **five of the nine Texas Supreme Court justices** `[DOC]`.
- **Money against defectors.** Rep. Lyle Larson (R) proposed **limiting gubernatorial board
  appointments for donors giving more than $2,500**; Abbott moved against him in the 2018 primary
  `[DOC]`. **That is the fairest available framing of the donor-appointment question: a sitting
  Republican legislator thought it serious enough to legislate against, and paid for it.**
- **Limits are real.** He lost a year-long voucher fight to rural Republicans before winning later.
  The PUCT is proceeding with contested 765-kV transmission over property-rights conservative
  objections, prompting complaints that bureaucrats are "ignoring Abbott" — **useful evidence that
  his directives do not automatically bind his own agencies.**

**Verdict: the characterization of executive-directive governance is sustained, and the August 3rd
order is a textbook instance. A letter, no statute cited, immediate operational effect.**

## 6. Appointments, and two corrections about who he controls

**The Governor does NOT appoint ERCOT directors.** Under SB 2 (2021): 12 directors, 9 voting,
**eight independent members chosen by a Board Selection Committee**, plus four ex officio (ERCOT
CEO, OPUC Public Counsel, PUCT Chair, one PUCT Commissioner). His influence is **indirect**, through
the PUCT commissioners he appoints who sit ex officio and whose commission has jurisdiction over
ERCOT. **Who appoints the Selection Committee could not be verified** `[UNV]`; check PURA 39.151
before publishing.

Two things a reporter should notice about the current board: the chair is **a former Republican
congressman** and the technology committee chair is a **tech private-equity executive**, so it has
been reconstituted with political and corporate-governance profiles rather than utility engineers.
And **the ratepayer advocate on the board, Benjamin Barkley, is a former lawyer in the Office of
the Governor** `[DOC]`. That is a fact, not an allegation, and it is relevant whenever OPUC's
position on data center cost allocation is reported.

**PUCT** is up to five commissioners, six-year staggered terms. **Chairman Thomas Gleeson was the
PUCT's own executive director with 15-plus years inside the agency; Commissioner Courtney Hjaltman
came from the Office of Public Utility Counsel, the statutory residential ratepayer advocate**
`[DOC]`. **That is an unusual composition for a commission often assumed to be industry-captured,
and it may explain why the PUCT has been receptive to a ratepayer-protection framing.** The
commissioner appointed October 2025 could not be named `[UNV]`.

**TCEQ's current roster could not be retrieved** `[UNV]`. **This is a real gap**: for a beat
covering data center water, backup generation air permits and gas plant permitting, that roster
matters as much as the PUCT's.

**The Nueces River Authority is an appointments story and the sharpest documented contradiction on
this beat** `[DOC]`. **Abbott appoints all 21 board members and designates its president**, and can
remove them for inefficiency or neglect. As of mid-2026 the authority was near financial collapse:
desalination funding months from running out, three contracts expired without renewal, a CFO
warning it could run out of money by end of August 2026. **He publicly threatened to "take over and
micromanage" Corpus Christi while applying little public pressure to the authority whose entire
board he appoints.**

## 7. Corrections to claims we would otherwise repeat

- **The tort reform criticism, stated accurately.** The 1984 settlement was an inflation-indexed
  structured settlement paying **$14,000 monthly plus $400,000 every three years as of 2013**
  `[DOC]`, disclosed by Abbott himself in 2013. **Any specific lifetime total is `[UNV]`** and
  circulating figures are extrapolations, not court records. His case was a **non-medical liability
  suit, a category that remains uncapped in Texas today**, so the caps he backed would not have
  applied to it, and the bulk of a catastrophic-injury recovery is economic damages the caps do not
  touch. **"He capped the damages he collected" is false. "He benefited from a tort system he then
  worked to restrict" is defensible.** Publish the second, never the first.
- **The lawsuit counts measure different things.** **31** lawsuits against the Obama administration
  during his **AG tenure**; **at least 44** spanning AG tenure **through his first term as
  governor**. Do not conflate them.
- **CUBI biometric settlements are Attorney General actions, not Abbott's.** Meta $1.4 billion
  (July 2024) and Google $1.375 billion (May 2025) `[DOC]`. **CUBI dates to 2009, before his
  governorship. Do not credit these to him.**
- **Uri and renewables.** He publicly **blamed renewable energy** in the storm's first days while
  the failure was overwhelmingly gas supply, wellhead freeze-offs and unweatherized thermal plants
  `[DOC]`. **This is the most durable factual criticism of his crisis performance and it is well
  sourced.** The fair counterpoint also belongs in the record: SB 3 weatherization, the SB 2 ERCOT
  governance overhaul and the reserve buildout are real, and there has been no Uri-scale failure in
  five subsequent winters. Whether that is policy or weather is genuinely contested.
- **His posture during the Paxton impeachment is `[UNV]`.** He is widely described as
  conspicuously silent, and **no contemporaneous statement or non-statement could be sourced**.
  Verify before characterizing.

## 8. Working with his office, practically

**How he announces.** Almost everything enters the world as a **press release on gov.texas.gov**.
On this beat the pattern is distinctive: **the directive is a letter to agency heads, announced by
press release, with the underlying letter not always posted.** No signing ceremony for TRAIGA.

**Site mechanics, which matter for a collector** `[DOC]`: roughly 4 to 5 items a day, heavily
weighted to appointments. **There is no working pagination and no site search** (`?page=2`,
`/page/2`, `/search?q=` and `/sitemap.xml` all fail). The category endpoints do work:
`gov.texas.gov/news`, `/news/category/press-release`, `/news/category/appointment`. **Post URLs are
predictable slugs**, `gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-<headline-slugified>`, occasionally
suffixed `-8-10` when two posts collide on a day, **so a URL can often be guessed from a headline.
There is no visible archive, so archive aggressively.**

**Public information requests: the record is bad and it is specifically documented** `[DOC]`. A
2025 request for Abbott-office communications with SpaceX, Tesla, X and Neuralink employees drew a
**$244.64 processing fee**, then opposition from both his public information coordinator **and
SpaceX's lawyers**, then an AG ruling mostly siding with Abbott, then **1,374 pages released with
approximately 1,200 entirely blacked out**. What was readable: incorporation documents, committee
agendas, grant information, and a happy hour invitation. **The competitive bidding exemption
covered 974 pages.**

**Expect a fee, an AG referral, third-party intervention, and the competitive bidding exemption
asserted over exactly the material this beat needs** — incentive negotiations, load commitments,
siting correspondence. **Request from PUCT, ERCOT, school districts and counties instead**, where
JETI agreements and interconnection filings surface more reliably.

Also checkable and worth pressing: **his JETI vote on a $16.8 billion project was not disclosed
even after the statutory decision deadline passed** on July 15th, 2026 `[REP]`.

**Where decisions actually surface first, and it is not his office:** PUCT open meetings and
Interchange filings, ERCOT board and stakeholder materials (Batch Zero notices, planning guide
revision requests), and Texas Legislature Online from January 2027 for the promised codification.
**RTO Insider, Utility Dive, POWER Magazine and Latitude Media have been consistently ahead of
general-interest outlets on this beat throughout 2026.**

## 9. Genuinely contested, report both sides

1. **Audit or moratorium?** His office says audit and never uses "pause". Reuters, Axios, Ars
   Technica, Fox News and the Houston Chronicle all led with "pause" or "halt". **The functional
   answer is that ERCOT stopped Batch Zero. The textual answer is that the document orders an
   audit.** Report both.
2. **Do the standards have teeth?** The Express-News says busywork; industry lawyers say open-ended
   risk; BNEF prices the delay at up to $15 billion. **Both can be true: high uncertainty cost, low
   substantive requirement.**
3. **Did the June 10th letter cause the PUCT and ERCOT actions, or claim credit for them?**
   Unresolved, and the dates make it a fair question.
4. **Whether the grid is fixed.** He says yes. A January 2026 expert piece said "we should be
   worried."
5. **Terafab's real size.** $16.8B first phase against a $55B figure, with reporting of a
   $38 billion gap against SpaceX's own filing.
6. **Operation Lone Star's cost and results.** Nearly every figure originates with the state, and a
   ProPublica, Tribune and Marshall Project finding that DPS counted **over 2,000 arrests with no
   link to the operation** contaminates the whole statistical series. **Do not republish OLS
   statistics as fact.**
