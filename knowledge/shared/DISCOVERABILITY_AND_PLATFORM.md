# PART 1 — AI DISCOVERABILITY

## 1. Corrections to the plan, which called for things that are now dead

| Thing | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **`FAQPage` JSON-LD** | **DEAD.** The FAQ rich result "is no longer shown in Google Search results", announced May 2026 and removed from the docs in June 2026. Before removal it was shown only for well-known authoritative government and health sites | **[V]** |
| **`SpecialAnnouncement`** | **DEPRECATED** as of July 31st, 2025 | **[V]** |
| **Google sitemap ping** (`/ping?sitemap=`) | **DEAD.** Announced June 2023. Use accurate `lastmod` plus the `Sitemap:` line in robots.txt | **[V]** |
| **Google Indexing API** | **Unusable for us.** It "can only be used to crawl pages with either `JobPosting` or `BroadcastEvent` embedded in a `VideoObject`". A docket page is neither | **[V]** |
| `Report`, `GovernmentService` | Valid vocabulary, **no documented consumer**. Harmless as an extra `@type`, but expect nothing | **[V]** |

**The plan's AI-discoverability wave must be rewritten around this.** FAQPage was named in it and would have been wasted work. It may still be worth emitting as clean machine-readable question-and-answer structure for LLM crawlers, but **nothing on the site may claim it does anything for search.**

## 2. llms.txt, honestly

The proposal is real, well specified, and authored by a serious person (Jeremy Howard, published
2024-09-03, last revised 2026-08-10). It is **a community proposal, not a standard**: no RFC, no
working group, no vendor commitment.

**Every adoption claim behind it is about publication, never consumption.** The evidence gathered
from the consumer side:

- **Google**, in its own AI-features documentation: "There are no additional requirements to appear
  in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary", and "There's also no
  special schema.org structured data that you need to add." **No mention of llms.txt anywhere**,
  including in its robots.txt spec.
- **Anthropic's** crawler docs name `ClaudeBot`, `Claude-User` and `Claude-SearchBot` and describe
  robots.txt as the control surface. **No mention of llms.txt.**
- **Perplexity's** docs name their bots and tell publishers explicitly how to be included.
  **No mention of llms.txt.**
- **OpenAI's** bot documentation was unreachable (503). **Unverified** `[X]`.

**No major AI crawler documents that it fetches or honors `/llms.txt`.** Chrome's Lighthouse
audits for its presence, so there is at least one automated reader, which makes it cheap hygiene
rather than a distribution channel.

**Publish one, because it costs a single generated file from a build that already holds the
sitemap in memory. Build no strategy on it, and never let the site claim it does anything.** That
claim would be exactly the sort of unverifiable assertion this project's rules exist to prevent.

## 3. The strategy the evidence actually supports

**Make the docket a first-class machine-readable dataset, and announce every change the moment it
lands.** Three parts, each with a named consumer.

**(a) `Dataset` JSON-LD, generated by `docket_build.py`.** Google Dataset Search is a documented,
currently operating consumer, which is more than can be said for llms.txt, FAQPage or
SpecialAnnouncement. Required `name` and `description` (50 to 5000 chars); include `license`,
`temporalCoverage`, `spatialCoverage` (Texas), **`variableMeasured`** naming the Grid Watch series,
`creator`, `version`, `identifier`, and a `distribution` block whose `DataDownload` points at the
raw JSON. Generated, never hand-written, so its numerals inherit `numeral_lint`.

Layer `NewsArticle` on deck pages, `BreadcrumbList` on the hierarchy, `Organization` once.

**(b) Publish stable versioned raw JSON and CSV, linked from every page that renders them.** This
is the part most publications skip and the part that matters most for citation. A crawler that can
fetch `ledger/docket.json` gets the whole record without parsing HTML, **and a model quoting a
number out of a JSON field is far less likely to mangle it than one reading a rendered table.** It
also closes the loop on the compute-not-generate law: the published artifact is the computation's
output, so any citation is verifiable by refetching.

**Uniqueness beats markup.** A fact-checked Texas AI decision record with claim ids does not exist
anywhere else, and that is the actual reason to expect citation.

**(c) Announce and permit.** IndexNow on every merge; accurate `lastmod` plus the `Sitemap:` line
now that the ping endpoint is gone; and **`Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-input=yes, ai-train=yes`**
in robots.txt, which is the only documented machine-readable way to say **yes** rather than no.
Verified in the wild on Cloudflare's own robots.txt.

## 4. The negative control, where most publications lose without noticing

**Do NOT block `Google-Extended`, `GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `Claude-SearchBot`, or `PerplexityBot`.**

- Google states outright that **"Google-Extended does not impact a site's inclusion in Google
  Search nor is it used as a ranking signal"** `[V]`. Blocking it forfeits Gemini training and
  grounding and buys nothing.
- Perplexity says directly: **"To ensure your site appears in search results, we recommend allowing
  `PerplexityBot`"** `[V]`, and notes PerplexityBot is "not used for training AI models".
- Anthropic ships three distinct tokens. **Blocking `ClaudeBot` alone does not block citation.
  Blocking `Claude-SearchBot` does.**

**For a publication whose entire purpose is to be the record people cite, a permissive robots.txt
is not a concession, it is the product strategy.** Say so in a comment block in the file, in plain
language. It costs nothing and it is the honest statement of intent.

**Before the final robots.txt ships, re-verify OpenAI's bot documentation** (503 this session). The
training-versus-citation split is exactly what the posture argument depends on.

## 5. IndexNow, and why it fits this repo specifically

Free, no account. Key is 8 to 128 chars hosted as `{key}.txt` at web root. Up to **10,000 URLs per
POST**, and submitting to one endpoint shares it across all participants: **Bing, Yandex, Naver,
Seznam, Yep, Amazon** `[V]`. **Google is not a participant** and is not mentioned anywhere in the
docs.

**It fits this build unusually well.** `docs/` is already a deterministic function of the ledgers,
so the set of URLs that changed on a merge is **computable from the diff** rather than guessed.
That is precisely IndexNow's input.

Traps: engines set undisclosed per-site daily thresholds, over-submission returns **429** and wants
a 10-minute wait, and submissions count against crawl quota. Bing's index is what several AI answer
engines read, which is the real reason to bother.

**No CI-usable structured-data validator API exists** `[V]`. validator.schema.org exposes none and
the old Testing Tools API is retired and covered only Mobile-Friendly and AMP. **The CI answer is
to assert required properties in Python against the JSON-LD we emitted**, which fits this repo's
gate pattern exactly, then use the free Google Search Console API's Enhancements report as the
post-hoc check on what Google actually parsed.

# PART 2 — THE PLATFORM

## 6. Compliance: free tiers that restrict commercial use

The consulting wing makes these real. **Treat as blocking.**

1. **Stadia Maps free plan: "Commercial use not allowed"** `[V]`. Unambiguous, stated as a property
   of the Free plan. **Do not use anywhere.** Protomaps or OpenFreeMap replace it with no
   compromise.
2. **ElevenLabs free plan.** The pricing page lists **"Commercial License"** as the capability the
   $6/month Starter plan adds over Free. It never says in words that Free forbids commercial use,
   and the ToS was unreachable. **Treat Free as non-commercial until someone reads the ToS.**
   Kokoro is free and Apache-licensed, so the question likely never needs asking.
3. **GitHub Pages.** Quoted: *"GitHub Pages is not intended for or allowed to be used as a free
   web-hosting service to run your online business, e-commerce site, or any other website that is
   primarily directed at either facilitating commercial transactions or providing commercial
   software as a service."* A public fact-checked record with a services page is **not** primarily
   directed at facilitating transactions, so today this reads as compliant. **It stops reading that
   way if the consulting wing adds checkout, gated client deliverables, or a hosted product on the
   same domain.** Write it down now, because the decision that trips it will not feel like a
   hosting decision when it is made. Cloudflare Pages free is the drop-in escape hatch.
4. **Nominatim.** Not a commercial ban, but **"Systematic queries... downloading all POIs in an
   area"** is prohibited outright and reselling geocoding results is bannable. **Use the US Census
   Geocoder instead**, which is keyless and US-only, and US-only is not a limitation here.
5. **Piper relicensed to GPL-3.0** (`OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl`; the original `rhasspy/piper` was
   archived 2025-10-06). Commercial use is permitted and producing audio does not infect the audio,
   but redistributing a derived work carries copyleft. Kokoro's Apache 2.0 sidesteps the analysis.

**Not free at all:** Plausible cloud (30-day trial, then $9/month), **Gemini TTS ("Not available"
on the free tier)**, OpenAI TTS.

**Verified clear for commercial use**, so nobody re-checks: Pagefind (MIT), Orama (Apache 2.0),
MiniSearch (MIT), **Kokoro-82M** ("deployed in numerous projects and commercial APIs"),
OpenFreeMap ("Yes"), Protomaps (ODbL), Census Geocoder, IndexNow, **Fraunces (OFL), Manrope
(OFL 1.1), JetBrains Mono (OFL-1.1)** — all three of our typefaces are clear — GoatCounter
("small-to-medium business ... is fine"), Cloudflare Turnstile, Workers, KV, R2.

## 7. Traps in the stack we already chose

| Service | Free tier | **The trap** |
|---|---|---|
| **Workers KV** | 100,000 reads/day, **1,000 writes/day**, 1 write/sec to the same key, 1 GB | **1,000 writes/day.** A read counter that writes on every pageview breaks at about 1,000 pageviews. **Must batch**: accumulate and flush on an interval, or shard counters and reconcile at build. **The single most likely thing to break quietly.** |
| **Supabase** | 500 MB db, 50,000 MAU, 500,000 edge invocations, **2 active projects** | **"Free projects are paused after 1 week of inactivity"**, and restore is a **dashboard action, not automatic**. Mitigation: one authenticated query per day from a scheduled job counts as activity. |
| **Buttondown** | Free to **100 subscribers**, **at most one email a day to the entire base** | The 100-subscriber wall arrives precisely when the discoverability work starts working. And **the docket alert lane and any future Grid Watch lane compete for the same daily send** unless they are separate lists. |
| **Cloudflare Workers** | 100,000 req/day, 100 scripts, **5 cron triggers per account** | **10 ms CPU** (not wall time) kills anything computational. **5 cron triggers** is low for a repo that wants scheduled watchers. Choose fail-open or fail-closed deliberately. |
| **GitHub Pages** | Site max **1 GB**, bandwidth 100 GB/mo soft, **10 builds/hour** soft | The **1 GB site cap** interacts directly with accumulating run artifacts, PDFs and video in `docs/`. This is why video artifacts go to **GitHub Releases** (2 GiB per file, **no total size limit, no bandwidth limit**, 1000 assets per release). |
| **GitHub Actions** | **Free for public repos** on standard runners | Larger runners bill even on public repos. **Scheduled workflows are best-effort: cron can be delayed or skipped, and a repo with no activity for 60 days has schedules auto-disabled.** That is the whole case for the dead-man switch. |
| **Turnstile** | 20 widgets, **unlimited challenges**, 7-day analytics | Almost no trap. Works on GitHub Pages. |

## 8. Adopt these

1. **IndexNow** — computable from the build diff, buys Bing and five others.
2. **`Dataset` JSON-LD** — the one structured-data type with a live documented consumer.
3. **`Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-input=yes, ai-train=yes`** — one line, the only way to say yes.
4. **Pagefind** (MIT) — fully static, no service, ~100kB at our page count, runs as a post-process
   over built `docs/`. Architecturally identical to the in-browser ask engine: index at build time,
   answer in the reader's browser, nothing sent anywhere.
5. **healthchecks.io** — 20 checks free, no card. See section 9.
6. **Kokoro-82M** — Apache 2.0, 54 voices, runs local. **Removes a vendor from the daily video's
   critical path entirely: no key, no quota, no price change, no terms revision, no outage.** For a
   routine that ships autonomously every day, that is worth more than a marginal quality edge.
7. **Google Search Console API** — free, 1,200 QPM. How we find out whether 1 to 3 worked, and the
   only free thing that reports what Google actually parsed.
8. **US Census Geocoder** — keyless, 10,000 per batch, returns tract, block and FIPS. **Produces
   measured coordinates from an authoritative source**, which satisfies the numerals law where a
   commercial geocoder's proprietary estimate would not.
9. **Protomaps PMTiles, Texas extract** — ODbL, commercial OK, and **it becomes a file we own** that
   nobody can rate-limit or discontinue. OpenFreeMap is the friendlier fallback and is also
   commercial-OK, but has one maintainer and states outright it offers no SLA.
10. **GitHub Releases for video** — keeps rendered video out of `docs/` and away from the 1 GB cap,
    inside the permissions boundary we already have.

## 9. The dead-man switch

**An ordinary monitor cannot do this job.** The failure to fear is not "the collector errored", it
is **"the collector never ran"**. A job that never started cannot report its own failure, and an
uptime check against the website returns 200 on a stale page. Only a monitor that alerts on the
**absence of a signal** catches it.

**healthchecks.io free tier, one check per daily collector.** 20 checks, no card. Cronitor is more
cron-native but caps free at 5 monitors; UptimeRobot and Better Stack are website monitors with
heartbeat bolted on.

Configuration that matters:

- **Period 1 day, grace 3 hours.** The grace must cover GitHub's real cron variance, not the
  schedule we wrote. One hour pages on noise and trains us to ignore it.
- **Use all three endpoints.** `/start` at the top, the bare ping on success, `/fail` on failure
  with `if: failure()`. This separates "ran and broke" from "never ran", **and only the second is
  silently losing a day.**
- **Gate the success ping on evidence, not on exit code.** This is the part that matters most.
  Ping only after asserting that the ledger gained a line dated today **and** that the collector's
  self-test passed. Otherwise a run that fetches nothing, writes its explicit unverified record,
  and exits 0 will ping green, **and we will have built a switch that lies.** Since a failed fetch
  writes an unverified record by design, **an unverified record must raise a distinct alert**: the
  series survived, but the day's measurement did not.

Three layers, because layer one shares fate with the thing it watches:

1. healthchecks.io ping from the collector workflow.
2. A second workflow at a different hour reading only the last line of the ledger and checking the
   date.
3. **A Cloudflare Worker cron** (we have 5 free) that fetches the **published** Grid Watch JSON
   from the live site and checks the newest date. This watches the **output**, from outside GitHub,
   so it survives an Actions outage **and catches "collected fine but the site never rebuilt"**, a
   failure the first two layers are blind to.

Two keep-alives worth adding to that same Worker: **one authenticated Supabase query per day** to
prevent the 7-day pause, and enough repo activity monthly that GitHub never auto-disables the
schedules at 60 days.

**Route alerts to the draft mailbox** named in `CLAUDE.md`, which a human already checks daily. An
alert that lands somewhere new does not get read. The address is deliberately not repeated here:
it belongs in one place, so a repoint is one edit.

**The honest limit:** none of this verifies the number is right, only that a record arrived. The
value-sanity gate (bounds and day-over-day delta against the model) belongs inside the collector,
in this repo, not in any vendor.
