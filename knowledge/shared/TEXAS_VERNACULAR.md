# Texas vernacular — drawing it so a Texan nods

Compiled 2026-08-11. **Incomplete: the research pass was truncated in delivery and only its
addenda arrived intact.** What is here is the highest-leverage material. A follow-up pass owes us
the regional detail sections (per-region light, vegetation, buildings, vehicles), the everyday
kit (courthouse square, main street, gas stations, ranch and oilfield equipment, clothing by
region and job), food, music and speech, and the full fauna and flora drawing notes. Marks:
**[V]** verified, **[U]** unverified, verify before drawing.

---

## 1. The sentence to build everything on

> **A Texan forgives a stylized drawing. A Texan does not forgive being told they live somewhere
> they don't.**

Style is free. Place is not. We can draw in any idiom we like, as long as the region, the light,
the vegetation and the people are right for the county the story is actually about.

## 2. The two decisions that matter most

**One: cast and set every piece regionally, on purpose.** Never default to a generic Texas. The
Panhandle and Houston must not share a green. A story set in Reeves County does not get Hill
Country limestone.

**Two: make the everyday utilities and the demographics honest.** Which brings us to the failure
mode most likely to happen in our specific pipeline.

## 3. Failure modes structural to a flat-vector, thick-outline engine

These are not general art notes. They are the specific mistakes a code-drawn SVG library will
make on its own unless something stops it.

- **Default character equals white man in a hat.** Vector libraries get built in the order they
  are needed, and the first character authored becomes the default reach forever. **Build the
  cast demographically FIRST, before any episode needs it**, so the easy reach is not the wrong
  reach. Recommended base set: a Hispanic woman engineer in flame-resistant coveralls and a hard
  hat; a Black woman executive in business dress; a South Asian data center technician; a white
  rancher in a straw hat and pearl snaps; a Vietnamese-American small business owner; a Black
  petrochemical operator; a Hispanic man in a norteño straw hat; a white woman in scrubs with a
  medical center badge.
- **Caricature drift.** In a thick-outline idiom, exaggerated lips, noses and skin values slide
  toward racial caricature without anyone intending it. **House rule: facial feature variation
  lives in shape language applied evenly across all characters, and skin tone is a fill value
  that never changes the line work.**
- **Symmetry.** Vector tooling pulls toward symmetry and real Texas objects are not symmetric. A
  hat crease is hand-shaped and lopsided. A mesquite is never balanced. A courthouse tower is
  centered but the square around it is not. A windmill fan is a full disc but the tail vane swings.
- **Too-clean silhouettes.** Add a rust drip, a dent, a leaning post, a missing letter. The
  aesthetic is **maintained but worn** — not new, not ruined.
- **Palette laziness.** One "Texas palette" across all regions is the tell.
- **The state silhouette as a crutch.** Reserve it for one recurring brand element. Not decoration.
- **Sunset gradients everywhere.** Texas has hard noon light, humid milky light, blue-norther
  slate light, and dust-brown light. Vary it.

## 5. Mapping the vernacular onto our actual beat

Our subject is data centers, the grid, ERCOT, oil and gas, water and AI companies. The visual
vocabulary has to serve that, not a tourism board.

**Grid and ERCOT** — a Panhandle wind farm with synchronized red aviation lights; a West Texas
transmission corridor with lattice towers marching to the horizon; a thermostat and a ceiling fan
in a Texas living room. The 2021 ice reference exists but is heavily used **[U]**.

**Data centers** — the honest siting is Abilene, the Permian, San Antonio, DFW and the Panhandle.
Draw a low windowless slab on caliche, a substation yard with switchgear and gantry structures, a
transformer bank, cooling units in rows, a fence, mesquite outside it, and **a pumpjack or a wind
turbine visible behind it**. That contrast — the newest industry sitting on the oldest landscape
— is our best recurring composition and it is true.

**Oil and gas with AI** — a Midland office tower on a flat plain; a worker in flame-resistant
coveralls holding a tablet.

**Water** — a drought-cracked stock tank with a bathtub ring; a center pivot; a playa lake.
**Water is the argument underneath most Texas energy stories and almost nobody draws it.**

**Policy** — the pink Capitol dome; the Great Walk's black and white diamond pavement **[V]**; a
committee room.

## 6. Animals drawn correctly, from what survived

- **Armadillo** — small, housecat-sized, and **they jump straight up** when startled **[V]**
- **Javelina is NOT a pig** — a collared peccary, a different family **[V]**
- **Roadrunner** — nothing like the cartoon **[V]**
- **Great-tailed grackle** — the parking lot bird every Texan knows, and the one most worth
  drawing because everyone recognizes it and nobody draws it **[V]**
- **Pronghorn** go **under** fences, not over. This single detail is why fencing devastated them **[V]**
- **Longhorn horn shape differs by age and sex** — check TLBAA breed guidelines before drawing **[V]**
- **Mesquite** — low, crooked, wide, lacy **[V]**
- **Bluebonnet** — the white banner spot turns magenta after pollination, so a real spike carries
  both **[V]**

## 7. Cultural handling

The truncated section owed us fuller guidance on Tejano, Black, Indigenous, border and colonia
representation. Until that pass is redone, the standing rules are the ones in
`TEXAS_HISTORY.md` section 9 and the character-library rules in section 3 above, plus:

- **Six Flags Over Texas is retired as a motif.** One of the six is the Confederate flag, and
  even the theme park of that name removed them in 2017 **[V]**. Use the Republic-era and current
  Lone Star only.
- **Lotería imagery and Day of the Dead iconography are appropriation, not shared culture.** Do
  not use them. Talavera tile geometry as a *pattern* system is fine.
- Texas has removed 31 Confederate monuments, more than any state **[V]**. Monument imagery is a
  live fight and not decoration.
