## 1. The number that settles our editorial posture

**University of Texas / Texas Politics Project, June 2026** (margin of error ±2.83%). *"Do you
support or oppose the construction of a data center in your community?"*

| | Support | Oppose |
|---|---|---|
| **All Texans** | **29%** | **56%** |
| **Republicans** | **42%** | **44%** |
| Independents | 17% | 62% |
| Democrats | 15% | 71% |

**Republicans are split 42 to 44 — essentially tied.** Data centers are not a partisan issue in
Texas. They are a **within-coalition** issue, and the coalition splitting is the governing one.

Compounding it: a Tribune analysis found the **majority of planned or under-construction
facilities sit in state House districts that voted Trump and elected a Republican in 2024**, and
**nearly half of planned data centers are in unincorporated areas** with no zoning power at all.

**What this means for the "Texas first, and AI is transformational" posture:** it is not a
contrarian position, it is the position that actually describes the electorate. A majority of
Texans are wary and a plurality of Republicans are too, while the state is building the largest
AI infrastructure concentration on earth. Anyone writing this as boosters versus doomers is
describing a fight that is not happening.

## 4. The tension nobody is reporting

**Abbott and Patrick are pushing in opposite directions.**

Abbott's answer is *make the data centers bring their own power*. Meanwhile **Patrick and
Schwertner are trying to block the transmission lines that would carry that power** — the
**$33 billion, three-line 765 kV Permian Basin Reliability Plan**, authorized by HB 5066 in 2023,
which passed near-unanimously.

| Who | Position **[STATEMENT]** |
|---|---|
| **Schwertner** | PUCT should **reject the applications**; the process needs "a complete overhaul" before they advance; will legislate in the 90th |
| **Patrick** | The lines are **necessary**, but "the current process being utilized by the PUC is broken and must be reformed" |
| **Kolkhorst** | Pause it. *"We voted on House Bill 5066, and I'm not sure we knew what that was going to become."* |
| **Sparks** (R-Midland) | Property-confiscation concern, **despite his district benefiting** |
| **Todd Staples**, TXOGA | Build it. West Texas paid for transmission everywhere else |
| **Oncor** | Proceed. "Timely implementation of these projects remains critical" |

**And a sitting Republican statewide official is attacking the governor from the right.** Sid
Miller, Agriculture Commissioner, on the Aug 3 order: *"Without legislative action, this
directive is all hat and no cattle — empty political rhetoric wrapped in meaningless fluff.
Texans need laws with teeth, not another press release."* He wants a special session. Abbott has
not called one.

## 5. The bills, with their actual votes

**HB 149, TRAIGA** — author **Capriglione**, Senate sponsor **Schwertner**. Signed June 22nd,
2025, **effective January 1st, 2026**. Committee votes: **House 13-0, Senate 11-0** **[VOTE]**.

Substance: disclosure when a **government agency** deploys AI; prohibitions on AI built to incite
self-harm or crime, on government social scoring, on unlawful discrimination, and on **biometric
capture for identification without consent**; a **DIR-run sandbox** up to 36 months; a
**seven-member Texas AI Council**. Enforcement is **exclusively the Attorney General's**, with a
**60-day cure period**, penalties of **$10,000 to $200,000**, and **no private right of action**.

**Two things not to get wrong:** every recorded committee vote was **unanimous and bipartisan**,
so there is no roll-call basis for calling TRAIGA partisan. And **exclusive enforcement changes
hands in January 2027** when a new Attorney General takes office. That is a live story.

**SB 6** — author **Phil King**, coauthor Schwertner. Committee votes **10-0** and **12-0**.
Signed June 20th, 2025, effective immediately. **Coauthors include Alvarado, Blanco and Eckhardt
— Democrats.** SB 6 is not a partisan artifact either.

**SB 819**, renewable siting and permitting — author **Kolkhorst**. Senate Business and Commerce
reported it **7 ayes to 4 nays**, the only contested committee vote in this set. **Passed the
Senate. Died in House State Affairs.**

## 6. Framing traps that will mark us as outsiders

- **Do not call this Republicans versus Democrats.** Republicans split 42 to 44 in their own
  communities. SB 6 was coauthored by three Democrats. TRAIGA cleared committee unanimously
  twice. The real cleavages are **House versus Senate**, **Abbott versus Patrick**, and **rural
  Republican districts versus Republican leadership**.
- **Do not say "the Legislature killed SB 819."** The **Senate passed it**. It **died in House
  State Affairs**. Naming the wrong chamber is the fastest way to lose a Texas reader.
- **Do not call Abbott anti- or pro-data-center.** In eight months he went from "epicenter of AI
  development" and a $40 billion Google announcement to a moratorium, while having taken a
  reported $2 million-plus from tech-linked donors. **Report the arc, not a label.**
- **Do not treat property rights as a conservative-only argument.** In 2025 it cut against wind
  and solar in SB 819. In 2026 the same Hill Country landowners are using it against transmission
  built for oil and gas. **Kolkhorst is on both sides of it**, and that is not hypocrisy, it is
  the argument working as designed.
- **Do not call the August 3rd order a moratorium without the caveat.** It does not reach
  projects with **on-site generation**, and it does not reach **non-ERCOT Texas** — El Paso has
  several large data centers. Its duration is undefined.
- **Do not assume a bill number equals an outcome.** HB 5066 passed near-unanimously in 2023 and
  produced a $33 billion fight in 2026.
- **Do not confuse cities and counties.** Counties generally have **no zoning power**. That is the
  whole ballgame for unincorporated siting, where nearly half of planned projects are going.
- **Do not report a company name from a tax filing as fact.** Meta took abatements in Fort Worth
  and El Paso as **Winner, LLC** and **Wurldwide, LLC**. Delaware does not require owner disclosure.

## 7. Local control is where the real fight is

- **February 2026** — Hood County rejected two moratorium attempts after **Sen. Bettencourt**
  warned commissioners they **lacked legal authority** **[STATEMENT]**
- **May 2026** — Hill County passed the state's first data center moratorium, then **rescinded it
  a month later after a developer sued for $100 million**
- **June 2026** — **San Marcos** became the first Texas city to ban them; a state lawmaker has
  already said he plans to challenge it
- **At least 34 Texas counties** have formally asked the state for more disclosure

## 8. How to actually get the records

- **Counties generally lack zoning authority; cities have it.** The single most important
  structural asymmetry on this beat.
- **Water data requires a Public Information Act request**, and **aggregate requests succeed more
  often than facility-specific ones**. Municipal providers refuse by citing a Utilities Code
  provision shielding individual customer accounts.
- **ERCOT treats project-level data as private** and releases aggregates in meeting slides — but
  it **has released county-level data to the Tribune and the Houston Chronicle**, so that ask is
  winnable.
- **TCEQ**: "permits by rule" for backup diesel generators and "standard permits" for gas plants
  **require no public participation**. The Federal Operating Permit may be the first public bite.
- **The Comptroller publishes all local incentive agreements — but uncategorized**, so you must
  already know the company name.
- **TDLR** is the underused backdoor: large construction projects register under the Elimination
  of Architectural Barriers Act, and the database carries owner, location and estimated cost.
- **The two structural obstacles are NDAs and Delaware shells.** Microsoft announced this spring
  it would stop using NDAs. Adrian Shelley of Public Citizen: *"There's now an incentive to
  conceal the fact that a project is a data center as long as that is practically possible."*

**Budget extra time for agencies.** PUCT did not respond to Tribune questions about the audit's
scope, or on the transmission fight.

## 10. The ten fights that define the 2027 session

1. **Who pays for interconnection**, and whether it lands on other ratepayers
2. **The $33 billion Permian transmission plan** — Schwertner wants the applications rejected
3. **The sales tax exemption** — Abbott says eliminate it; 147 qualifying projects, individual
   amounts secret by statute
4. **Mandatory disclosure and registration** — after **28 of 377 companies** answered the state
   water survey, which Rep. Brad Buckley called "pretty pathetic"
5. **Local control versus preemption**
6. **Emergency curtailment** — Gleeson wants PUCT authority to talk to data centers directly,
   which today must route through the utility
7. **Foreign ownership** — extending the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act to data centers
8. **Water**, with jurisdiction scattered across utilities, river authorities, groundwater
   districts and TCEQ
9. **Renewables siting, round two** — the SB 819 coalition is intact
10. **TRAIGA under a new Attorney General**
