# Texas language — the words, and the ones that give you away

Compiled 2026-08-11. This governs every written and spoken surface: captions, slide strings, VO
scripts, site copy, email. It is about **word choice**, not pronunciation (see
`TEXAS_PRONUNCIATION.md`) and not drawing (see `TEXAS_VERNACULAR.md`).

Confidence marks are the same set: **A** two sources agree, **B** one authoritative source, **C**
crowd-sourced only, **D** unverified, **⚠** genuinely contested.

---

## 1. The civic terms we will get wrong by default

These are the ones a publication covering Texas government will misuse in its first month, and
each error is the kind a Texan spots instantly.

**A county judge is an executive, not a judge.** `[C ⚠]` The Texas county judge presides over
commissioners court, sets the county budget, and is the county's emergency management director,
which is why they are on television during hurricanes. **They are not required to be a lawyer.**
In smaller counties they also hold limited judicial duties in the constitutional county court, and
that is where the confusion comes from. **Never write "a county judge ruled" without checking.**

**Commissioners court is the county's governing body**, not a court. `[C]` County judge plus four
elected commissioners. It is a legislative and executive body with a judicial sounding name. No
apostrophe in Texas usage `[D]`.

**The Railroad Commission does not regulate railroads.** `[C]` It stopped in 2005. It regulates
oil and gas, gas utilities and pipeline safety. Established 1891, the state's oldest regulatory
agency, three elected commissioners. **Explain this every time it appears**, because it confuses
Texans too.

**Precinct means three unrelated things** `[D]`: a commissioner's precinct, a justice of the
peace or constable precinct, and a voting precinct. They do not align. Say which.

**"The Rangers" has two referents** `[C]`. The Texas Ranger Division is DPS's investigative arm.
The Texas Rangers are the baseball club in Arlington. Disambiguate on first reference, always.

Other terms worth carrying:

| Term | What it is | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| **the Lege** | The Texas Legislature. Rhymes with edge. Insider shorthand | D |
| **sine die** | Session adjournment. **The Capitol convention is SY nee DY**, anglicized. Classical Latin marks you as an outsider | C ⚠ |
| **interim charges** | Topics assigned to committees between sessions. **The interim is where next session's bills are actually built**, so these are news | D |
| **the calendar** | The scheduling mechanism. **In Texas the calendar is the weapon**, and killing a bill by calendar is the normal way bills die | D |
| **the Big Three** | Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Speaker. **The Lieutenant Governor is unusually powerful**, often described as the most powerful office in the state | D |
| **the Comptroller** | Elected. **The revenue estimate is a hard constitutional ceiling on appropriations**, the single most consequential number in Texas budgeting | C ⚠ |
| **MUD** | Municipal Utility District. Issues bonds for water and sewer outside city limits, repaid by district property taxes. Ubiquitous around Houston. Said as "mud" | D |
| **appraisal district** | Sets values. **The taxing units set rates.** This split is the source of most Texas property tax confusion | D |
| **voter-approval tax rate** | The current statutory term. **"Rollback rate" is the older term and using it dates you** | D ⚠ |
| **recapture** | The formal name. **"Robin Hood" is the colloquial and political name** for school finance recapture | C |
| **the PUF and the AUF** | The Permanent University Fund goes to the UT and A&M systems **and to no other public university in Texas**, which is a permanent political grievance for Texas Tech, U of H and UNT | C ⚠ |
| **ISD** | Independent School District. Always attached: "Katy ISD" | C |
| **UIL** | Governs **far more than football**: one-act play, marching band, academic meets. Said "you eye ell" | C |

The Legislature meets **biennially for up to 140 days** in odd numbered years, 31 senators and 150
representatives `[C]`. Everything else is a special session, which only the Governor can call and
whose agenda only the Governor sets.

## 2. Energy and grid, the colloquial layer

**"The grid" always means ERCOT** unless stated. ERCOT serves more than 25 million customers and
about 90 percent of the state's electric load `[C]`. **El Paso, parts of the Panhandle and parts
of East Texas are not on it**, and getting that right is a credibility marker (see
`TEXAS_CITIES.md`, where it is a hard filter in the data model).

**ERCOT is used as a verb and an epithet** `[D]`. Texans blame ERCOT for things ERCOT does not
control, and that gap is itself a story worth telling once.

**"Uri" alone is sufficient** `[C]`. It means the February 2021 storm and grid failure, at least
246 dead, and it functions as a date marker: before Uri, since Uri. No explanation needed.

**Do not call Uri a rolling blackout.** `[C ⚠]` Load shed is the industry term for controlled
rotating curtailment. **In February 2021 the rotation broke down and the outages stopped
rolling.** People went days without power. The phrase is both technically wrong and locally
offensive.

**Frac versus frack is a register choice** `[D ⚠]`. The industry writes **frac**. General media
writes **fracking**. Use "fracturing" or "frac" when quoting the patch, "fracking" for a general
audience, and never mix the two inside one piece.

**Mineral rights versus surface rights is the single most important Texas property concept**
`[C]`. The estates can be severed, and **in Texas the mineral estate is dominant**: the mineral
owner may use as much of the surface as reasonably necessary. Surface owners frequently do not own
their minerals. This is structural to data center siting, wind leases and pipeline routing, and
it is the fact most often missing from national coverage.

## 3. Everyday vocabulary that localizes copy instantly

**Land.** A **tank** or **stock tank** is a pond, an earthen or steel rimmed water impoundment for
livestock, and never a vehicle `[C]`. This is the highest-value single item on the list. A
**playa** is an ephemeral closed basin that holds rain, not a beach, and the Llano Estacado is
dotted with thousands. **Caliche** is the hardened calcium carbonate crust, and also the crushed
material ranch roads are made of. An **arroyo** in West and South Texas is a **draw** in Anglo
ranch speech. A **motte** is an isolated stand of trees on open prairie.

**Direction words carry fixed meanings** `[D]`. "Out west" is west of roughly Abilene. "Down
south" means South Texas and the Valley, **not the American South**. "Back east" is anywhere east
of Texas.

**"The Valley" has exactly one referent** `[B]`: the Lower Rio Grande Valley, meaning Hidalgo,
Cameron, Willacy and Starr counties. It is a delta floodplain and not a geological valley. It
never means any other valley in Texas.

**"The South Plains" is Lubbock. "The Panhandle" is Amarillo.** Swapping them is a West Texas
tell `[D]`.

**Roads are a regional split that localizes copy in one word** `[D ⚠]`. The road paralleling a
freeway is **the feeder** in Houston, **the service road** or **access road** in DFW and Austin,
and **frontage road** in TxDOT's own documents. "The Loop" means 610 in Houston and 410 in San
Antonio, so **always name the number**. **FM** is Farm to Market, **RM** or **RR** is Ranch to
Market, and RM designations cluster west of I-35.

**Weather.** A **blue norther** is a fast autumn cold front that drops temperature sharply `[B]`.
TSHA notes the term is uniquely Texan but the claim that the phenomenon is unique to Texas "is
considered folklore rather than meteorological fact", which is exactly the kind of distinction
this publication should get right. Texans distinguish **sleet** (bounces), **freezing rain**
(the dangerous one, makes glaze) and an **ice storm** carefully, because the road consequences
differ.

**"The drought" takes the definite article** `[D]` and means the drought of record (1950 to 1957)
or the current one, depending on the speaker's age. Always "the one that broke us."

**Oilfield.** A **roughneck** works the rig floor, a **roustabout** is general labor usually on the
production side, the **tool pusher** is the rig boss, and the **company man** is the operator's
representative who outranks him in practice. **Produced water** comes up with the oil and is
brackish, and volumes rise as fields age. **Saltwater disposal** wells are associated with induced
seismicity in the Permian, which is a live regulatory story. **The patch** is the oilfield as a
place and a culture. **The Basin** is the Permian, where **Midland is the white collar side and
Odessa the blue collar side**, a distinction that is real and locally felt.

**Food.** The distinctions that carry social weight: a **kolache is sweet** and a **klobasnek is
savory**, the latter invented by Czechs in Texas, and calling a sausage roll a kolache in West or
Caldwell will get you corrected `[C]`. Brisket is ordered **moist or lean**, and **"fatty" is not
the counter term** `[D]`. **Burnt ends are Kansas City in origin** and calling them a Texas
tradition is a small but real error `[D ⚠]`. A **barbecue joint** is the term of respect; "BBQ
restaurant" signals you do not know the form, which grew out of meat markets rather than
restaurants. **Cream gravy is the only correct chicken fried steak gravy.** **Tex-Mex and interior
Mexican are different cuisines**, and using them interchangeably in Houston or San Antonio is
noticed.

**Speech features that are grammar, not error** `[C]`: **might could** and other double modals
(might should, used to could) are hedged, polite constructions. **Fixin' to** is an aspect marker
meaning about to and already oriented toward it, not merely going to.

**"Bless your heart" has a full range** `[C ⚠]`, and writing it as purely an insult is the
outsider version. Most commonly it is genuine sympathy. Then affectionate condescension. Then, yes,
a knife. Tone and audience decide.

**Generic "coke" is real but splitting** `[D ⚠]`. Younger and urban Texans increasingly say soda
or drink. Do not present it as universal present-tense Texas.

## 4. What not to say

### Instant outsider markers

| Do not write | Write | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "the Rio Grande River" | **the Rio Grande** | *Río* already means river. Flagged in every Texas newsroom |
| "Texas is basically one big desert" | Name the region | Texas holds pine forest, prairie, desert and subtropical delta. The most common factual outsider error |
| "Don't mess with Texas" as swagger | Nothing | **It is a TxDOT anti-littering slogan from 1986** `[D ⚠]`. Texans know this. Using it as a tough-guy motto is the tourist version |
| "Howdy" as a general greeting | Nothing | Genuine and institutional **at Texas A&M**. Elsewhere it reads as costume |
| "Big D" | **Dallas** | Dated and touristy |
| "Native Texan" for a transplant | "Texan", or the year they moved | **It specifically means born in Texas.** Misapplying it is noticed immediately |
| Calling DFW "Dallas" wholesale | **North Texas**, **DFW** | **Fort Worth's separate identity is a live sensitivity.** It is not a Dallas suburb. "Cowtown" from Fort Worth people is affectionate and safe |
| "the Valley" for anywhere else | the specific region | See above |
| "Texas gate" | **cattle guard** | "Texas gate" is a western Canadian term |
| "the Railroad Commission regulates railroads" | oil, gas and pipelines | Stopped in 2005 |

Also on the list, already in `config/brand.yaml` as hard-failed banned phrases: "everything's
bigger in Texas", "deep in the heart of", "the Lone Star State is no stranger to", "wild west",
"yeehaw", "saddle up". Plus **Texian** (a Republic-era word, not a modern self-description),
**cowpoke** and **buckaroo** (buckaroo is Great Basin, from *vaquero*).

### Dated, condescending, or offensive

- **"Illegals" as a noun.** Use "undocumented immigrants", "people in the country illegally", or
  "migrants". Texas politics uses loaded language on all sides and neutral construction protects
  the record `[D ⚠]`.
- **"Spanish" as a catch-all** for Hispanic Texans. It means from Spain.
- **"Indian" unqualified.** **The nation name is always the best choice**: Alabama-Coushatta,
  Ysleta del Sur Pueblo, Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas, Comanche Nation. Some tribal citizens
  use "Indian" self-referentially; a non-Native publication should not `[D ⚠]`.
- **"The Karankawa are extinct."** Contested and increasingly rejected. Descendants organize
  publicly as Karankawa Kadla. The extinct-tribe framing comes from older Texas textbooks.
  **Verify current language before publishing anything on Karankawa or Coahuiltecan peoples**
  `[D ⚠]`.
- **"Coahuiltecan" as a single tribe.** It is a grouping of diverse peoples, not one nation `[C]`.
- **Texas Indigenous history in the past tense.** **Three federally recognized tribes are in Texas
  today**: Alabama-Coushatta in Polk County, Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas at Eagle Pass, and
  Ysleta del Sur Pueblo (Tigua) in El Paso. The Lipan Apache Tribe of Texas holds state
  recognition; **federal status was not verified and must be checked before it is described**
  `[D ⚠]`.
- **"Remember the Alamo" as a punchline.** The Alamo's interpretation is actively contested in
  Texas, over whose story it is, over enslavement, and over the Tejano defenders.

### Hispanic, Latino, Tejano, Chicano

This varies by region, generation and politics, and it is noticed.

| Term | What the research shows | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| **Hispanic** | **In Texas this is the default and the majority preference**, noticeably more so than in California. **If we use one word statewide, use Hispanic** | C ⚠ |
| **Latino / Latina** | Widely accepted, more common on the coasts, second to Hispanic in Texas | C |
| **Latinx** | **Do not use in Texas general-audience copy.** Roughly half of US Hispanics were not aware of it, majorities prefer Latino or Hispanic, and one cited figure has 75 percent saying it should not be used. **It reads as an outsider marker to the audience it describes.** Use only when quoting someone who used it | C ⚠ |
| **Tejano** | **Not a synonym for Hispanic Texan.** It claims deep, often pre-1836 Texas rootedness. A recent immigrant from Michoacán living in Dallas is Mexican or Hispanic, **not Tejano.** Also a music genre | C ⚠ |
| **Mexican-American** | Safe and precise when accurate. **Do not apply to Texans of Salvadoran, Honduran, Colombian or Puerto Rican origin**, a growing share of urban Texas | C |
| **Chicano** | Generational and political. Proudly self-applied by Movimiento-era Texans and younger activists, **rejected by many in between**, especially in South Texas. **Never apply it to someone who has not claimed it** | C ⚠ |

**The operating rule:** use **Hispanic** as the general Texas term. Use **Mexican American**,
**Tejano** or **Chicano** only when it demonstrably fits the specific person or group. Avoid
**Latinx** except in direct quotation. **Where a community or individual has stated a preference,
that preference wins over any style rule.**

## 5. Is Texas Southern? Report the disagreement

Texans genuinely disagree, and **reporting the disagreement is more accurate than picking a
side**.

| Position | Basis | Where held |
|---|---|---|
| **Southern** | The Census Bureau classifies Texas in the South. East Texas is culturally Deep South: pine, Baptist, cotton and timber. Texas seceded | East Texas, historians of the nineteenth century |
| **Southwestern** | West Texas, the Trans-Pecos and the Panhandle are high plains and desert, with ranching and Mexican-American and Plains Indigenous history and no Southern agricultural pattern | West Texas, El Paso |
| **Borderlands** | South Texas and the Valley are bicultural and bilingual, organized by Spanish and Mexican history rather than Confederate | South Texas, the Valley, Laredo |
| **Its own thing** | Former republic, distinct legal traditions (community property, homestead, the dominant mineral estate), its own grid, its own myth | Statewide, and the most common answer |

Wikipedia's Hill Country entry notes the region "represents the border between the American South
and Southwest", which is the useful detail: **the transition runs through the middle of the
state.**

**Never assert flatly that Texas is or is not Southern.** Half the audience is lost either way.
The accurate framing is that Texas is in the South by the census and in East Texas by culture, and
that it runs out of the South somewhere around I-35 and does not agree with itself about where.
