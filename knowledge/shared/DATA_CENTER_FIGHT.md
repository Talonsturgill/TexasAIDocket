# Who is funding the Texas data center fight

Snapshot compiled 2026-08-11. **This is a live fight roughly four months old, so this document
dates fast.** Marks: **[DOC]** a filing or registry record actually fetched, **[REP]** credible
journalism actually read, **[UNV]** could not confirm.

---

## 0. The finding, and the framing that has to travel with it

**The pro-data-center side in Texas is organized through a Virginia 501(c)(4). The opposition's
named Texas groups have no legal existence at all.** Both halves are documented, and **the
asymmetry is the story.**

And the framing that keeps it honest:

> **Every faction in this fight is funded by an AI fortune.** Meta has pledged $65 million to
> state races. Leading the Future raised over $50 million in five months. Anthropic self-reported
> $20 million to a safety-side nonprofit. The Democratic Senate nominee's allied super PAC took
> $10 million from Reid Hoffman. **The distinguishing fact is that the local opposition, on both
> partisan flanks, is not funded by anyone at all.**

A piece that reports the Meta money without the Hoffman money and the Anthropic money is not
credible. Carry all of it.

## 1. America Connects, and what "Texas Connects" actually is

**[DOC] America Connects, EIN 99-3320283.** 501(c)(4), NTEE S01, 535B E Braddock Rd, Alexandria
VA. **IRS ruling date August 1st, 2025.** BMF revenue **$1,380,000**, assets $115,976. One return
on file, a 990-O for the period ending December 2024, filed April 3rd, 2026.

**[DOC] The IRS Business Master File contact name is "% ELECTION CFO"**, a campaign-finance
compliance firm, not a person or a program officer. **A social-welfare organization routing its
IRS correspondence through an election compliance shop is a documentable structural fact.**

**[REP]** Texas Connects, described as a nonprofit "dedicated to educating Texans about the data
center industry," **was put together by the Data Center Coalition** and mirrors Virginia Connects.
Its first ad was titled "Built for Texas" and claimed the facilities use "minimal water." The
Coalition did not answer questions about where the videos would air or what it expected to spend.

**[REP]** The umbrella is **America Connects**, spanning Virginia, Texas, Indiana, Pennsylvania,
Ohio, Georgia and Oregon "Connects" sites, **all linked by the same Google Ads ID**. Virginia
Connects spent roughly **$700,000 in FY2024** on mailers and texts, and per its Form 990 **Josh
Levi, the Data Center Coalition's president, is also Virginia Connects' principal officer.**

**[DOC] That link is independently corroborated from the primary record**: the IRS BMF entry for
the **Data Center Coalition, EIN 84-2248368**, carries care-of name **"% JOSH LEVI"** at a Leesburg
VA address.

**[DOC] "Texas Connects" is not a legal entity.** No IRS exempt-organization record exists. The
Texas franchise registry returns only an unrelated California-ZIP company. **The "nonprofit
dedicated to educating Texans" is a Virginia c4's Texas-facing brand.**

**[DOC] The obvious domains are dead ends.** `americaconnects.org` and `texasconnects.com` are
byte-identical 114-byte parked-domain landers. `texasconnects.org` resolves to an unrelated Austin
marketing agency. Worth noting because a reader who goes looking hits exactly these walls.

**One caveat that must not be stepped past.** `[DOC]` A Texas franchise taxpayer named **AMERICA
CONNECTS**, taxpayer ID 32105219029, Austin ZIP, exists in the Comptroller registry. **The public
JSON returns only name, taxpayer ID and mailing ZIP. That does not by itself prove it is the same
legal person as the Virginia c4.** The name and the beat match. The identity is not established by
the record read. **Do not close that gap in prose.**

## 3. The money into races

**[REP]** **Leading the Future**, an AI super PAC network **created in August 2025**, raised **more
than $50 million** through year end. Backers named: **Greg Brockman** (OpenAI co-founder),
**Joe Lonsdale** (Austin, Palantir co-founder), **Marc Andreessen** and **Ben Horowitz**.

**American Mission**, its appendage, was the most prolific AI-related spender in Texas
congressional races, **more than three quarters** of all AI-related PAC spending in Texas through
mid-March: roughly **$747,000** for a candidate who **previously served as chief lawyer for Elon
Musk's super PAC**, **$511,000** in TX-08, **$600,000** in TX-19. **At least seven Texas
congressional candidates collectively received more than $2.8 million** from AI-linked super PACs.

**[REP]** On the safety side, **Public First Action**, a bipartisan nonprofit **backed by
Anthropic, which self-reported a $20 million donation**. It does not disclose donors. Aligned PACs:
**Defending Our Values PAC** and **Jobs and Democracy PAC**.

**The naming tell, reported rather than inferred:** the groups "have otherwise shrouded their
connections to the industry, going by generic-sounding names such as Jobs and Democracy PAC and
Defending Our Values PAC, and running ads that do not mention the technology."

**[REP] State level, which is where the siting fight lives:**
- **Abbott has received over $2 million from people and companies linked to the tech and AI
  industries since last year.**
- **Meta plans to give $65 million this midterm cycle** to state politicians supporting the AI
  industry, in both Republican- and Democrat-led states, through new super PACs. Its national
  vehicle is the **American Technology Excellence Project**.
- **Forge the Future**, the Meta super PAC aimed at Republicans, **spent over $1 million in Texas**
  in the 2026 primary, including nearly **$174,000 for state Rep. David Cook**.
- **AI-aligned super PACs spent about $4.2 million in Texas this primary cycle, all but $150,000
  to Republicans.** **Attribution matters: that count comes from the Tech Oversight Project**, a DC
  nonprofit that supports regulating data centers, so it is an interested party's figure.
  `[DOC]` Tech Oversight Project is EIN 87-4209086, BMF revenue $910,105. It is small.

### The refusal, which is the most interesting fact on the pro side

**[REP]** **State Rep. David Cook rejected Meta's support** in a February 26th statement, and **his
attorney sent Meta a cease and desist letter the same day**, demanding it stop voter outreach on
his behalf because he had never spoken to anyone at the Meta super PAC and "does not want any
help." Cook has committed to filing legislation giving counties "reasonable authority" to regulate
data centers. **He beat an anti-data-center activist in that primary anyway.**

### The foreign-funding allegation

**[REP]** A cluster of May to August 2026 stories pushes a theory that data center opposition is
foreign or Chinese funded, including a congressional investigation request, an E&E News piece on
Republicans probing it, and an NPR piece headlined **"The theory taking the rich by storm: China

an allegation, name who is making it, and note it is unsubstantiated. The astroturf allegation
runs the other direction here.**

## 5. The conservative and rural opposition is the real political story

**[DOC] Texas Farm Bureau**, from its own publication. President **Russell Boening** wrote to Lt.
Gov. Patrick and Speaker Burrows urging a "balanced approach that protects landowners, agriculture
and the state's economy," citing "the demands data centers place on critical natural resources,
especially water and electricity, in rural regions already facing groundwater depletion, limited
surface water supplies and recurring drought." **TFB delegates adopted formal policy at the 2025
Annual Meeting supporting oversight of where and how these facilities are built.**

**A conservative membership organization with a formally adopted policy position against unmanaged
data center siting.** It cuts directly across the partisan map.

**[REP]** Other cross-cutting conservative opposition, on the record:
- **Greg Harrell, Hood County GOP chair**: *"We love liberty and love a lack of regulation. Data
  centers are taking advantage of it. They saw an opportunity."*
- **Rep. Wes Virdell (R-Brady)**, after a White House meeting: *"I left with the impression that
  our concerns fell on deaf ears."*
- **Rep. Helen Kerwin (R-Glen Rose)**, who demanded an "IMMEDIATE PAUSE," then: *"The word
  'regulation' scares me, as a grassroots conservative."*
- **[REP]** A June poll found **71 percent of Texas Democrats and 44 percent of Texas Republicans
  oppose data centers, with only 14 percent of Republicans strongly in support.**

## 6. Civic and goodwill spending, the least-covered channel

### Ellis County: $27.6 million from one developer in one summer

**[REP]** **Compass Datacenters** (founder and CEO Chris Crosby):
- **$15 million donation for a new Ellis County animal shelter**, approved June 2026.
- **$12.6 million, 40,000-square-foot building donated to Texas State Technical College** to expand
  its data center pathway program.
- **Ellis County Judge John Wray**: *"There is no strings attached... this is the first time our
  county to my knowledge has entered into a community benefits agreement with a gift of this
  nature."*
- Waxahachie resident **Susie Hall**: *"I hate to think that we're going to be obligated to Compass
  because there will be something they're going to want in return."*

**The chronology, stated as chronology and nothing more:** the county approved the $15 million
shelter gift in June. **Ellis County had offered a tax abatement on Compass' proposed 890-acre
property, and that item was withdrawn before the June 23rd commissioners meeting**, which Crosby
attributed to the "noise" around it. **Two facts, adjacent in time. Assert no exchange.** Crosby
also said he welcomes new regulation and that Abbott "should raise the bar for everyone."

**[DOC]** Compass holds multiple Texas franchise registrations (Compass Datacenters LLC plus DFW
III, DFW IIIA, DFW IIISE and fund entities), consistent with **a per-site SPV structure. Multiple
LLCs per project is standard practice and is worth explaining to readers as the reason a county
often cannot tell who it is dealing with.**

### El Paso, which matters because it is not on ERCOT

**[REP]** Meta expanded its El Paso project to 1 GW and **more than $10 billion**, sought a
**community engagement manager**, and met with critics and city leaders. El Paso then **adopted
tougher rules for future data center developments** and considered a no-tax-breaks proposal.
**[DOC] El Paso is not in ERCOT, so Abbott's grid-connection moratorium does not reach it.**

**[UNV]** For **Hood, Hill, Hays, Brazos, Grimes, Taylor, Shackelford, Childress, Nueces, Bastrop,
Milam and Ector**, no named 2025 or 2026 civic gift surfaced. **Report as "not found in accessible
sources," never as "none exists"** — these appear in commissioner and school board agenda packets,
which are not indexed by any tool available here.

**[REP]** **Somervell County has a tax abatement deal with Amazon for a 600 MW data center, and its
commissioners nonetheless unanimously approved a resolution in May 2026 opposing new data
centers.** A county can take the deal and still turn.

**Local government**: Texas Association of County Officials $48,512,869; Texas Municipal League

**Read the local government row carefully and say so in print.** TACO's $48.5 million is

organizations that lobby part time.

**The cleanest single comparison:** **Leading the Future raised more than $50 million in five
months of 2025. Texas Impact, the interfaith group organizing faith communities against data

## 8. The politicians, with the money adjacent

**[REP] Ken Paxton**, AG and Senate nominee: his political operation **received at least $448,000
as of June from data center company executives and political committees**, per FEC filings.
**The structural fact: Hood County asked his office in February 2026 for an opinion on its
authority to pause data center development, and that ruling is still pending** per the AG's own
site. A **Black Mountain Energy Storage** vice president of land **donated $17,000 to PACs
associated with Paxton's campaign in May**; Black Mountain is building a data center in Fort Worth.
**Report the pendency and the donations as adjacent documented facts. Assert nothing.**

**[REP] Sid Miller**, Agriculture Commissioner: has asked Abbott for a one-year moratorium, a
special session, and **for Abbott to return donor money from data center companies**, and says he
has not heard back. **A sitting statewide Republican asking the governor to give back industry
money is the sharpest fact in this file.**

**[REP] Charles Schwertner (R-Georgetown)**, SB 6 co-author, **told the PUCT to reject the
$33 billion Permian transmission applications**. An author of the large-load bill opposing the
transmission build is a genuine crosscurrent. The Permian Basin Reliability Plan would build three
765kV lines, **"estimated at about $33 billion, a cost ratepayers will foot."** More than 100
people testified for 15 hours against it.

**[REP] James Talarico (D)**, Senate nominee, called for regulation and an "AI dividend."
**His allied super PAC Lone Star Rising took $10 million from Reid Hoffman**, who has spoken in
favor of data centers. Talarico has separately called for banning super PACs. **Both sides of this
fight are taking AI-fortune money and the piece must say so.**

**[REP] Wayne Christian**, Railroad Commissioner, supports data centers on religious grounds.
**Paul Bettencourt** plans to challenge the San Marcos ban. **Clayton Tucker (D)**, agriculture
commissioner candidate, is running with data centers as his top petition issue.
