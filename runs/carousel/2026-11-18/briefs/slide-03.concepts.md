# Slide 03, THE INSTRUMENT, three concepts

1. **The swath over the state (studio, three quarter from the front and above).** An illustrative survey satellite in silver and graphite foil hangs high in a cerulean studio sweep, its blue cell wing on a diagonal, and from its nadir aperture a dusk gold glass fan of eight wedges opens down onto a pale enamel outline of Texas inlaid flush in the sweep floor, the fan's footprint wide enough to cover the whole state. Only "A view of all of Texas" earns this, because the picture is literally one instrument whose one view takes in the entire state, with east at frame right.

2. **The aperture macro (studio, 30 cm from the baffle, looking up into it).** A worm's eye macro into the blackened baffle and machined lip of the nadir instrument, with eight gold slivers of light leaving the aperture like the blades of a fan. It is precise and unlike any neighbour, but the headline's "all of Texas" has nothing in it, so it could run on any satellite story.

3. **The orbit limb (outdoor world, high above the Gulf at dawn).** The satellite small against a curved horizon of the Gulf coast at first light, the gold swath trailing a strip across a hazy land below. It says "from orbit" but puts a large geography with a gold sheet in frame, which is too near frame 1's slab under its gold air sheet, and the craft shrinks to a speck that can't carry "precision".

**Pick: concept 1.** It is the only one where swapping the headline breaks the picture: the fan is sized to the state and the state is the floor it lands on, so the sentence and the drawing say the same thing.

Type: one line of headline low across the deep front of the sweep floor, the dek under it in four lines, the floor in front of the inlay kept empty and deepened toward indigo so the type sits on it, the satellite and fan owning the top two thirds.
