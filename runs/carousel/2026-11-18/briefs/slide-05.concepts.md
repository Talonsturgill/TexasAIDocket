# Slide 05, THE FIT, three concepts

1. **The staked plot (CHOSEN).** A graded caliche pad in a fall pasture, seen from a 9 m lift through a shift lens so the sky stays in, carries a 10 by 10 grid of lath survey stakes: FIG.r2_of_100 of them wear bluebonnet flagging filled column by column from the west and the rest stand bare at the east edge, each throwing a long diagonal shadow, a worker and a pickup at the edge for scale. Only this beat has it because a survey stake is how a fit is laid on land, and 79 flagged of 100 is the R squared counted out as a share of stakes rather than charted.
2. **The archery field.** Four target butts stand on a mown range at morning, three with clean faces and one stuck with arrows in a 10 by 10 grid of holes, a camera low behind the shooting line. It says contest and winner, but arrows read as hits and would teach a reader that R squared is an accuracy rate, which the dek says it is not.
3. **The tally board.** A 30 cm macro of a walnut pegboard on an ice studio sweep with 100 drilled holes and FIG.r2_of_100 bluebonnet pegs seated in them, the empty holes catching shadow. It counts cleanly, but a studio object is frame 6's medium and a board of pegs is a chart in wood.

**Pick.** Concept 1, because stakes driven to fit a grid in Texas ground is a picture of a fit and nothing else, it keeps the camera walk at a staked plot as the storyboard plans, and its world (outdoors, a lift, a sky) differs from the relief before it and the studio after it.

**Type.** The headline sits centred in the sky band above the horizon, two lines, and the dek centred over the far pasture beneath it, so the grid owns the lower 60 percent and nothing crosses the stakes.
