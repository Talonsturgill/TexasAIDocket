# Slide 02 concepts, WHY SATELLITES ("Ground monitoring is costly")

1. **One station, all that land.** A single fibreglass air monitoring shelter behind its chain link fence, a worker at the door and a pickup beside it, stands small and low on an immense empty caliche flat that runs to one mesa on the horizon under a huge cerulean morning sky. Only this beat is about how much ground one costly station leaves unwatched, and the empty miles around it are the argument.

2. **Worm's eye at the inlet.** A camera at the shelter's roof, close on the stainless sample inlet and its rain cap filling the lower frame, with the met mast's cup anemometer turning above and nothing but sky and a far flat horizon behind. It says "this is the machine that breathes one spot of air", the hardware cost made intimate, but the land it misses is only implied.

3. **The long road in.** A low wide view down a caliche ranch road from the pickup's dusty tailgate, the road running a long way to one tiny white shelter far off at its end, tyre ruts and mesquite the only marks. It makes the cost a journey, the distance a crew drives to one site, rather than the land a site can't see.

**Pick: 1.** It is the one a stranger reads in a second as "one small station, endless land", which is exactly why monitoring is costly and missing in places; concept 2 shows the instrument (frame 3 already owns the instrument as a crafted object) and concept 3 says travel, not coverage. Frame 1 is a relief from above and frame 3 a studio product shot, so a ground level outdoor world is unlike both.

Type: the headline high in the empty sky, left, two lines at large size, the short dek under it; the sky above the horizon at 0.62 is kept clean of cloud behind the type, the station sits right of centre on the flat so the eye drops from the words to it.
