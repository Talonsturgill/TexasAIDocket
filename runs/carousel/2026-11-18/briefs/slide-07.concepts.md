# Slide 07 concepts, NEIGHBOURS

1. **A courtyard floor in late shade (material relief, plan, close).** Exactly 254 chiselled limestone flagstones in sand mortar fill the frame, the bluebonnet slate among them gathered into a few contiguous clumps in the sunlit floor, while the cool shade of an unseen live oak lies across the top left where the type sits. Only this beat has it because a floor is the one everyday thing where "which stone sits next to which" is the whole picture, so clumping is seen rather than charted.

2. **A pieced quilt on a bed (studio, textile, three quarter).** A hand pieced quilt of 254 irregular patches draped over a bed end in the explainer studio, the indigo patches sewn in neighbouring runs rather than scattered, stitching and folds catching a soft key. It belongs to this beat because a quilt is literally patches chosen to sit beside one another, though the bed edges toward the desk and chair furniture the last decks used.

3. **A cobbled plaza at a worm's eye (spatial, low camera, sky).** A camera a hand's height off a plaza of dark and pale cobbles, the dark ones running together in a near clump and a far one, the plaza going soft toward a cerulean sky where the headline sits. It reads clumping through perspective, though it shares the outdoor sky and low lens of frame 8 and makes the 254 count unreadable in depth.

**Pick: 1.** The floor is the only concept where all 254 stones are visible and countable, the clumps read at a glance at 432 px, and its medium (a plan relief) is unlike the studio row of frame 6 and the pasture at a shoulder of frame 8.

**Composition with the type.** The key comes from the upper left (the deck's relief light), so the shade of an off frame canopy falls naturally over the top left. The headline sits there in three lines in a column about 700 px wide, the dek under it in a narrower column, both on the cool shaded stones that the canopy makes quiet. The sunlit floor takes the right and the lower half, where the largest slate clump is the focal point just below and right of the dek.
