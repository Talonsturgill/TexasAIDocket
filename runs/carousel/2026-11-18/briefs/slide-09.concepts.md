# Slide 09 concepts, WHERE TO LOOK FIRST

**A. The pixelated land, one sharp circle, one gold block (chosen).** A high oblique aerial over an illustrative stretch of Texas land in which every patch outside one circle has been flattened into a satellite block that averages its own height and colour, receding in a grid to a cerulean horizon, while a small air monitor station stands at the centre of the one circle where the terraced ground keeps its full detail. One block over a small town, far from that circle, is framed in raised dusk gold, so the picture says exactly the beat: the coarse reading covers everything, the monitor sees sharply only around itself, and the method points at the place with no monitor.

**B. Lifting the flagged tile.** A studio close up of one satellite block of land lifted out of its grid like a core sample by a dusk gold edge, a town and its streets showing on its top, the empty socket below it in a field of blocks. Only this beat could have it because the gesture is "look here first", though it loses the monitor and so the "where no monitor stands" half.

**C. The road into town.** An eye level wide of a two lane highway running into a small town under a dusk gold rectangle of measured air, a lone monitor shelter far behind in the ditch of another road. It reads, but frame 8 already stands at eye height outdoors over a pasture, so it would repeat the neighbour's camera and world.

**Pick A.** It is the only one that puts the satellite's coarse view, the monitor's sharp view and the flagged place in one picture, which is the sentence "where to look first, especially where no monitor stands", and its aerial raking relief differs from frame 8's shoulder height pasture.

**Composition.** The horizon sits high, near y 300, so the sky and the fogged far blocks make a quiet top band. The headline is set centred in that sky on one or two lines, the dek centred under it over the hazy far grid. The sharp circle sits mid left and the gold block lower right, both in the steep, well lit near half of the frame, with the key low from the east (frame right) so every block throws its shadow left.
