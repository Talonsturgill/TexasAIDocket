The story in two lines: a UT Dallas team paired satellite readings of eight air pollutants from 2022 with public CDC cancer
prevalence data for all 254 Texas counties and used machine learning. Counties with more pollution tended to have more
cancer, sulfur dioxide was the strongest environmental predictor, the east has the worst air and the west the best, and
high cancer counties sit near each other. The researchers say it is an association between places, not evidence that
pollution causes cancer in any one person, and satellite data may be less precise than ground monitors, which are costly
and missing in places. Published online March 5th in Environmental Monitoring and Assessment; UT Dallas announced it
November 6th.
