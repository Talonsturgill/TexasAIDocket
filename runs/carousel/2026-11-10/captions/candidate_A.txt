A Frontera node held two Xeons.

Jose Rizo-Rey's lab at UT Southwestern in Dallas ran a 5.3 million atom simulation on 64 of those nodes. It ran at 43 nanoseconds per day. Frontera retired in October after more than eight million jobs.

On Vista at UT Austin, eight GPU nodes gave him "almost the same speed." Most Vista nodes run NVIDIA's GH200, a Grace CPU paired with a Hopper GPU. So the easy reading is new chips.

Rizo-Rey says the speed took a lot of GROMACS optimization with help from TACC staff.

One lab's account, not a benchmark. Still, Austin supplied the speed twice, in hardware and in people.

The deck draws one tray per node and the membranes those atoms make up.

Frontera's successor Horizon has its power infrastructure complete and its AI hardware going in. Who tunes the code for the next Texas lab in line?
