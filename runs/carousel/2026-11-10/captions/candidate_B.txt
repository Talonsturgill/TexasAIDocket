Frontera was retired in October.

For most of its life it was the fastest academic supercomputer in the United States. It ran more than eight million jobs over seven years at TACC in Austin.

What does a lab do when its machine retires?

Jose Rizo-Rey of UT Southwestern in Dallas ran a 5.3 million atom simulation on 64 Frontera CPU nodes. On TACC's Vista eight GPU nodes gave him "almost the same speed." He says it took a lot of GROMACS tuning with TACC staff. It is one lab's account and not a benchmark. The deck draws each node as one tray.

What replaces Frontera is still going in. Horizon is in its first phase of early operations. Its power infrastructure is complete and its AI hardware is going in. A researcher with early access says full production comes soon.

If a Dallas lab now runs on eight nodes, what will Texas labs ask of Horizon?
