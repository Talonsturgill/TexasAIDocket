# Storyboard, 2026-08-27, carousel no. 9

## The synthesis, and why this deck rather than the other two

Three directors pitched. Every one of them independently put the deck on the Blackland Prairie
east of Austin at night, which is a strong signal that the story's own material is real rather
than chosen. What separated them was the structural law.

**THE MADE THING is the spine, and it wins on one idea.** Machine guard yellow appears on every
frame, always at the boundary between where a machine works and where a person stands, and on
the closing frame that same yellow stops being a fence and becomes a threshold you walk across.
That is the deck's argument delivered in one colour, and it converts this story's single
structural weakness into its payoff. `texan_check` at selection reported **deadline NO, next
step NO** on this story. A closing frame where the barrier turns into a doorway is the answer to
exactly that.

**THE PLACE AND THE PEOPLE gave the light law, and it is grafted whole.** One lamp, fixed, with
a computed inverse square falloff, and the rule that **the light reaches exactly as far as the
record does.** What is documented is lit. What neither release states falls off into the dark.
The made thing already had one high bay fixture. This makes that fixture mean something, and it
means the deck never has to say the word absent because absence becomes a lighting fact.

**THE LEDGER OF ASKS gave the copy law, and it is not optional.** Every absence on every frame
is written as **not stated in a named document**, never as a thing that did not happen. That is
what `absence_check` requires and it is the line between a record and an insinuation. Its
cut-in-clay material is NOT taken, because deck no. 6 spent milled console recesses and deck
no. 7 spent an anodized air gap, and a third recess deck lands on the ledger's own avoid list.

## The four laws, and every frame obeys all four

1. **ONE LAMP.** A single high bay fixture, azimuth fixed, with a computed inverse square
   falloff. The lit area is what the record holds. There is no second light anywhere in nine
   frames, and no frame contains a horizon, a sky or a star field.
2. **THE YELLOW BOUNDARY.** Machine guard yellow, `#E7B819`, appears on every frame and only
   ever at the line between where a machine works and where a person stands. Its position is
   computed from that frame's own geometry, never placed by eye. On frame 9 and nowhere else it
   is a threshold rather than a barrier.
3. **FABRICATED, NEVER CARVED.** Every form is assembled and every joint shows. Nothing anywhere
   is milled, engraved, embossed or incised. Where the record has a gap, the assembly has a
   positively drawn missing part, an empty tapped hole or an unstamped field, and never a blank
   area with a caption on it.
4. **NOTHING DEPICTED THAT NOBODY HAS DESCRIBED.** No frame draws Amazon's plant, Amazon's
   machinery, the site, or any person. Neither release states what any of them look like and
   inventing one would be a fabrication in the largest type on the page.

Banned outright, and these are in the acceptance lists rather than in good intentions: hazard
chevrons, diagonal stripes, warning triangles, pictograms, and any red emergency stop. Yellow
here means a boundary. It never means danger, because danger is a verdict this record cannot
carry.

## Planned per frame median L*, which is a PLAN and is worthless until measured

12, 20, 16, 30, 14, 72, 26, 50, 22. The deck median plans to 22. The ledger's own `avoid_next`
line says a deck that declares a value band without measuring it is a deck to avoid, so this
gets measured off the nine rendered PNGs at 216 by 270 and the MEASURED numbers go into
`ledger/carousel/artwork.json`, never these.

The light deck cap matters here. `config/brand.yaml` allows one light deck per eight runs and
deck no. 8 yesterday already breached it, so this deck sits well below mid or it is wrong before
it is drawn.

Computed by `out/2026-08-27/compute.py` over `ledger/docket.json` and declared in
`out/2026-08-27/aggregates.json`: 13, 12, 91.

Drawn rather than stated, and the one exception this inventory has to name: frame 3 prints `0`
as the origin of its own scale, and frame 9 prints `E1.012`, which lives in c20's note rather
than inside any quoted string. The origin is a fact about the axis and not a quantity about the
world, and the untruncated-scale doctrine requires it to be visible, so it is drawn where the
scale starts and it measures nothing. Round two's integrity judge read the sentence that used to
close this section, "No frame prints a figure that is not on one of those two lists", against the
run's own render and refuted it on both. An inventory that overstates itself is the same defect
as a gloss that overstates its set, one page apart, so the exceptions are written down instead.

The item ids frame 4 prints are not figures and are not on either list. Each is a record identifier, `tx-2026-00NN`, and it names the row rather than measuring anything. Frame 9's `9:00` is the same case as `E1.012` above and comes from the same place, c20's note, and round three's craft and integrity judges both found it named nowhere while E1.012 had been granted an exception one line earlier. Two rounds of repair each fixed the exception that had just been raised and left its twin, which is this run's recurring shape and is written down in the run record.

Nothing else. Every other figure on every other frame is on one of the two lists above.
