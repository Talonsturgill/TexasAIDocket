# Carousel no. 17 — storyboard and slide dossiers

Run 2026-09-06. Docket item `tx-2026-0125`. Claims file `out/2026-09-06/claims.json`, 25 claims
and 3 measured absences. Computation `out/2026-09-06/compute.py`, output `computed.json`.

## The synthesis, and why this treatment

Three directors pitched. **The threshold lens was taken whole and it is the spine.** Two lawful
routes into a Texas classroom, drawn as two built openings in one wall, in one Central Texas
September morning's sun. The argument needs no adjectives, because a reader can see which
opening has a stop and a strike milled into it and which one does not.

**The other two lenses lost on the same ground and both gave something up.** The measured day
drew the difference as periodicity, one route that meets against one that never closes, lit by a
sun computed for a printed clock time on every frame. It is the more beautiful idea and it puts
nine chances to be caught wrong on nine frames, because a Texan knows what eight in the morning
in September looks like and one frame whose light disagrees with its own printed clock turns the
deck's best idea into its worst tell. Ground and population drew the state as a body of Blackland
clay and descended to a hand's width of soil. It is the more Texan idea and its own author named
the fault, which is that its frame 3 answers an agenda line with an invented concrete monument,
a decoration of the evidence rather than a close reading of it.

Two things were grafted onto it after its own self-critique named them, and two more were taken
from the lenses that lost.

**Its frame 3 was its weakest by its own account**, a plate with words on it, which is one step
from the label-chip defect two earlier decks were marked down for. The plate stays and its
letters are now MODELLED AS CAST BRONZE STANDING PROUD OF THE PLATE, with the deck's one sun
raking across their sides, so the frame is a casting rather than a caption. An earlier draft of
this plan called them recessed and the drawing did not follow, and the plan was the half that was
wrong: a cast bronze plaque carries raised letters, because that is what casting a plate does.
The words are still the board's own.

**Its frame 5 carried the counter-image on a drawing convention most readers have never met.**
The isolux plot stays, because light that never meets a closing line is exactly what a rolling
application window is, and the failure it risks is measurable rather than a matter of taste. The
frame's own script asserts a minimum contour spacing and the acceptance item names the number,
so a moiré at feed size fails the frame instead of surviving to a judge.

## What the deck must not do, taken from the record rather than from taste

**The school is not named until frame 7.** That is structural. Frame 3 prints a line from the
board's agenda for its August 31st to September 4th, 2026 meeting, and that line belongs to a
later application cycle than the one this school went through. A reader who has not yet met the
school can't read frame 3 as being about it, and by frame 7 the deck has said which route is
which. `rejected` in the claims file carries this as its sixth entry.

**A place is a boundary or a name, and never a dot.** The Texas town names on frame 9 are a read
list from the school's own location menu, claim c25, and they are set as a column the frame CROPS
rather than closes. No numeral goes near them and no map appears anywhere in the deck. The list's
boundary is unsettled and reporting puts the Texas figure above thirty, so a frame printing ten
would contradict c22 in this same deck.

**No person, no face, no child.** A deck about who may teach a child that draws a child has
invented the one thing no source describes.

**Red is reserved and unspent.** The rolling route has no closing date and the board's meeting
has already ended, so there is no dated door a reader can act on. Spending the reserved colour on
a URL would be the drawing inventing an urgency the record refuses.

## The register

Two doorways as one architectural diagram in hard Central Texas September daylight, one sun at
one azimuth and one altitude on every frame, no people, no signage that is not a quoted string,
and the door hardware carrying the argument.

## The value arc, and the one inversion

Planned median L*, to be MEASURED off the rendered PNGs at feed width and rewritten from the
measurement rather than from this plan.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| planned L* | 34 | 22 | 18 | 26 | 30 | **74** | 20 | 28 | 44 |
| MEASURED L* | 21.8 | 21.0 | 26.1 | 9.5 | 27.0 | **82.5** | 16.8 | 6.6 | 13.7 |

**The measured row is the one that counts and it is written from the renders**, at 270 by 338,
the grid every prior run used, and it is REWRITTEN after every render pass rather than after the
first one, into BOTH this table and `measurements.json`, which held two different answers for one
round because only the table was rewritten. Deck median 21.4 against a planned 28, well under the
light cap.

Round 3's repairs moved four frames and round 4's moved four more. Frame 1 fell to 21.9 as the
room behind the wall went properly unlit and its speck field stopped filling the declared focal
with white. Frame 5 rose to 26.0 as the isolux bands were terraced and the figure's plate became a
soft reserve with no edges. Frame 7 rose to 16.8 as the soffit's near end took its own light
instead of sitting under an opaque slot. Frame 9 rose to 13.7 as the black bar came off its focal
and the head above the camera stopped being a flat rectangle. Frame 6 fell from 86.1 to 82.5 across rounds 4 and 5, which is
the one number here worth stating plainly. Its declared focal, the lightest area in the whole
deck, was a 98 percent cream scrim painted OVER the concrete that had just been re-laid for it,
and in round 5 it also carried four label bars printing strings frame 4 already prints. Putting
the concrete back on top and taking the labels off cost the frame 3.6 L and gave it the material
the acceptance list had asked for since round 1. The inversion is still larger than planned.

Three frames came out darker than planned and the reason is the same on all three. Frame 4's
jamb, frame 8's poché and frame 9's reveal are each mostly one dark material with a small lit
subject in them, and a MEDIAN reads the material rather than the subject. The inversion is
larger than planned rather than smaller, 82.5 against 74, so the one thing the arc had to
deliver is delivered. **The close is the frame this row argues with**: it was planned at 44 and
measures 13.7, because the reveal the camera stands in fills most of it and only the opening is
lit. That is the frame it draws and the number is recorded rather than the plan being defended.

**The inversion is frame 6 and nowhere else.** It is deliberately not at frame 5, because the
bright full bleed frame has sat at position 5 in five earlier decks and no gate can see that.
The two largest junctions are 5 to 6 and 6 to 7, which is the pair a reader remembers.

## Structural laws, which hold on every frame

1. **ONE SUN.** One azimuth and one altitude, computed in code from a stated rule and never
   printed on a frame, on every frame that carries drawn light. An earlier deck declared an
   elevation in its dossier and rendered nothing like it, because the number lived in the plan
   and in no drawing step.
2. **NO PERSON, NO FACE, NO BODY, NO CHILD** on any of the nine frames.
3. **HARDWARE IS DRAWN ONLY FOR A FUNCTION A SOURCE STATES.** A board can refuse, so its route
   gets a stop and a strike. Where no source states a function the jamb is uncut and lit, never a
   dark rectangle.
4. **A PLACE IS A BOUNDARY OR A NAME, AND NEVER A DOT.** Taken whole from the lens that lost,
   because it is sharper than the rule it replaces. Ten dots is a count drawn in ink, and this
   deck may not print a campus count OF ITS OWN. So the towns are set as type rather than as
   marks on a map, and no map appears anywhere in the deck. The one figure that may stand beside
   them is the reporting's own, quoted and named to the outlets that counted it on the face of
   the frame, because ten names beside no total invites a reader to take ten as the answer and
   the reporting says otherwise.

   THIS LAW ASKED FOR A CROP FOR TWO ROUNDS AND NO FRAME EVER DREW ONE. A column running off the
   bottom edge was the device, and the render closed the column at Lake Travis with a third of
   the frame below it in both rounds. The device is dropped rather than re-promised, because the
   quoted count is the stronger answer to the same question: a crop implies more and a count
   says how many more and who counted them.
5. **THE TWO OPENINGS ARE IDENTICAL IN SIZE** on every frame they share, asserted by that
   frame's own script.
6. **THE BUILDING IS A DIAGRAM AND NOT A PLACE.** No address, no signage that is not a quoted
   string, no context that would let a reader locate it.
7. **THE MEETING DATES ARE AUTHORED COPY AND NEVER A VERBATIM SLOT.** The board's own string
   carries a dash the house rules forbid, and restyling a quotation falsifies it. The deck writes
   the dates in house style and quotes the agenda's words instead.
