Texas A&M says Florida's state drone team made its tool standard procedure.

CLARKE rates the damage to each building in a color coded view. A road model flags damaged roads so crews can route around them. A trained operator reviews every assessment before it reaches decision makers.

Tom Manzini of the development team says CLARKE turns drone imagery into damage assessments in minutes instead of 18 to 24 hours.

Texas A&M says the models learned from more than 21,000 damaged homes in 10 major disasters, Hurricane Harvey among them. It says 125 responders from 63 agencies in 14 states and Puerto Rico have trained on CLARKE. The November 24th release and the project site name no Texas agency or county among its users.
