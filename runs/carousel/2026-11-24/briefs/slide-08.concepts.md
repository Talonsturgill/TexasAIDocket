# Slide 08 concepts, WHO HAS TRAINED (c8)

1. THE HELMET WALL. A training room's painted pegboard wall runs away from the camera at a rake, five rows of rescue helmets in white, lemon, orange and sky blue, each with its lit crown and a long cast on the board under a honey window key, and one responder seen from behind lifts the last helmet onto its empty peg. Only a count of trained people can be a wall of helmets that each belong to someone, and the one still in a hand says the count is people who did the training, not equipment bought.

2. THE CLASSROOM FLOOR FROM THE CATWALK. Looking down from a mezzanine at a training hall floor set with folding tables, 125 helmets each resting on its own closed binder in long rows, the light falling in window bars across them. It shows a roster as seats in a class, but it reads as a top down tray, the chart look the judges have charged.

3. THE CUBBY TOWER, MACRO. A 30 cm macro along one row of steel gear cubbies, each holding a helmet and a folded reflective vest, the near cubby in sharp focus with tool marks and the far ones melting into honey bokeh. Intimate and tactile, but at macro scale the full count of 125 can't be read in one frame, which breaks the data in art.

PICK: 1. It is the only one where every unit is countable at feed size, the act of one person adding the last one ties the number to people trained, and an indoor wall breaks hard from frame 7's outdoor road and frame 9's aerial street.

Composition: the wall fills the left two thirds from top to bottom, receding into a corner. The perpendicular end wall, plain painted plaster under the window light, is the quiet right column where the headline sits in three lines with the dek under it. The responder stands lower middle, back to camera, arm up.
