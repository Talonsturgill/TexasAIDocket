# Slide 02 concepts, THE OLD CLOCK

1. **The roadside ruler.** A rural chip seal farm road runs diagonally away from the camera on a long lens, ruled off by 24 white roadside marker posts at one even spacing, the stretch from marker 18 to marker 24 standing on a low peach shoulder band, with a tarped ranch house far back at the start, a responder in a vest by the first post and a utility pole leaning with its line down across the verge. Only this beat has it because it turns a count of hours into distance a responder still has to cover on a rural road, which is exactly where the copy says the information takes longest.
2. **The pole's shadow dial.** A leaning utility pole in a flooded yard seen from above at dawn, its shadow drawn 24 times as a fan across the wet ground like a sundial, the last six sweeps on peach water. Only this beat has it because the pole that fell is the clock that ran while nobody had a damage picture yet, but a fan of shadows reads as a diagram and the frame after is also a near plan view.
3. **The sandbag wall.** A low eye level close shot of a ranch house porch where a responder has stacked 24 sandbags in a long row along the slab, each one an hour's work, the last six on a peach tarp, the tarped roof above cropped by the top edge. Only this beat has it because each bag is labour by hand, the old way, but sandbags say flood prevention rather than assessment and the count reads as effort, not waiting.

**Pick: 1.** It passes point 1 best because the long rural road is the copy's own "particularly in rural areas" made literal, and an evenly ruled road reads as elapsed time without a chart. It differs from frame 1 (a high aerial over the neighbourhood) and frame 3 (a plan relief) by being a low long lens ground view.

**Composition.** Sky in the top fifth, the road band as a diagonal from the near left (marker 1, about y 780) up to the far right (marker 24, about y 520), the leaning pole breaking into the sky in the middle. The type sits low left over the near verge, which falls into cool violet shade, headline in two lines at about y 860, the dek under it in four lines above the footer. The posts are kit scale delineators (1.2 m) because a 10 cm raised pavement marker can't read at feed size across 24 hours of road, so the dek's picture sentence names a roadside marker.
