# Slide 04, ONE ROOF, three concepts

1. THE ROOF UP CLOSE. One brick ranch roof seen from 6 m above its back eave under a raking honey key: a torn patch down to tan OSB with nail rows, ragged shingle courses round it, a blue tarp corner held by sandbags, a live oak limb broken across the ridge, and one hollow cool rim drawn round the eave, with a short gold route on the street beyond. Only this beat has it because the headline's word is "every roof" and this is the deck's single camera close enough to show that the judgement is about one building's own damage, not the neighbourhood.

2. THE ADJUSTER'S LADDER. A worm's eye from a lawn at the gutter line, an aluminium ladder against a brick wall rising into a cobalt sky, the hollow rim crossing the frame overhead like a halo at the eave and torn tabs dangling from the drip edge. It belongs to this beat because a rim around one house, read from the ground, says this one building has been rated, but it puts a person's climb in the picture, which is frame 6's job.

3. SHINGLE MACRO. A 30 cm macro across one torn course: granules, a lifted tab, nail heads in OSB, the edge of the cool rim running through the depth of field like a glowing rail. It is specific to "each individual building" only by implication, and at feed size a stranger would not know it is a roof on one house.

**Pick: 1.** It is the only one where a stranger sees one whole house, its own particular damage and the single rim round it in one second, which is exactly "each individual building is rated". Frame 3 is a plan relief and frame 5 a lab interior, so an outdoor raking oblique on one roof shares neither world, camera nor medium.

Type: top left over the shadowed street and lawn beyond the ridge, two lines of hook and a four line dek, with the roof's back slope filling the lower 60 percent and the decking patch as the focal point in the lower third.
