# Slide 06 concepts, A PERSON CHECKS

1. **The tailgate desk at cobalt dusk.** A trained operator in an orange vest stands at the dropped tailgate of a work pickup on a caliche shoulder, turned three quarters away, face lit cool by a laptop seen only from the back of its lid, with an open hard case holding the folded survey drone beside it and, behind, a leaning utility pole with its line down and tarped roofs low on the horizon. Only this beat has it because it is the one frame where the person stands between the machine's map and anyone who acts on it, in the field, with the drone already grounded and boxed and no antenna in sight.

2. **Inside the cab, the windshield as the neighbourhood.** A worm's eye from the passenger footwell of a parked truck at night: the operator in the driver's seat, laptop braced on the wheel lighting the face, and through the windshield the dark street of tarped roofs the map describes. Only this beat has it because the review is a private, enclosed act of attention, but it hides the field case and reads as somebody working late in a car.

3. **The checker's hands, macro.** A 40 cm top down macro of the tailgate's ribbed liner: two gloved hands, a closed laptop, a grease pencil and the open case with its foam cut to the drone. Only this beat could carry hands at work, but with no face and no screen it can't say a person checked, and a top down tray is the chart look judges charged lately.

**Pick: 1.** It is the only one where a stranger reads "a person, in the field, checking before anything moves" in one second, and the grounded boxed drone plus the downed line make it a disaster field check rather than office work.

## Composition with the type

Camera at 1.3 m, 6.5 m behind and left of the truck's tail, the key low behind the camera's left shoulder (the sky's own sun) so the operator's turned face takes the honey key and the screen's cool spill together. Truck, operator and case own the right two thirds and the lower middle. The headline sits large in the upper left cobalt sky in three lines, the pole and its fallen wire rising in the right sky so the left stays quiet. The dek sits low left over the caliche foreground, under the truck's long navy shadow, clear of the footer.
