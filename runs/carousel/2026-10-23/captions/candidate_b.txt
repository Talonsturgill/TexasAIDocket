UT Southwestern in Dallas changed one line.

Its researchers gave three open source AI models pairs of matched emergency patients. With no stigmatizing detail, all three picked the higher risk one at rates well above chance.

Then "established patient with multiple chronic conditions" became "17 visits in the past 12 months." One added detail sometimes moved a model off the patient who later deteriorated. In some instances nearly 10% were incorrectly downgraded.

Beyond Dallas, hospitals are increasingly exploring AI to help prioritize care, the release says. First author Philip Jarrett says bias varies by model and each tool needs testing against real patient outcomes first.

The deck draws that line as tape.

The records came from California and Massachusetts. The release names no Texas hospital using these models for triage. If one starts, will its outcome test be public?
