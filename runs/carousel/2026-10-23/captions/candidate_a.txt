Before one added line, the models chose well. After it, sometimes they didn't.

UT Southwestern researchers in Dallas asked three open source AI models which of two emergency patients of similar severity should be seen first. With no social or stigmatizing detail in the record, all three picked the higher risk patient at rates well above chance. That is the promise of AI triage.

Then the wording moved. The neutral "established patient with multiple chronic conditions" gave way to "17 visits in the past 12 months." A single added detail sometimes shifted a model away from the patient who later deteriorated. In some instances nearly 10% of patients were incorrectly downgraded.

The records came from California and Massachusetts hospitals. First author Philip Jarrett says bias varies by model and each tool should be tested against real patient outcomes before use. The deck rebuilds the test as record cards.

The release names no Texas hospital using these models for triage. If one adopts them, how will its model read "17 visits"?
