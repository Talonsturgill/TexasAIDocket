A Texas statute and a UTMB release both describe AI in the health record.

UTMB in Galveston put OpenEvidence inside its health record in March. Clinicians get answers with citations. More than half of them use it for "clinical decision support," UTMB said on September 22nd.

Since September 1st, 2025, Sec. 183.005 of the Health and Safety Code has said a practitioner who uses AI for "diagnostic purposes" must disclose that use to patients. The record here can't say whether the section reaches this use.

A patient can ask in the exam room. The UT System Regents meet November 18th to 19th in Austin. The public may testify on a topic the agenda lists. A speaker sends the board's General Counsel a name and topic at least 24 hours ahead.

Will the November agenda list AI in the health record?
