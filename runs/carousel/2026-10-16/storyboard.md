# Storyboard, 2026-10-16
# "Anyone can ride it to the airport now. The flood fix is still listed in development"

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

On October 14th, 2026 Waymo opened its San Antonio robotaxi service to anyone with the app, San Antonio
International Airport included (c1). The city's Director of Airports, Jesus Saenz, called it a significant
milestone and "a new option to get there" in a statement Waymo published (c2, c3). The service had taken its
first public riders on February 24th, 2026 by invitation (c5, c6, c7), in a 60 square mile area Texas Public
Radio reports (c21).

On April 20th an empty Waymo was swept up in flood waters and washed into Salado Creek during heavy rains, and
no one was hurt, Texas Public Radio reports (c15, c16). Earlier that April another was stranded in floodwaters
near McCullough Avenue and Contour Drive (c17). Waymo filed a software recall with NHTSA, report received May 1st
(c14), 3791 potentially affected units of 5th and 6th generation automated driving systems (c12, c13). The
federal record says the software "may allow the vehicle to slow and then drive into standing water on higher
speed roadways" (c8), and that entering a flooded roadway can cause a loss of vehicle control (c9). TPR reports
the notice said the vehicle detected the flooded road and continued at reduced speed (c18). The pause was the
longest in San Antonio while Waymo reviewed its flood procedures (c22, c23), and in May the cars ran without
riders (c20). **The record, read October 16th, lists the remedy as under development, with an interim remedy of
tighter weather limits and updated maps (c10, c11).** The record carries it as tx-2026-0216.

**WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED.**
- **No resumption date.** The only source for one is behind the crawl boundary. No surface says when San Antonio
  service came back.
- **No San Antonio rider count.** Waymo's figures cover three cities (c4) and four (c6).
- **The units figure is the record's, 3791** (c13). TPR's "nearly 3,800" is its rounding (c19) and is not printed.
- **The April 20th interim update date (c11) is never tied to the creek** as cause or effect. Both dates appear only
  in their own sentences.
- **The car is illustrative.** It is unmarked, drawn to no maker's design, and the same drawing on every frame. It
  is never said to be the car in any report.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Three directors pitched THE WATER LINE (the federal sentence drawn as geometry, the car's relation to a water line
on every frame), 177 DAYS (one curb post a day, the car at the end of the line) and THE CURB (the car never changes
because the fix is software, so the place carries the turn).

- **From THE WATER LINE, the spine and the evidence.** The record's own sentence drawn as a car slowing into
  standing water on a divided highway (4), the 3791 units rendered one each (5), the pause as a car stopped at a
  still water's edge with its reflection (6), and the record read closely (8).
- **From 177 DAYS, the unit of a day.** A steel curb bollard with a reflective band, a thing every traveller walks
  past at an airport curb. The calendar field of posts, one row a month, is the evidence frame (7), and the close
  counts the posts along the island (9).
- **From THE CURB, the honest constraint.** The recall is software (c8, c10), so the car's outside never changes.
  Its geometry is identical on every frame and only its place and what touches it change. The curb opens and
  closes the deck.
- **Refused:** a Texas map of four cities (no Texan needs a map to find San Antonio, and the deck spends its MAP
  budget better as rendered evidence), the kerb painted as a timeline (7 px at 432, unreadable), two incident pools
  in one frame (implies the creek and McCullough are near each other), the carved verbatim headline as a
  TYPE_AS_OBJECT over the curb (the DOCUMENT frame carries the same words better), road studs as days (a stud at
  432 px is a speck).
- **The accent.** All three directors chose comal #2A7A9E, brand.yaml's "the water itself", the blue green of the
  Edwards Aquifer water San Antonio stands on, and the only cool note in a sodium lit deck. It means ONE thing: the
  line where water meets the car or the record. The waterline on the car in the creek (3), the leading edge of the
  standing water (4), the line where the car meets its reflection (6), the band on the April 20th post (7), and the
  rule beside the remedy field (8). It is lit with a small emissive so it holds against the sodium key.

## The world, and the laws that hold it

**NIGHT UNDER SODIUM, THE CITY AFTER DARK.** The chassis is `assets/js/deck/2026-10-16-curb.js` and every frame
loads it. It declares `sky: nightSodium`, tuned to a cool near black haze, with the key at azimuth 40, elevation 34:
the curb's lamps stand south east of the subject and high. +x is east and +z south.

The argument for this world, against the house register: standing water on a dark road is visible only by what it
reflects, and under city sodium a flooded lane becomes a mirror of the lamps. Every place this story happens is a
city place after dark, an airport curb before an early flight (c3), a street, a creek beside a lit road. lastLight
carried four of the last eight decks and floodlit carried no. 50's crossing four days ago.

1. **Start dark.** A near black sky, a pool where the subject stands, the world behind gone dark.
2. **Type is light, on the dark field**, and every wash only darkens.
3. **Comal is the only accent** and means the water line. Nothing else is blue and saturated.
4. Every standing thing gets `TXT.contact`, every frame `TXT.weather`.
5. **The robotaxi is on every frame**, and its geometry never changes.
6. **Nothing drawn is a real site, and nothing is a verdict on safety.**
