# Storyboard — carousel no. 31, 2026-09-21
# "NOT A WARNING"

## The synthesis, and what was taken from whom

Three treatments came back. **The spine is the first**, THE PAPER TRAIL AS PHYSICAL OBJECT,
because its two structural laws do the deck's work in the drawing rather than in a disclaimer and
neither of the other two had an answer as good.

The first is the MUTE WORLD LAW. Paper is the only thing in this deck that carries glyphs, so no
truck livery, no door decal, no street sign, no house number, no badge and no screen text appears
in nine frames. That is what keeps a document deck from being nine rectangles, and it is also what
makes it impossible to read any drawn bungalow as one real property, which matters because the
record names an Oak Cliff homeowner and gives no address.

The second is the ACCENT LAW. Bluebonnet is the machine's sight, paper is the city's language, and
they never touch. Every other treatment put the accent somewhere it would eventually have to mean
severity. This one puts it on the capture rectangle and nowhere else, so a reader learns by frame 3
that blue means the machine has said something about a thing, and the count field on frame 5 then
carries none at all. That absence is a refusal rather than a restraint. Painting 39 filled marks in
the machine's own colour would assert that the machine put them there, and the record establishes
where the citations landed and nothing whatever about cause.

**Grafted from the third treatment, THE STREET THE TRUCK COMES DOWN, the condition law stated as a
count.** The first treatment wrote a good paragraph about not drawing neglect. The third wrote it as
something a critic can verify in one pass, which is that not one of the eight detected conditions
appears on any house in nine frames and the eight exist in exactly one place as eight lines of type.
Also taken from it, the hundred are never paired, and the refusal to draw a fleet.

**Grafted from the second treatment, WHAT THE MACHINE COUNTED, frame 3 entire.** The first treatment
spent a frame on four paper slips carrying four counts. The second put the detection vocabulary on
an ordinary sound house with eight hollow marks and eight leaders, which is what the software
actually does, and it is a far better frame. Also taken from it, the letter's own negative space
law on frame 6, which is that the page carries no seal, no letterhead, no address block and no case
number because the record describes four lines and nothing else, and everything between them is
hairline rule runs at true line lengths.

**Overruled in all three, the counts as geometry.** All three wanted to draw 21,000, 10,000, 3,700
and about 300 somewhere. The first laid them on four separated slips, the second on four sheets on a
driveway, and the third put them in type. The third is right and the other two are a trap the second
named against itself in its own risk list. *"Four numbers on one frame read top to bottom in
descending order IS a funnel, whatever my structural law says, because a reader does not read
structural laws."* So those four counts never appear as geometry at all. They are numerals in copy
on the frames they belong to. That is a stronger discipline than drawing them apart and hoping.

**Overruled in the second and third, the remainder.** Both wrote "39 filled and 18 hollow". The
memo states 39 of the 57 and says nothing whatever about the rest, so the count of the rest is a
subtraction this deck refuses. 57 marks are stamped and 39 are filled, and no numeral for the
remainder appears in any frame, label, dek, caption or file.

**Overruled in the third, the ground.** It proposed #1F2A1C and the second proposed #373B33, both
estimated by eye. #222E16 was measured in code against the last six shipped grounds at dE76 16.57 to
its nearest, which is #2E2016 from yesterday, against a floor of 10.

**Slide 5 is a DIAGRAM and not a GRID, and the pixels decided it.** It was planned as a GRID,
which is what a count of 57 marks is. `layout_check` measures a GRID by whether the subject comes
apart into four or more separable pieces at 432 px, because a count a reader can't separate in the
feed is not a count. This deck's marks are TONER ON BRIGHT STOCK, so at feed scale the measurable
subject against a dark ground is the SHEET, which is one piece, and no size of mark changes that.
The frame is a drawn page carrying a field, a mono label and the memo's own sentence set under it,
which is what DIAGRAM names. Nothing about the drawing moved. The rotation still passes with three
distinct archetypes and no two the same in a row.

**Frame 7's primary image is the WHOLE FRAME and the pixels decided that too.** It was declared as
the carved band alone, which is where the letters are. `layout_check` measures a subject as ink
that is not type, and 1019 of that band's 1326 cells were under the headline, so the frame reported
a silhouette of 0.009 against a floor of 0.08 while having a perfectly good drawn surface either
side of the carve. The porch IS the image on this frame. It is one continuous run of boards at a
true 0.140 m pitch, cropped by both edges, with a routed channel cut into it, and the rect now says
so. The drawing did not change to satisfy the gate. The declaration was wrong and the gate was
reading it correctly.

**Overruled in the third, the screen.** It argued well for a line screen as the raster of a 1080 line
capture, and then named the moire risk itself, which is that sidewalk joints, eaves, shingle courses
and kerbs are all near horizontal. September 20th also measured a line screen getting WORSE as it
coarsened over its own ground. Stipple has no angle, so the whole family of risks disappears rather
than being managed. The probe frame decides it and the stated fallback is hatch at cell 7,
deliberately not cell 6, which shipped yesterday.

## The deck's own laws, which bind every frame

**THE MUTE WORLD.** Paper is the only thing in nine frames that carries glyphs.

**THE ACCENT.** Bluebonnet #4E5FA8 is the machine's sight and lands only on the capture rectangle,
its brackets and its detection marks. Frames 1, 2, 3, 8 and 9 carry it. Frames 4, 5, 6 and 7 carry
none. Never a threshold, never a band, never a severity ramp, never a fill behind type.

**THE RESERVED RED IS NOT SPENT.** The one dated thing here is a committee briefing on October
19th, which is a door a reader may walk through and not a window closing on them. Spending the
urgent colour on a date that asks nothing would be the courtesy notice's own move committed in this
deck's ink.

**THE CONDITION LAW.** Not one of the eight detected conditions is drawn on any house. The eight
exist once, as type, on frame 3.

**NO TWO QUANTITIES TOUCH.** One mark field, on frame 5, at one scale. The other four counts are
never drawn. Nothing is nested, chained, stacked, bracketed or arrowed into anything else.

**NOTHING IS DRAWN THAT HAS NOT HAPPENED.** Frame 9's committee room is empty, no briefer is
depicted, and the rectangle on its screen carries nothing.

**MORRISON IS NOT DRAWN.** She is the story's person and her frame went to her letter. Drawing a
mark on her roof would assert a defect the record does not establish, and her own documentation was
not carried into claims.json.

**NOBODY IS ACCUSED, AND NOBODY IS CLEARED EITHER.** The record does not support a claim that
the December promises were broken, and it does not establish that they were kept: every check on that
is the city describing its own process through a newsroom.
Frame 4 draws the promise straight and carries no accent. Frame 8 draws the person who walks the
property. Frame 5 gives the memo and Reed's counter the same measure. No frame anywhere uses the
word promise, broken or surveillance.
