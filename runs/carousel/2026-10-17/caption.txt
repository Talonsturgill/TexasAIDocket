Why do some tumors keep T cells out?

MD Anderson and other Texas institutions built an AI model called SPACER that helps explain where immune cells go and why they avoid some places. It reads gene activity across neighboring cells in intact tissue.

One answer may be mucins, sugar coated proteins many cancers make. They may help wall T cells off. In several melanoma and gastric cancer datasets more mucin was associated with poorer responses to checkpoint inhibitors. The release reports no diagnostic use.

SPACER ran on 37 datasets. In the team's benchmarking it was four to 21 times more effective than existing approaches at finding signals linked to cell recruitment. The Cancer Prevention and Research Institute of Texas helped fund the study.

Could a mucin reading tell a doctor whether a checkpoint drug will work?
