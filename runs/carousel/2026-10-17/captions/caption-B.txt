Four to 21 times more effective.

That range belongs to SPACER, an AI model that MD Anderson in Houston and Texas collaborators published October 13th. Next to the word cancer it reads like a cure rate.

It is the researchers' own benchmark against existing approaches at finding signals linked to cell recruitment.

Mucins may help create physical barriers that keep T cells away from tumors. In several melanoma and gastric cancer datasets more mucin was associated with poorer responses to checkpoint inhibitors. No use in patient care is reported. The Cancer Prevention and Research Institute of Texas helped fund the study.

The slides walk through what the model reads and what it found.

Linghua Wang says it helps turn insights into new ideas to test in the lab. The release sets no date. When those tests run, does the mucin barrier hold up?
