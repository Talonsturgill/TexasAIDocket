Why do some tumors keep T cells out?

MD Anderson and other Texas institutions built an AI model called SPACER to find out. It reads gene activity across neighboring cells in intact tissue.

One answer may be mucins, sugar coated proteins many cancers make. They may help wall T cells off. In several melanoma and gastric cancer datasets more mucin was associated with poorer responses to checkpoint inhibitors. No diagnostic use is reported.

SPACER ran on 37 datasets. In the team's benchmarking it was four to 21 times more effective than existing approaches at finding signals linked to cell recruitment. The Cancer Prevention and Research Institute of Texas helped fund it.

The frames build that wall in relief, T cells banked on its outer slope.
