# Storyboard — carousel no. 24, September 14th, 2026

Docket items `tx-2026-0156` and `tx-2026-0157`. Claims at `out/2026-09-14/claims.json`, 43
verified, 11 rejected, one declared absence. Figures at `out/2026-09-14/figures.json`, computed

## The story, in one paragraph

Texas ordered a comprehensive verification and audit of every data center advancing through the
interconnection queue. The instrument that audit turned out to be is a questionnaire. ERCOT
conditionally classified the large loads in Batch Zero, published a notice that carries no load
name and no total, and then issued every one of those loads a Verification RFI with ten business
days, a notarized attestation from each of three parties, a lock on submission, and removal from
Batch Zero for a load entity that can't supply what its provider needs. Seventeen large loads
carrying 6,608 MW have finished every other ERCOT process and have no approval to energize. The
one public account of what a classification did to a named project came from a developer telling
its own shareholders, where a project it filed as base load came back conditionally classified as
studied load.

## Why this story and not the others

Five scouts came back with four live beats. The alternatives were Kodiak's driverless triple
trailers on Permian lease roads, Amazon's Austin robotics plant, an Austin counter drone maker
taking a 191,000 square foot factory, and Jacinto City measuring decibels at a fence line against
a 50 megawatt inference building. Every one of them is a good story and three of them rest on
journalism or on a corporate release with no Texas county in it.

This one is built entirely on primary documents that were fetched and read this run, it is the
only one where the record already held the decision the deck turns on, and it answers a question
none of the others do. **An audit sounds like inspectors. This one is a form.** `dedupe_check`
put the nearest prior deck at 0.46, carousel no. 3 on August 19th, which was about the SCHEDULE
the Governor's directive broke. Its topic ledger entry was read in full rather than its title.
That deck could not have carried this one, because the questionnaire it turns on was published
twenty three days later.

`texan_check` on the candidate returns places Dickens County, body yes, deadline yes, next step
yes.

## The register

**DICKENS COUNTY AND THE CAPROCK ESCARPMENT, UNDER A LOW MOON.** This is the Croton Breaks,
where red Permian siltstone is cut by white gypsum seams under a caliche rim, and `c36` and `c37`
are why the deck is standing there rather than anywhere else. Red bed paper `#120C0B` and one
gypsum ink `#E9DCC0`, printed. Every frame's scene is drawn in greys into an offscreen twin at
true scale, then `TXINK.print` lays the paper, screens the tone into ink, lays the contour plate

## The value arc

    1   8      2  80      3   8      4  58      5   9
    6   6      7   4      8  62      9   6

Deck median planned at 8. Two inversions, at frame 2 and frame 4, and a third step at 8. The
first arrives at position TWO rather than at five, so a reader meets a 130 metre building in the
dark and is handed a white sheet of paper one swipe later. The middle walks 8, 58, 9, 6 rather
than sitting flat, because seven consecutive dark frames is how a contact sheet becomes one long
dusk.

**THIS ARC WAS RECALIBRATED ONCE, AFTER THREE FRAMES AND BEFORE SIX, AND SAYING SO IS THE POINT.**
The first plan read 11, 82, 12, 34, 14, 22, 13, 86, 26, a deck median of 22, and it was written
from reasoning about how much of each frame would be paper rather than from any measurement. The
first three frames built, 9, 8 and 7, came back at 6.0, 28.8 and 3.8. The reasoning was wrong in
one specific way worth writing down: **a frame's median is violently sensitive around fifty
percent coverage.** Frame 8's sheet covers 53 per cent of the frame, so its median lands in the
gap between the dark ground and the bright paper rather than in either, and 86 was never
reachable by a page that leaves a margin. A frame reads BRIGHT to a person long before its median
says so, and those are two different measurements of two different things.

**THE SECOND THING MEASURED, and it is a craft finding rather than a number.** A mid grey in the
offscreen twin does NOT come out as a mid tone. The screen turns the twin's tone into dot SIZE on
the paper, so a grey at L* 13 prints as sparse ink on the page's own ground and the frame's median
stays at the paper. Frame 7's sheet ground was lifted from `#16110F` to `#2A231F`, a plainly
visible change in the twin, and the rendered median did not move at all, from 3.8 to 3.8. **A
frame is bright in this register only where flat colour is painted in `over`**, which is what the
two document frames do and what nothing else in this deck does. That is why frame 7 is planned at
4 rather than lifted into a third bright frame: a map is not a document the public was handed, and
making it one to hit a number would have broken the deck's own law to satisfy its own arc.

**THE THIRD THING MEASURED, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT DECIDES A SCENE FRAME'S NUMBER.** Frame 6 was
planned at 14 and came back at 3.8, the only miss in the deck over a Munsell step. The first
diagnosis was that its yard was too dark, and a second measurement said otherwise before any of it
was written down. `tmp/bands.py` reports each frame's median twice, once over the whole frame and
once over the ART BAND alone, which is what is left after `TXINK.reserve` has laid its type bands
back down in the page ground. Frame 6's art band read 4.4 where every other scene frame read 8.9
to 9.5, so the yard was genuinely the darkest thing in the deck and the caliche pad under the
substation, at `#2C2620`, was printing as nothing at all.

That was fixed on its own terms. The pad is now a graded plane from `#4A4238` at the far fence to
`#7E7563` at the near edge, running off both frame edges with a scraped shoulder, two worn wheel
tracks and nine hundred depth-sized gravel marks in it, because caliche is pale rock and a yard is
graded down to it. **The art band went from 4.4 to 10.7, the strongest of any scene frame in the
deck. The frame median moved by 0.0, from 3.8 to 3.8.**

So the number was never the yard's to move. **A frame's median is decided by its reserve bands and
its sky before its art gets a vote.** Frame 6 reserves 680 px of 1,350 for type, 50.4 per cent, and
its night sky is another 406 px, so 80 per cent of that frame is page ground by construction and
its median is the page's. No recomposition reaches it either, because the reserve is laid OVER the
art rather than beside it. The same arithmetic explains frame 3, which reserves 61.5 per cent and
sits at 4.1, and it explains why frames 1, 5 and 9 all landed within a point or two of each other
at 6.9, 8.0 and 6.5 whatever was drawn in them.

Frame 6 was therefore planned six points brighter than four structurally identical siblings for no
reason the frame could act on, and the plan now reads 6. **The pad stays.** It was a real defect in
the image and it is fixed, and the fact that fixing it bought nothing on the gauge is the finding
rather than a reason to undo it. A frame reads bright to a person long before its median says so,
which is the first mechanic above arriving a second time from the other direction.

**FRAME 8 WAS CORRECTED A THIRD TIME AND THE REASON IS A CONTRADICTION RATHER THAN A MISS.** Its
`value_structure` said in one sentence that the sheet is the lightest thing in the deck and in the
next that the frame median was planned at 29, and both cannot be true in a deck whose frame 2 is
at 80. The 29 was measured off a first cut in which `TXINK.reserve` was painting dark ground over
the sheet's foot, and that reserve came off as a QA repair, because its own hard edge was running
through the source line as a strikethrough. Removing it was not a design change and it put the
frame at 62, which is where the prose always said it was. The plan now says 62 and the two
sentences agree.

So the numbers moved and the shape did not. Two bright frames, a third step, six frames on the
floor, a seventy six point spread. Recalibrating a plan from three measurements before the other
six frames exist is planning with better information. The frame 6 correction above is the one that
came after its render, and it is a correction rather than a capitulation for a reason that can be
checked by anyone: the art was fixed FIRST, the fix was measured, and only when the measurement
showed the frame's median could not respond to any art at all did the number move. Rewriting a
plan because nine frames missed would be fitting the plan to the art, which
`SLIDE_DOSSIER_SPEC.md` forbids and which is why these paragraphs are here rather than a silent
edit.

`out/2026-09-14/measure.py` prints the plan beside the measurement the moment a render exists, and
`panel_ready` compares the deck medians and refuses a miss over one Munsell step.
