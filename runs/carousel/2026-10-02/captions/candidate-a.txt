Software did not diagnose this child.

The child was enrolled in the Texome Project. Standard genetic analysis found no answer. Then AI-MARRVEL, a Baylor classifier trained on over 3.5 million variants, flagged a rare change in a gene called BRSK1.

That flag was a lead, and testing it took more families and fruit flies.

Through GeneMatcher the study grew to 10 affected people from seven unrelated families. In flies the gene is sff, for "sugar-free frosting." Switched off, the flies had trouble moving and lived shorter lives. The normal human gene largely corrected them. The patients' versions only partly did.

The Houston release from Texas Children's calls BRSK1 a likely diagnosis.

Texome offers free testing to families who can't pay for sequencing. It states a plan to sequence roughly 400 children. How many has it reached?
