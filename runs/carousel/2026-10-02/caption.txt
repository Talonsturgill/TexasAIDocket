Software did not diagnose this child.

The child was enrolled in the Texome Project. Standard analysis found no answer. Then AI-MARRVEL flagged a rare change in a gene called BRSK1.

That flag was a lead. Testing it took families and flies.

Through GeneMatcher the study grew to 10 affected people from seven unrelated families. In flies the gene is sff, for "sugar-free frosting." Switched off, the flies had trouble moving and lived shorter lives. The normal human gene largely corrected them. The three patient versions only partly did.

Every affected person had some developmental delay. Texas Children's calls the variants a likely diagnosis.

Texome takes candidates from underserved families with a suspected rare disease who can't pay. Testing is free and visits can be virtual. Could a family you know write to texome-project@bcm.edu?
