## The story (docket tx-2026-0257, claims in out/2026-11-22/claims.json, c1 to c20, the ONLY facts you may use)
The IC² Institute and Dell Medical School at UT Austin announced the AI Learning Lab on October 20th, 2026 (c1). The
announcement said it would launch later that month with 80 Dell Med clinicians (c2), a mix of faculty, residents and
students who meet patients in simulated encounters (c3). The patients are paid actors presenting a medical issue (c6).
Each clinician meets the patient, asks questions, synthesizes information and arrives at a diagnosis (c7). Half of
the clinicians have Claude at their fingertips to assist with diagnosing (c4). The other half rely on UpToDate, a web
based medical encyclopedia, with its AI capability disabled (c5). Researchers observe and compare the clinical
reasoning of both groups and analyze AI use across experience levels (c8). Researcher Sookja Kang's questions. How
quickly do participants click on their AI tool (c9)? Are they passively accepting AI output or actively interrogating
it (c10)? How much reasoning work gets handed off to the AI tool as the diagnosis evolves (c11)? The lab uses a live
patient encounter rather than a static text vignette (c12), because, in researcher Tu-Quyen Dao's words, a large part
of solving a real case is interacting with the patient to gather information (c13). They measure with rubrics,
observation and analysis of post encounter interviews (c16). Stated aims include a taxonomy of LLM engagement
strategies (c19) and identifying new clinician skills that grow out of AI augmented reasoning (c20). Vice Dean of
Education James Korndorffer says AI is going to be, and should be, used (c14). The release cites a 2026 American
Medical Association survey, 81% of doctors using AI in practice against 38% reported in 2023 (c15, attribute to the
release citing the AMA). Results could help Dell Med and other schools decide how and when AI enters the curriculum
(c17). It is a joint project of the two (c18).
