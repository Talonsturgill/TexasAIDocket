# Slide 05 concepts, QUESTION ONE (c9), "How fast do they click?"

1. THE RAIL AT REST. A worm's eye eleven centimetres above a warm limestone desk, looking down an anodised indigo timing rail whose brass graduations tighten as they recede toward a laptop glowing plain cyan, the knurled brass carriage parked hard against the near zero stop and three brass question tabs at the rail's foot with the first lit. Only this beat has it because the question is literally a reach measured in time, and a rail with nothing yet travelled shows the distance to the tool while timing nothing.

2. THE HOVERING CURSOR HAND, IN SILHOUETTE. A long lens from behind and below a clinician's cropped shoulder and forearm, the index finger held a centimetre above a trackpad whose laptop screen glows blank cyan, a brass stopwatch on the desk beside it with its hand at the top. The click is the subject, but kit hands read soft up close and a stopwatch implies a timed result, so it risks both a mannequin and an outcome.

3. THE PLAN OF THE REACH. An orthographic plan straight down on an exam room desk drawn as a cast relief, a dashed brass arc swinging from a chair to a laptop like an architect's door swing, the arc unlabelled. It names reach as geometry, but a top down plan reads as a chart (charged lately) and frame 4 is already a plan view relief.

**Pick: 1.** It is the only one that could not run under another headline: a rail measuring the way to a glowing tool, at zero, is "how fast do they click" before anything has happened, with no hand to fail and no result implied. It also differs from frame 4 (plan relief) by camera and from frame 6 (slips seen from 60 cm) by its worm's eye macro scale.

**Type.** Headline across the top over the quiet blue sweep in two big lines, left aligned. The dek sits in a short column at left, above the horizon, beside the laptop which stands right of centre at the end of the rail. The rail rises from the lower left to the laptop, so the lower half is all object and the upper half is all quiet wall.
