# Slide 3 concepts, THE ACTOR ("The patient is an actor")

1. THE DOORWAY. A simulation room seen from its open doorway at standing eye height, an indigo jamb cutting the near left, an actor in a patterned gown sitting on a paper covered exam table with their back to the reader and a clinician on a rolling stool turned three quarters away, both backlit by a tall morning window, a single page lying face down and untouched on a near desk. Only this beat has a live encounter caught mid conversation with the written case literally turned over, and the reader standing where the next clinician walks in.

2. THE DRESSING ROOM. A close studio still life of an actor's mirror ringed with warm bulbs, a folded patient gown and a patient wristband on the counter in front of it and a hanger empty on the hook, nobody in frame. It says "actor playing patient" in one glance, but it shows the costume and loses the live encounter, which is half the beat.

3. THE CUTAWAY SET. A raked aerial over a three walled exam room built as a stage set, its outside walls braced with timber and sandbags, a stage lamp on a stand, inside it the actor and clinician lit like a scene. It reads "performance", but it risks reading as fake or televised, and it shares frame 1's cutaway miniature world.

**Pick: 1, THE DOORWAY.** It is the only one that carries both halves of the sentence, a person on the table who is playing the patient and a conversation happening live with the page face down, and its eye level, in the room camera sets it apart from frame 1's cutaway model and frame 4's flat switch relief.

Composition: the room floor is a deep cobalt sheet vinyl with the window's warm light lying across it, so the bottom third is a saturated quiet field. The headline sits bottom left in two lines over that floor and the dek runs under it, short of the desk at bottom right. The top two thirds carry the window, the actor and the clinician.

## Rebuild after the round 1 critic (5.5)

I kept the doorway concept and staged it as a simulation. The window is gone. The back wall is now a one way mirror with two observers in silhouette in a dim booth behind it, and there are two wall cameras with lit tallies and a boom mic hung over the pair. The actor has a blank clipped role badge and sits on the table's foot end with her shoulders turned to the clinician. He sits three quarters away from the reader, leaning in, with one hand raised. The face down page has its back edge lifted. The floor is violet sheet vinyl with welded seams, and the desk no longer reaches the footer.
