# Selection — September 18th, 2026

## The story

Hays County, Texas. Three commissioners court agendas in one month, each about whether a machine
may write an official record, and no published disposition for any of them.

- **September 1st.** The court's agenda carries an item acknowledging the Sheriff's Office
  commitment "to not activate or implement Axon Draft One", described on the agenda itself as "an
  AI-enabled report-writing feature within the Axon AI Era platform". The commitment reaches any
  new AI feature the platform later offers, and conditions operational use on returning to the
  court for approval.
- **September 8th.** The same court takes up the "use, implementation, oversight, and governance
  of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in connection with surveillance equipment" and contemplates
  adopting a policy statement.
- **September 15th.** The County Clerk's Office asks the same court to "obtain AI Minutes through
  Govably, Inc." for "$19,200.00 for year one", and to waive the purchasing policy that requires
  three quotes.
- **The spine, and it is an absence.** The county's own Court Minutes 2026 archive lists nothing
  later than "07 28 2026". No outcome for any of the three is on the public record.

## Why this story and not the others

**It is AI IN USE rather than a filing about AI.** `APPLICATIONS.md` is explicit that the docket
already publishes every decision every day, so a deck that narrates a filing back is the deck this
product should stop making. This one is about two machines doing a specific job, writing a police
report and writing the minutes of a public meeting, and about one county reaching opposite
postures toward them inside fifteen days.

**The record now holds it.** Phase 5 admitted `tx-2026-0168`, `tx-2026-0169` and `tx-2026-0170`,
so the deck is built on decisions the docket carries rather than on something the site does not
publish.

**It is genuinely placed.** `texan_check` returns Hays County and San Marcos at selection, a body,
and a date. The county is a real Texan's county rather than a statewide abstraction, and the
record had three Hays County entries before today against fifteen for Harris.

**The instrument is new.** The last eight decks read ERCOT board presentations, NSF award records,
radiology journals, a hospital press release and a state board rule. **A county commissioners
court agenda, read straight off the county's own portal, is an instrument this product has not
used.** `dedupe_check` returned "nothing close" against the full thirty day window on entities and
keywords both.

## What was considered and turned down

- **The statewide Flock plate reader story**, which is the loudest thing in Texas this week. Turned
  down on two grounds. No primary document for the TxDOT permit suspension could be fetched, so it
  rests on journalism alone, and the AI framing is contested by the police departments running it.
  A deck whose central claim is "this is AI" that the operators deny is a deck this record can't
  carry. **El Paso's own plate reader vote went into the record instead**, as a re-verification
  finding on `tx-2026-0147`, which is where it belongs.
- **Kodiak's driverless Permian fleet.** Thirty five trucks with nobody in the cab is a strong
  image and the release names no Texas county. The Permian spans two states. Held in the seed.
- **The FAA's Zipline environmental assessment.** `faa.gov` returned 403 on four attempts, so the
  only account of it is a news story about a document nobody here read.
- **Samsung and Mistral.** The release names no Texas site, so applying it to Taylor would be
  inference rather than reporting.

## The hazard this story carries, named before the deck was planned

**No outcome is published for any of the three items, and the fact checker rejected the framing
that the court "gave opposite answers" by name.** An agenda states what was scheduled and never
what was decided, and the county's minutes archive stops in July. Every frame and the caption are
built so a reader can't come away thinking the court resolved anything. That constraint shaped the
deck more than any other decision in it, including drawing the two page frames at different
distances and rotations so they never read as a before and an after.
