# Carousel no. 77, November 14th, 2026: one wafer, one million

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

Texas Tech's Whitacre College of Engineering received an additional Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund award of over $19
million (c1), announced November 13th, 2026 (c2). It will help Texas Tech establish a next-generation computing cluster in
Lubbock (c3). The cluster is not built and the announcement gives no install date (c4). A cluster is servers working as a
single unified system on massive, parallel workloads (c5). The project will integrate AI with high-fidelity simulation data
and experimental results (c6) to shorten development cycles for semiconductor devices and materials (c7). The dean says
Texas Tech will be one of the first universities with access to the newest generation of Nvidia chips to power the cluster
(c8). Roland Faller leads the team (c9). Planned work includes AI-driven materials discovery and modeling (c10) and
AI-enabled epitaxial growth of ultrawide-bandgap semiconductors (c11). Conventional design relies heavily on physics-based
modeling (c12). The president thanked Governor Abbott and state leaders (c13). Earlier awards from the fund went to a clean
room and to wide and ultrawide bandgap work (c14): $12 million in January (c15), matched by Texas Tech for a $24 million
project (c16), and roughly $4.5 million in May (c17). The fund is administered by the Texas CHIPS Office in the Office of
the Governor (c18) and was created by the Texas CHIPS Act in 2023 (c19).

The fact check held Shield AI's X-BAT prototype out of the record, because no source places it in one county. It found no
team count on the page and none is printed. The governor's own listing is off limits to this project and is not cited.
"Over $19 million" is a floor and "roughly $4.5 million" is approximate, so no frame sums, subtracts or divides them.

Docket item tx-2026-0247, Lubbock County, on ERCOT.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Base: ONE WAFER, ONE MILLION. The story is three payments from one fund to one college, and the only honest comparison the
claims allow is counted size. The money pays for semiconductor work grown on wafers, so the counting unit belongs to the
story. The two inexact figures become physical states rather than footnotes: exact is a boat capped at both ends, roughly
is four wafers and a cleaved half, over is nineteen wafers in a boat with no far end plate. Grafted from ONE LAYER AT A
TIME: frame 7's growth surface with a top layer of separate islands that have not joined, which is the cluster's job drawn
without a result. Grafted from HARVEST IN, MACHINE NOT YET: the close names only what the November 13th announcement leaves
out. Dropped: layer thickness as dollars (it reads as a stacked bar chart), the field world and the cotton row ruler (it
implies a site the record does not hold), the Capitol, and a reactor build no claim needs.

## The world, and the laws that hold it

`floodlit`, staged, and entirely indoors. One cold LED flood hangs high on the left at azimuth -58 and elevation 28, so every
cast runs down and to the right across the bench and every wafer rim catches it. The lab is dark around that one source. A
warm strip low on the back wall sits BEHIND the clear quartz and the mirror wafers, because glass on a dark field shows
nothing unless something lit is behind it (THE STAGE, rule 6). No frame has a sky, so the lastLight horizon band the queue
records can't appear. The reliefs (2, 7, 9) read the same key from the chassis. Type is light on the dark wall. A wash only
darkens.
