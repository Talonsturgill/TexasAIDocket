# Carousel no. 71, November 7th, 2026: the curbside camera

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

Dallas put AI enabled cameras on its sanitation brush trucks (c1). The city's December 2025 briefing to the council's
Finance Committee says the cameras face sideways from the vehicle and capture images from the public right of way
(c5), take photos as the truck drives at 25 to 35 MPH (c4), have a fixed focal length with no zoom (c7), and blur faces
and license plates (c6). Its software detects eight listed property conditions, from bulk trash participation to high
grass (c8). The same briefing says "NOT automated ticketing" (c12) and "There is a human in the loop for all actions"
(c13). The contract is $852,000 a year (c10), an estimated $2,556,000 over three years (c9), and the city may end it on
10 days' written notice (c11).

The city's September 8th memo, as FOX 4 and Dallas Express reported it, says the cameras scanned 10,000 properties
(c46), that about 3,700 property owners were sent courtesy notices (c53), that Code Compliance opened about 300 cases for
in-person inspection (c52), and that 57 citations went to 34 properties after code officers reviewed and confirmed the
detections (c47, c50). A citation comes only after an officer inspects the property and notifies the owner (c47). The
city says a courtesy notice is not a citation and requires no action (c54). By the city manager's memo, 39 of the 57
citations were in Council Districts 4, 8 and 7 in southern Dallas (c55). The software assigns blight scores that set the
order of review (c51). Council member Chad West withdrew a move to cut the funding once the city manager agreed to a
briefing (c56).
