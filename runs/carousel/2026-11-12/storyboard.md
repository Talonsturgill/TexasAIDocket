# Carousel no. 75, November 12th, 2026: the roster

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

Since May 28th, 2026 every company operating automated vehicles commercially on Texas roads must hold an active
authorization from the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (c26), under Senate Bill 2807 (c22, c25). The department's
Company and Vehicle Summary Report, data as of October 29th, 2026 (c20), lists 17 companies (c18) and 3,164 automated
vehicles (c19). Waymo lists 1,259 (c16) and Tesla Robotaxi 1,080 (c13). Avride lists 344 (c2), and fourteen companies
list fewer than 100, down to International Transport Eng at 7 (c6). The holder keeps its own list of active vehicles
(c35). An authorization carries no fee (c32) and never expires (c33). The City of Dallas asked the department to require
crash history and to make approved operators public (c50), and the department answered that the Transportation Code
doesn't allow it to require crash history (c51) and that public access turns on Chapter 552 (c52). The department treats
an operation as endangering the public at serious bodily injury (c38) and says enforcement below that is unlikely (c37).
A concern about serious bodily injury or death goes to its Enforcement Division (c36). On the two freight lanes in the
record a person still sits up front: Volvo says its Waabi hauls run with an observer in the driver's seat (c62, c63),
and Kodiak and Charger say they run with a safety driver behind the wheel (c64, c65).

The fact check held the deck to its words. The report never says authorized, commercial or SB 2807, so the link rides
on the program page (c26, c35). The freight statements are never joined to the Waabi or Kodiak rows. No source says the
public can or can't look an authorization up, so the deck claims neither. Atlas Energy Solutions' 38 are never called
Kodiak trucks. Concerns, not complaints.

Docket item tx-2026-0188, updated this run with the October 29th report. Statewide, with Dallas, Houston and Laredo named
only through the claims that name them.

parking lot. One stand-in car stands for ten listed vehicles, in rows of ten bays, so two names fill two long columns
and fourteen companies never fill a single row. Grafted from THE CREDENTIAL: the steel rule with a hard stop at May
28th and graduations running off the edge with no end stop (5), the cleanest physical sentence for no expiry. Grafted
from THE SEAT: the cab with a person in the driver's seat (8), kept apart from the lot and carrying no accent, so the
freight statements never touch a roster row. Dropped: the card drawer (a census in a box reads as a chart at feed
