## The story (docket item tx-2026-0194, statewide, health-and-education)
The Texas Education Agency scores the written (constructed) answers on English STAAR tests with an automated scoring
engine first. It began in December 2023 (c9, c21). As TEA described the design in December 2023 and March 2024, every
written answer goes through the engine and at least 25 percent are also routed to people (c13, c22); where a person scores
it, the person's score counts (c17). The engine is programmed per question on about 3,000 human scored field test answers
(c20), and TEA calls it a closed database rather than AI that teaches itself (c23). Spanish STAAR answers are scored by
people only (c16). TEA's reasons: the redesigned test brought 6-7x more written answers a year (c19); scoring by hand would
cost $15 million to $20 million more a year (c10, c18) and need four to five times as many scorers (c11). A spokesperson
says the system keeps human judgment at the center (c12).
THE TURN: districts began asking for human rescores of reading answers. 2,758 requests in spring 2024 (c1), 21,620 the next
year (c2), 64,489 in spring 2026 (c3), more than 23 times the 2024 count (computed). People did every rescore (c7, c14). The
state raised more than 13,300 reading scores after the reviews (c4), at least one in five of that spring's requests
(computed floor), while fewer than .5% of all open-ended reading responses changed (c5). Dallas ISD sent more than 5,100
reading tests and 34% came back higher (c8). A district pays $50 per test if the score does not change (c6); the fee is
waived if it changes (c15).
