# Carousel, November 17th, 2026: the machine reads first, and districts asked a person to read again

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

The Texas Education Agency scores the written answers on English STAAR tests with an automated scoring engine first, since December 2023 (c9, c21). As TEA described the design in December 2023 and March 2024, every written answer goes through the engine and at least 25 percent are also routed to people (c13, c22), and a person's score counts (c17). Each question's engine is programmed on about 3,000 human scored field test answers (c20), and TEA calls it a closed database rather than AI that teaches itself (c23). Spanish answers are scored by people alone (c16). The redesigned test brought 6-7x more written answers (c19). Hand scoring would cost $15 million to $20 million more a year (c10) and take four to five times the scorers (c11). Requests to regrade reading answers went from 2,758 in spring 2024 (c1) to 21,620 (c2) and 64,489 this spring (c3). People did every rescore (c7, c14). The state raised more than 13,300 reading scores (c4), while fewer than .5% of all open-ended reading responses changed (c5). Dallas ISD sent more than 5,100 tests and 34% came back higher (c8). The fee is $50 a test when the score holds (c6) and waived when it changes (c15).

The fact check held the TEA design figures to their December 2023 and March 2024 documents, left the 2026 counts on the Texas Tribune's reporting because no TEA page answered, rejected a 42/58 split whose referent was unclear, and separated the 27,200 figure (a different TEA review) from the requests. The deck leaves c24 to c26 out.
