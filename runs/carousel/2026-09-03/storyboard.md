# Storyboard — carousel no. 14, 2026-09-03

## The story

The Texas A&M University System's VISION supercomputer entered the TOP500 at number 66.
Two pages the System publishes describe who it is for, and both of them use the future tense. A
third page, the machine's own documentation, says the present tense out loud. Access is
invitation-only under a controlled beta.

**The deck is not a gotcha and must never read as one.** The two texts never disagreed. The
documentation is the System publishing its own limit, which is the most creditable thing in the
whole record. The deck's job is to make a reader notice a verb they read past, then be fair about
what the verb means, then hand them the real next step.

## The synthesis, and what came from where

Three treatments were pitched. The spine is **the two documents**, in a transmitted-light register,
and it won for four measured reasons rather than on taste.

- **A probe rendered before any dossier was written proved its hero technique.** A word knocked
  clean through an opaque leaf with light behind it passes the art-crossing-glyphs gate and the
  occlusion gate with zero failures, and it reads at size without a plate, a scrim or a halo. The
  halo is spent from deck 12, so a defence that needs none is worth having.
- **It answers the run's hardest constraint honestly.** No source fetched names a county or a
  city for the West Campus Data Center, so this deck may not put the story on the ground. A
  register built from the institution's own paper needs no ground.
- **Nothing in the fourteen entry artwork ledger is lit from behind.** Every one of the thirteen
  shipped decks has a directional key. This one has no key at all, because the light is under the
  work rather than over it, and no frame casts a shadow.
- **It is buildable.** Masks, wedges, lattices and transition fronts are 2D compositing with
  predictable failure modes, where the alternative wanted a raymarched wall, a Zdog rig and a
  contour set in one deck.

**Grafted from the room and the queue treatment**, and it improves the deck materially.

- **Slide 8 is that treatment's best single idea**, twelve figures and eight figures drawn
  identically with no opening on any of them, never summed. It replaces the spine's own slide 8
  and it makes 8 and 9 rhyme, because slide 9's registration pins are what an institution that has
  completed technical integration is punched to.
- **Filled means the source states it. Outlined means the source calls it expected.** Slide 9's
  future leaf position is outlined for exactly that reason.
- **Twelve and eight are never added.** They are different kinds of institution and no source adds
  them. `compute.py` records the refusal rather than just omitting the value.
- **The two compound counts are never joined.** c18 says approximately 10.4 million on the
  project's own use cases page and c19 says more than 10 million in about a week. Nothing in the
  record joins them, so slide 5 prints both with their own attributions.
- **The reserved red stays unspent, and the deck says why.** c16 gives no date. A colour whose one
  job is a deadline a reader can act on stays in the box when the record holds no deadline.

**Grafted from the scale and population treatment**, which was not taken as the spine because its
terrazzo register collides with deck 3 inside the recent window, and it said so itself.

- **The count nobody publishes.** The documentation states the access rule and states NO NUMBER,
  so how many researchers can use VISION today is not in this record. That is CLAUDE.md's own law
  about publishing the size of what is not public, and it is the strongest single line in the
  three pitches. It goes on slide 8, beside the twenty figures, and it names the page it looked at
  so `absence_check` has a document to find.
- **About a week is not a duration this record can turn into a rate**, so nothing anywhere divides
  a compound count by a span.
- **The coordinates footer takes the source domain rather than a county**, which the third
  treatment independently reached with the same precedent the second one named.

## The register

**A university print room, seen entirely by transmitted light.** Diazo prints on a light table, one
document over another, lit from behind the paper rather than from above. Nothing in nine frames
casts a shadow, because there is nothing above to cast one. Depth is density and occlusion.

**Density is agreement.** Where two documents state the same thing, two impressions stack and the
ink goes to double density. Where one states it alone, single density. Where neither does, the
light comes straight through. Nothing else in the deck encodes with density.

**Blueline is a published document. Sepia is a person quoted.** Nothing else wears sepia.

### The value arc

which is written by resampling each render to 216 by 270 and taking the median L\* of the pixels.**

Per frame median L*, computed:

`73.1 · 94.0 · 17.8 · 76.9 · 16.5 · 26.9 · 16.6 · 92.3 · 92.3`, **deck median 73.1**.

**THE FIRST VERSION OF THIS LINE WAS MEASURED ON THE WRONG GRID AND DISAGREED BY 1.1.** It read a deck median of 72.0, resampled to 216 by 270 by a script written for this run. Every prior run measured on 270 by 338, and `shipped_check`'s measured-figures gate reads `measurements.json`, which is written by `measure.py` on that grid. Two grids is two homes for one figure, which is the defect that gate exists for, so this line and the artwork ledger now both derive from that file and nothing here is resampled twice.

**The inversion is slide 7 and it runs the wrong way.** Every prior inversion is a bright frame in

### Structural laws every frame is held to

1. **No cast shadow anywhere.** Each frame publishes its cast shadow draw call count and asserts
   zero. Slide 7 is an exemption to the backlight, never to the shadow law.
2. **Density is agreement**, and nothing else encodes with density.
3. **Blueline is a document, sepia is a person quoted.**
4. **Filled is stated, outlined is expected.**
5. **Every figure is a DOM or SVG text node in JetBrains Mono with tabular numerals**, never
   Fraunces, so a measurement always looks like a measurement.
6. **No county, no city, no coordinate, no place name.** The footer carries the facility name c7
   gives rather than a coordinate, because the brand's county first footer has nothing legal to
   print on this story.
7. **No numeral absent from `claims.json` or `computed.json`.**
8. **Twelve and eight are never added, and the two compound counts are never joined.**
9. **Furniture sits at 80px or more from every edge**, measured on the probe, which warned at 60.
10. **No maroon and no burnt orange.** A university colour turns a record into a booster deck.

### How a frame ASSERTS something, and why it is not `window.__txProbe`

Deck 13's ledger entry describes frames publishing measurements to `window.__txProbe`, and this
run checked whether anything reads it. **Nothing does.** `render.py` collects `text_nodes`,
`rules`, `svg_plates`, `canvases`, `encodings`, `contacts`, `leaders`, `console_errors` and
`page_errors`, and no gate in the repo looks at a global of that name. An acceptance item resting
on it is unverifiable, which is deck 13's own second finding wearing a different hat.

So this deck asserts through the two channels that are actually wired up.

- **A thrown error.** `render.py` records an uncaught exception as a `page_error` and `qa.py`
  turns every page error into a FAIL. So a frame that counts what it drew and throws when the
  count is wrong cannot ship. **This is what makes an acceptance item fail a frame that draws
  NOTHING**, which is the craft memory's fourth instinct and deck 13's own lesson.
- **`data-encodes` on `<body>`.** qa.py measures the declared regions at 432px wide, reports the
  CIELAB distance and rank separability, and FAILS a `differ` pair under 4.0 dE. That is the only
  mechanism here that judges what the art SAYS rather than what the markup claims.

A frame still writes its measurements to `window.__txAssert` so a pixel critic reading the page can see it

## Three plans that changed during the build, and why

A dossier that quietly follows its render is not a plan. These three changed, each for a reason
that came from a gate rather than from taste, and the change is recorded here rather than absorbed.

- **Slide 2's hook was "The list publishes two numbers."** `aggregate_check` detected "two numbers"
  as a count the deck invented, and it was right. The two figures live in ONE claim, so there is no
  claim per unit to declare it from, and declaring it any other way would have been the dishonest
  route the gate's own header warns about. The hook is now "What was run, and what was rated.",
  which carries the same idea and invents no number.
- **Slide 8's hook was "Twelve universities. Eight state agencies."** The counts moved to tags
  beside their own courses and the hook took the argument instead. A first attempt read "Twenty
  sheets." and that was worse, because twenty is twelve plus eight and no source adds them.
- **Slide 5 was rebuilt around its opening.** The first render put the two counts under an empty
  pale shape and the contact sheet showed a frame with nothing in it. The counts now sit INSIDE the
  punch, so a reader is looking through the sheet at what the machine did, and the frame asserts
  that every corner of both rules lies inside the opening.

## The frame that would be cut if the deck had to be eight

**Slide 3.** The counts are inventory. Slide 2 already establishes that the record carries real
measured figures and slide 5 already proves the machine does real work, so slide 3 carries the
least argument of the nine. Cutting it would cost the punched aperture drawing, which slide 5's
enlargement depends on, so the cut is not free.

## Deck level risks, named before they happen

- **Nine backlit frames with no shadow anywhere could read as nine flat frames.** Four frames carry
  depth by occlusion or by a density ramp rather than by value alone. This is the biggest bet in
  the deck.
- **Slides 4 and 6 both carry sepia** and could resolve to one picture. That pair is measured with
  `bespoke_check` rather than asserted to hold.
- **Slides 3 and 5 are the same object at two magnifications**, which is deliberate and is also
  exactly how a closest pair happens. Measured, not assumed.
- **Diazo reads as generic blueprint kitsch** if drafting furniture creeps in. No title blocks, no
  neatlines, no borders anywhere.
- **The tense argument can be heard as pedantry.** Slides 4 and 8 carry the fairness and slide 9
  carries a real next step. If any of those three lands weakly the deck reads as a gotcha, which is
  the one thing this record can't afford.
