## The world, and why the register is dark
The light deck allowance (one in eight) was spent on September 30th, so this deck must measure
DARK: deck median L* under 60, ideally 25 to 40 like nos. 39 to 41. That rules out overcast and
high noon as they render by default. Choose among `blueHour`, `nightSodium`, `stormFront`, or
`goldenHour` INTO the sun. Recent worlds: blue hour (no. 39, October 1st), golden hour (no. 40),
storm front (no. 41), high noon (no. 42). A hospital is the building that never goes dark, and
the craft refresh this morning was about exactly that: at blue hour the facade and the sky sit
at nearly one tonal density and the lit windows carry the warm against the cool. If you pick
blue hour, tune it so it does not look like no. 39 (no. 39 was a highway town sign, mauve
suppressed, clouds off).

## Craft refresh this morning
- Blue hour exteriors work when the walls sit at nearly the sky's tonal density and interiors
  glow warm against the cool (studiomatrx.org, Mood and Atmosphere with Light, August 2026;
  scottkelby.com guest post, Seamus Payne).
- Window glass in three.js: MeshPhysicalMaterial transmission costs an extra render pass; lit
  windows read better as emissive panes plus an environment reflection (threejs.org docs).
- Glass realism comes from varying the glass across windows rather than one material for all
  (evermotion.org tutorials).
