# Phase 9 — the directors room, and what was taken from whom

Three directors were spawned in parallel, each handed the current rotation rule in its spawn
prompt because their own definitions under `.claude/` still carry the superseded one and no
routine may edit them.

## What the showrunner measured before synthesising, and it changed two decisions

**1. The screen's value ceiling on a dark ground, measured rather than reasoned about.** Both
director A and director B planned an EXEMPTION, painting some frames unscreened after the press,
because each believed its chosen screen could not reach the light end of its own value arc.
`out/2026-09-17/screen_ceilings.json` is a printed black field, mid field and white field through
seven configurations on this deck's own ground and ink, read at 432 px:

    halftone c6   floor  4.0   mid 53.6   ceiling 87.0
    halftone c8   floor  4.0   mid 53.6   ceiling 88.0
    line     c4   floor  4.0   mid 45.3   ceiling 84.1
    line     c5   floor  4.0   mid 44.2   ceiling 83.3
    hatch    c6   floor  4.0   mid 19.8   ceiling 42.6
    hatch    c7   floor  4.0   mid 21.2   ceiling 46.6
    stipple  c5   floor  4.0   mid  9.4   ceiling 25.2

Director B chose `hatch` at cell 6 and planned frames at L\* 45 and 52. The ceiling is 42.6, so
that plan could not print and B said so itself as its risk 6. Director A chose `line` at cell 5
and planned an exemption it did not need, because line c5 reaches 83.3. **Neither exemption is
taken.** The deck runs ONE screen with no frame painted outside it, which is what the chassis law
asks for, and the screen is `halftone` at cell 6: it has the widest range of the seven, it is the
newspaper photograph register this story belongs in, and it is the one that does not run parallel
marks under the parallel ruled hairlines the message motif is made of, where a line screen would
moire.

This is the mirror of the defect the 2026-09-16 run measured from the other side, where a pale
stock turned the ceiling into a floor and cost that run a re-plan of its closing frame.

## The synthesis, and what was taken from whom

**The base is director C's treatment**, on the lens THE GAP. Four reasons, and none of them is
that it was the longest.

1. **Its one image is the best in the room and it is the method rather than a metaphor for it.**
   The bottom 140 mm of the printed release at one to one on a table at night, and down the
   right margin the eight search terms that established the absence, each with a box beside it,
   all eight empty. A reader is not told the release is silent. They are shown the search.
2. **Its accent law is the strongest of the three.** The accent outlines an empty rule on four
   frames and fills one card on the fifth. Hollow everywhere the door is not, solid at the one
   place it is. Every use is a stroke or a small card, so the eight percent ceiling is never
   near.
3. **It carries the next step texan_check asked for at selection.** Frame 9 is a counter, in the
   morning, with the two things a Texan can actually do, and its dek keeps c18's caveat that the
   statute requires a complaint mechanism rather than that one was confirmed to exist.
4. **It chose halftone for a reason the showrunner's own probe independently confirms**, and it
   spends TYPE_AS_OBJECT on nothing with an argument worth keeping: every strong sentence in
   this story is a quotation off a page, and carving one into the ground would turn a live
   statutory ambiguity into a slogan.

**Grafted from director B, and it is the single best idea any of the three had.**

> **THE GLYPH LAW. A drawn line of type gets glyphs if and only if this run fetched those
> glyphs.** The release, the article and the statute are set in real type, verbatim, because
> those documents were read. **The patient's own message is the one unglyphed document in the
> deck**, drawn as ruled hairlines at true line lengths, because nobody published what it says.
> And nothing anywhere is drawn as a filled bar, a strike or a smudge, because a black field
> says something was REMOVED and the record says nothing was ever written.

This is load bearing rather than decorative. Director C's frame 2 draws a plain language draft
on a clinician's display, and no document this run read publishes one word of what such a draft
says. Under B's law that frame's draft is ruled hairlines and the fabrication cannot happen.

**Grafted from director A: the accent hex, on the measurement rather than on taste.** C's law is
kept exactly and its colour is not. `signal_open #4FC79A` measures dE 23.30 against the window
where A's `standby #EFA31D` measures 39.95, and `signal_open` is a site status token rather than
a deck accent, which brand.yaml's rotation note makes the exception rather than the rule. Amber
also carries both halves of C's law honestly, reading as not yet written where it is hollow and
as the thing to do where it is filled.

**Not taken, and why.** Director A's frame 9, a parking garage three miles away lit by the same
phone eight frames later, is the most beautiful single payoff pitched today. It is cut because
it leaves the deck's one continuous surface for one frame and buys an image at the cost of the
spine, and because it closes on a person who can do nothing rather than on the counter where
they can. Director B's frame 9, a phone lying on the statute page with one more empty ruled line
under the last, is cut for the narrower reason that it ends the deck on an absence when
`texan_check` has already said in writing that this story's risk is having no next step.
