# Carousel, November 25th, 2026: the second finding

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

UTMB Health announced on November 19th that AI now supports its lung cancer screening work (c2). The program began in 2016 for patients at high risk (c3), directed by Dr. Shawn Nishi (c4). The old review sometimes took weeks or even months (c5), and the follow up window had often closed (c6). The current system, developed with Bunkerhill, reviews radiology reports (c8, c9), and any nodule on a scan that includes part of the lungs (c10) is flagged for patient navigator Amanda Rodriguez (c11, c12), whose dashboard computes a validated risk score to prioritize follow up (c13, c14). Much of the chart review is automated (c30). Dr. Nishi calls AI an assistant, not a decision maker (c15, c16). Roughly two thirds of the nodules found came from patients not having lung cancer screening (c17). Dr. Nishi's patient in her 20s had a scan after a car accident, a nodule found incidentally, lung cancer on biopsy and resection for cure (c18 to c20), and named seven or eight relatives with cancer (c21, c22). Federal guidance recommends screening at 50 to 80 with a 20 pack-year history (c28). The fact check carried 28 claims and rejected any accuracy figure, any count, any after adoption timing, Bunkerhill's other marketing figures, the Brock score as UTMB's, and robotic bronchoscopy as part of the flagging system.

Docket item tx-2026-0260, Galveston County, health-and-education.
