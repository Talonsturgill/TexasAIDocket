# Slide 2 concepts, "Weeks, sometimes months" (THE OLD WAY)

1. THE LEANING TOWER. Nine stuffed manila charts stacked on a powder coated records cart in a records room, each with a colour coded edge label (six periwinkle, three frosted white), dog ears, sheets spilling, one rubber band, the stack leaning out past the right edge while low apricot window light rakes mullion shadows across a violet wall and long caster shadows over the floor. Only the old way has this picture: review done by hand, a pile a person has to work through, and the last light of the day crossing it.

2. THE CLOSING WINDOW. A records office window at eye level with venetian blinds three quarters down, the last apricot slit of light falling across an in tray of charts on a sill, a staff member's shoulder cropped at frame edge. The follow up window that "had often closed" made literal, with the blinds as the closing.

3. THE AISLE. A worm's eye view down a long aisle of violet lateral filing cabinets, drawers pulled out and bulging with charts, one distant staff member walking away at the far end under a single window. Scale and distance stand for weeks of manual chart review, a corridor rather than a pile.

**Pick: 1.** It carries the brief's figures as countable objects (nine charts, six periwinkle edge labels) and the weight of manual review in one leaning subject, and the raking last light adds the clock without a number. Concept 2 is subtler but its meaning rides on a pun a stranger may miss at feed size, and concept 3 has no countable unit. Frame 1 is a studio object shot and frame 3 a plan relief, so a lit interior at standing eye height differs from both neighbours.

Type: a column on the left over the shaded violet wall, the headline in three lines high, the dek under it, because the tower leans away to the right and leaves that half quiet.
