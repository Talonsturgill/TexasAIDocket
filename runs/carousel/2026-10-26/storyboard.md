# Storyboard, 2026-10-26
# "The drones came first."

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

Zipline opened drone delivery in the Austin area on October 9th, taking First Flight orders in Round Rock, DroneXL
reports (c32), and Zipline's own announcement says First Flight starts in Round Rock (c28). The City of Round Rock's own
page places the facility near North Kenney Fort and Joe DiMaggio Boulevards, in its extraterritorial jurisdiction (c2),
and says drone delivery hubs "are not specifically addressed under the City's current zoning ordinances" (c1). DroneXL
reports the hub sits outside the city's limits (c55). The city says the FAA regulates airspace and flight paths (c4),
drones may fly over residential areas on approved paths (c6), and it can't regulate those paths (c7). Inside city limits
it may decide where drone infrastructure sits (c8) and keep a hub from sitting next to homes (c11). It is working with
Zipline toward a hub behind the Renaissance Square shopping center on northbound I-35 (c9) and says that site will help it
develop regulations for future facilities inside city limits (c10). Service reaches eligible customers within three miles
of the ETJ facility, the city says, citing the company (c3). Operational feedback goes to Zipline at community@zipline.com
and safety concerns to the FAA (c13, c14).

The machine. Zipline says each aircraft reserves its own route in an FAA approved traffic management system before
launch (c19), carries an onboard detect and avoid system (c18), runs flight computers that detect and respond to issues
on their own (c20), and deploys a parachute when it can't resolve an issue (c23). Trained staff monitor flights from a
remote operations center (c24). Every one of these is the company's own claim.

The other order. Rowlett's City Council minutes of May 20th, 2025 (c46) record a motion adding drone hub and kiosk uses to
its zoning code, passed 6-0 (c47), approved as ORD-014-25 (c48), with Zipline as the applicant (c51). A councilmember said
"the SUP for the hub is the control" (c54). DroneXL reports Rowlett wrote hubs into its code before Zipline opened there
(c43), a medium claim the deck attributes.

The fact check moved six things, and every surface obeys them.
1. **The hub's county is not stated by any source.** The deck names Round Rock and Williamson County only where a source
   does (c31, c41), and never places the hub in a county.
2. **The noise complaint routing is DroneXL's paraphrase.** The city routes "operational or company-specific feedback"
   to Zipline (c13). The copy says that and nothing about noise.
3. **The 200 foot buffer is not Rowlett's adopted rule** (c53 is a recommendation) and appears nowhere.
4. **"Before Zipline opened there" is DroneXL's**, attributed on frame 7 and in the caption.
5. **The FAA draft's Austin figures are DroneXL's relay** (c36, medium) and are not drawn.
6. **The lifetime mileage figures disagree** (c25, c30) and none is printed.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Director B's order of operations is the spine. Both cities face the same aircraft and the same FAA, so the one real
difference is the order: in Rowlett the rule was on the dais first, in Round Rock the aircraft was over the yards first,
and the city's rule waits on a trial hub. The deck shows that order with physical things: a hub on prairie clay, a city
limit sign, a ring drawn three miles out, a dais with six lit microphones, a staked empty pad.

Grafted from Director A: the cover at the aircraft's own height over the town's lamps, and the ring of the company's
three mile reach as a measured drawing. Grafted from Director C: the aircraft docked with leaders to what the company
says it decides, and the operations room where people watch, the AI in use beside the zoning question. Refused from A:
the 300 foot elevation, because carousel no. 33 on September 24th drew "It holds at 330 feet" as its frame 2, and A's
thirty towers for thirty Chargers, because c36 is a medium relay. Refused from B: the pod over a fence on the cover, which
is no. 33's cover, and a Rowlett page set in perspective type. Refused from C: the parachute canopy, which would show an
incident that did not happen in Round Rock.

## The world, and the laws that hold it

**LASTLIGHT, A STAGED WORLD, WITH THE SKY BEHIND THE SUBJECT.** The chassis is `assets/js/deck/2026-10-26-tether.js` and
every frame loads it. It declares `sky: lastLight` with the key at azimuth -55, elevation 8: the last light low in the
south west. On a camera at +z looking -z the key comes from the front left, every lit edge is on the left, and every cast
runs long to the right and away. +x is east and +z is south. Frames that look north east have the key behind the camera's
left and light their subject warm against a dark sky. Frames that look south west rim their subject against the seam.

The argument for this world. The subject is a thing in the air, and a near black sky is the only field where a pale
airframe, its nav lights and a thin lit tether hold contrast. The pools fall only where something meets the ground, the
pad, the yard, the dais, the empty site, and those are the places a city has any say.

1. **Start dark.** Near black sky, a warm seam low on the key's side, pools only where something stands.
2. **Type is light, on the dark field**, and every wash only darkens.
3. **Dusk gold #E0956A is the only accent** and means one thing: what comes down to the ground. It is the pod's band on
   frames 1, 2 and 8 and the cradle's landing pad ring on 5. It never sits against the seam.
4. Every standing thing gets `TXT.contact`, every exterior frame `TXT.weather`, every frame `TXDECK.finish`.
5. **No logo, no wordmark, no maker's likeness.** The aircraft, pod and dock tower are drawn to illustrate and to no
   maker's design, and every frame that shows them says so in its dek or a label.
