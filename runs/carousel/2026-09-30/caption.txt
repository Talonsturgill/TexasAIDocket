Who reads a long written STAAR answer first?

Unless the test is in Spanish, a machine does. The Texas Education Agency has done it that way since December 2023. In the agency's chart the machine's score stands on about 75% of them. The agency said scoring them all by hand would have cost $15 million to $20 million more a year.

The machine learned from about 3,000 human scored answers per question. Low confidence scores, blank answers and a random sample go to people, and the person's score counts.

The agency said about 27,200 students got more credit on the reading test after content experts reread short typed answers. It said the engine scored none of them.

A parent can take a concern to the district. Its testing staff can request a human rescore for a fee. If the score changes, the fee is waived.
