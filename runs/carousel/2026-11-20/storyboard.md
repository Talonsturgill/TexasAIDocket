# Carousel, November 20th, 2026: the ridge was the job, a drone that checks its fix against its own motion

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

East Texas A&M researchers in Commerce (c14), led by professor Jinoh Kim (c16), tested whether AI can spot false location in a drone's flight records (c3). GPS spoofing sends false signals that trick a receiver into reporting the wrong location (c1), which could send a civilian drone into restricted airspace or set it down somewhere unsafe (c2). The release's own example is a search drone sent into the next valley (c18). The method reads only the drone's motion sensors, how fast, which way and over how much time (c4, c15). The team used 96 real flights (c5), 42 normal and 54 spoofed (c6), held back 22 for testing (c8) and compared eight models (c9). Given 10 consecutive readings, Chronos scored 97.5% on a balanced measure (c11). The study used recorded flights and the in flight check is next (c12). The fact check carried 26 claims and dropped the NSF API amounts, any Chronos maker and the county.

Docket item tx-2026-0253, Hunt County, research-and-science.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Base: THE RIDGE WAS THE JOB (the ridge lens). A Texan feels a search drone turning toward the wrong valley more than the word spoofing. Grafted from THE TWO TRACKS: the shadow rule, so the reported position, frosted glass, never casts a shadow and the measured motion, amber, always does. Grafted from THE FLIGHT RECORDS: the glass read window over exactly ten readings on frame 8, and the sealed held out records returning on frame 9. Declined: a tape metaphor for digital logs on every frame, because a literal reading would misdescribe the data.
