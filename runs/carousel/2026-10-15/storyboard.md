# Storyboard, 2026-10-15
# "The transcript an AI made reached the Supreme Court of Texas"

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

In a Montgomery County wrongful termination suit (c7), the employee's side produced the written transcript
of a deposition with an AI transcription platform (c6, c16), beside a deposition video recording its lawyer
calls certified (c13). The company did not object at the deposition and asked the trial court to strike the
transcript several weeks later (c10). The trial court struck it in April 2025 because no certified shorthand
reporter prepared it (c8), and the Texas Court of Appeals for the Ninth District denied relief, Courthouse
News reports (c9). The mandamus was filed May 29th, 2025 (c2). The Supreme Court of Texas heard it on October
6th, 2026 (c1, c23, c6), eight of nine justices sitting (c22), twenty minutes a side, with five of the real
party's twenty given to the Texas Deposition Reporters Association as amicus (c25). The court's own rules
already let a written transcription of a non-stenographic recording be used to the same extent as a
stenographic deposition, and let a court require a certified reporter's transcript for good cause (c26 to
c29). At argument Justice Bland asked who stands behind the transcribed product (c12), Justice Young named
escape hatches (c20), Justice Hawkins asked why set aside an efficient tool (c18, c19), Justice Busby asked
about proportionality (c17), and the Chief Justice asked "Why are we here?" (c11). Courthouse News read the
bench as inclined to allow it (c21). **The court has not ruled.** The record carries it as tx-2026-0127.

**WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED.**
- **Bryant is from Conroe**, per the court's schedule (c24), not Houston as Courthouse News has it.
- **"Appeal" is Courthouse News's word.** The court lists a petition for writ of mandamus (c23). No surface
  calls the Supreme Court proceeding an appeal.
- **No source names the AI platform**, so nothing on the deck names one, and no screen shows a brand.
- **No source says the transcript lacked a signature or a certificate.** The transcript is drawn to
  illustrate, its certificate page drawn as the place a certified reporter's attestation would go, and no
  surface says the real page was blank.
- **The argument was at 9:00 a.m.** (c1). The court frames are windowless rooms. No exterior shows the
  argument, and no surface sets it in the evening.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Three directors pitched THE EMPTY CHAIR (the reporter's empty station carried through the deck), TWO RECORDS
OF ONE HOUR (the page and the camera side by side, the page moving through every room the case passed
through) and THE LONG ROAD UP (a granite stair with one riser per month from the strike to the argument).

- **From THE LONG ROAD UP, the spine and the bookends.** The stair is a count a reader can see: 18 risers,
  one per calendar month from April 2025 to October 2026, computed. The cover sits on its bottom tread, frame
  5 reads it in elevation with the filing and the argument on it, and the close looks back down from the top
  landing with the doors still shut. The hero is its transcript, bound, whose last leaf is a certificate page.
- **From TWO RECORDS, the deposition.** The lit corner room seen from the Montgomery County pines at last light
  is frame 2, and the camera beside the page at one scale is frame 3.
- **From THE EMPTY CHAIR, the bench.** Nine seats, eight justices, one chair empty, staged as one subject.
- **Refused:** a stenotype standing outdoors on a walk (surreal, and no stenotype was at this deposition, so
  drawing one in the room implies a reporter attended), the field of forty limestone blocks (a conceptual
  sculpture cut off from the case), the fan folded steno paper (2D, and the deck already renders eight
  frames), the time rails built into counsel tables (contrived courtroom furniture). The lectern's rail of
  twenty brass segments is kept, because a lectern timer is a real thing an advocate watches.
- **The accent.** Two directors chose bluebonnet #4E5FA8 for different meanings. It is used here for ONE:
  certification, the blue of certifying ink. It marks the clause of Rule 203.6(a) naming a certified court
  reporter (6), the deposition reporters' association's five minutes on the lectern rail (8), and the
  certificate's signature rule on the open last leaf (8, 9). It is printed unlit at its hex on pale paper and
  on the brass, where a dark blue still reads, never on the dark field.

## The world, and the laws that hold it

and every frame loads it. It declares `sky: lastLight` with the key at azimuth -58, elevation 7: the sun just
