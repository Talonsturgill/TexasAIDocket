# Carousel no. 68, November 4th, 2026: the watched stroke

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

Permian Resources filed its third quarter 2026 results with the SEC on November 4th (c4). It says
its Production Operations team has deployed an AI-powered artificial lift monitoring system to increase runtime and
optimize well performance (c1), and that it is integrating its proprietary subsurface data with large language models
for development strategy, wellbore placement and drilling parameters in real time (c2). The release prints no measured
result for either tool. It credits record runtimes to power and compression projects (c18), never to the monitor. It
reports 457,528 barrels of oil equivalent a day, up 22% (c6), of which 208,029 barrels are crude (c7), and its fastest
well ever at over 2,700 feet a day (c8). Its 2025 annual report puts about 67% of its net leasehold in Texas and 33% in
New Mexico (c15) and names Reeves County, Texas for drilling, completion and water connection work (c17). Its own
cautionary note lists artificial intelligence and machine learning among the factors in its outlook (c5). On May 21st
the Texas Tribune reported the Railroad Commission said it was not aware of any use of AI by oil and gas companies
(c27), 167 days before this filing (computed).

The fact check found no county but Reeves in a primary document, no well count, no runtime figure, no vendor and no
model, so nothing of those is drawn as fact. ExxonMobil's automation plan is verified (c19 to c25) and stays out of the
frames: its post never uses the word AI, and this deck is about the one company that did.

Docket item tx-2026-0240, admitted this run on the SEC filing.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Base: THE WATCHED STROKE (the watcher lens). One kit pump jack recurs as the subject and its crank advances 40 degrees a
frame, so the deck is one stroke left open at the close, which is runtime drawn rather than stated. The AI is never drawn
as a gadget, because the record names no device. Grafted from SCRIBED, NOT CUT: the acreage split as two cast iron
masses sawn from one block at one scale, a material relief rather than an aerial of counties, which was the base
treatment's hardest and least certain frame. Grafted from TWO LAMPS: the camera walks the reader onto the pad and back
out to the gate. Dropped: the ExxonMobil rig floor frames, the county aerial, and every drawn controller box.

## The world, and the laws that hold it

`lastLight`, staged. The key is azimuth -70, elevation 6: +x east, +z south, so the sun is just down in the west
south west. A camera looking west has the seam behind its subject, every lit edge a rim and every cast running toward the
camera. One subject sits in a pool on the caliche pad and the basin behind it goes dark. Type is light on the dark sky.
A wash only darkens.

## The continuity devices

CONTINUITY: MOTIF_EVOLUTION, CAMERA_MOVE, VALUE_ARC

   frame on every frame that shows it (40, 80, 120, 200, 320, skipping the three reliefs), so the horsehead rises and falls across the deck and the
   revolution is still open at the close.
2. **Camera move.** Low at the jack (1), straight down on the page (2), up the polished rod (3), back to a cutaway (4), straight
   down on the rods (5), across the pad to the tanks (6), low at the pipe racks (7), straight down on a tank roof (8), and out to the gate (9).
3. **Value arc, one dusk.** The seam is widest behind the cover, narrows as the camera turns from it through the middle
   of the deck, and is a thin line beyond the gate on the close.
