# Story brief, August 26th, 2026

## The decision

`tx-2026-0095`. On August 12th, 2026 the University of Texas System Board of Regents amended the
system's capital improvement program to include Phase 1 of the UT Dell Medical Center at UT Austin,
approved the total project cost for that phase and authorized the money from Revenue Financing
System bond proceeds. The board's own background states the debt is expected to be repaid from
clinical revenues.

## Why this story and not the others

Eight items were admitted today. Four were plausible decks.

- **The Senate water hearing on September 1st** (`tx-2026-0096`) is the most actionable thing in
  the record, with a room a reader can walk into in six days. It was set aside because data center
  water shipped four days ago as PUCT Project 58482, and a hearing notice is the filing shape the
  routine warns against reaching for because it is easy to source.
- **The Rice self-driving laboratory** (`tx-2026-0098`) is a strong application story and it is
  the second choice. It loses on place, because a cloud laboratory has no ground a Texan stands on.
- **Houston Methodist's imaging AI** (`tx-2026-0101`) has the best human material in the batch and
  the thinnest document behind it. One hospital blog post is not a deck about a decision.
- **This one** carries a billion dollars, a named county, a partner every Texan has heard of, a
  dated next step next month, and a measured absence that no other source has published. It is also
  the clinic beat rather than another filing, which is the correction `APPLICATIONS.md` exists to
  make.

`dedupe_check` on the entities and keywords returns nothing close against the six entries in the
thirty day window. `texan_check` at selection returns places Travis County, body yes, deadline yes,
next step yes.

## The spine, and it is a negative

The regents publish each committee's pages as its own file. The Facilities Planning and
Construction Committee's document runs its own pages 124 to 143 and carries six items, of which the UT Dell
Medical Center is item 3.

**A case-insensitive search of all 20 pages of that document for artificial intelligence, the
standalone word AI, machine learning and algorithm returns zero.**

The same search over the combined 307 page agenda book returns 24 matches on 16 pages. The finance
committee's own 66 page extract returns 7. Among the matches, in the same book and on the same two
days, is a medical branch amendment whose own title reads "to provide AI technology platform" at
"Funds: $9,850,000", and an audit committee plan naming "a focus on the use of artificial
intelligence".

So the board writes the word. It writes it about a contract two orders of magnitude smaller. It
does not write it in the item that carries this hospital.

**And this hospital is the one that gets called AI-native.** UT Austin's own April release describes
a center "where technology, data and AI are embedded to support clinicians", and the donors funding
it describe "one campus designed for the AI era". The board item instead says the campus
incorporates "digital technology, robotics, and automation" and that care "will be supported by a
digitally enabled infrastructure".

## The second spine, which arrived while reading page 132

The item's own cost table opens **"Building Cost $0"**. The largest line is "Site Development
650,000,000". The rest is fixed equipment, furniture, institutionally managed work, design
services, project management and an eighty five million dollar contingency.

A billion dollars, and none of it is a building. Phase 1 is called Early Work and Site Preparation
and the milestone list gives "Construction Notice to Proceed September 2026".

Both spines are the same shape. Read the document and the headline changes.

## What this deck may NOT say

**This is a distinction and not a gotcha, and every reviewer will be asked to hold the deck to it.**

Both documents are the university's own. A board resolution is a legal instrument, and a governing
board describing what it is voting bonds against has reasons to write conservatively that have
nothing to do with doubt. The deck may show a reader the two vocabularies and may name the gap. It
may not decide for the reader what the gap means, and it may not claim the board never writes AI,
because the board demonstrably does, elsewhere in the same book.

The counter-image is therefore not an add-on. It is the load-bearing frame.
