# Storyboard, August 26th, 2026 — the UT Dell Medical Center, Phase 1

## Synthesis

**Treatment B, "bare ground under a Balcones morning", taken as the spine**, with grafts from the
other two rooms. Three directors pitched. The document lens read the agenda book as a bound object,
the ground lens read the site, and the two vocabularies lens set the two documents in typographic
register.

The ground lens wins on one idea the other two could not produce. **Built things cast shadows.
Unbuilt things do not.** That law runs through every frame and carries the deck's whole argument in
the drawing, so no frame has to accuse anybody of anything. Its companion law does the same work on
the second axis. **What the board funded is at or below grade. What the institution promised is
above it.** It also wins on place, which is the weakness of the other two, because a bound book and
a pair of quoted strings are the same object in Ohio and 27 acres on the Balcones shelf are not.

It also matches the caption, which matters more than usual today. The caption room's winner opens
"The billion buys no building" and pivots on the cost table before it reaches the vocabulary, so the
deck reaches them in that order too.

### Grafted in, and what each graft fixes

- **From the two vocabularies lens, the measured plate on slide 7. IT DID NOT SURVIVE THE PANEL
  AND IT SHOULD NOT HAVE BEEN TAKEN.** `TX.svgPlate` was to measure the advance width of the exact
  string `"AI and "` and draw a granite plate at that width, with an identical empty span in
  register above it, so the difference between the two documents was a measurement rather than a
  drawing. This paragraph called it the best single image any room produced. The measurement had
  no subject. The two documents share no sentence: the board item's technology language is
  `c12`, "digital technology, robotics, and automation", and the release's is `c24`, "AI and
  advanced computing". Neither list contains the other, so there is no minimal pair to difference
  and no width to measure, and the frame reached three judges printing a board attributed line
  that appears in no claim in the file. Two of the three named it a hard fail.
  Slide 7 is rebuilt as two identically treated seated blocks, one per document, each carrying
  that document's own list whole and verbatim under its own date. The granite plate and the
  register are gone with the claim they were measuring, and the composition now refuses to rank
  the two rather than measuring a gap between them. See the frame's own `replanned` block.
  The lesson is not about slide 7. A synthesis that names a technique the best thing in the room
  before checking that the record can carry it has picked the image first and gone looking for the
  claim afterwards, and that is the order this project exists to reverse.
- **From the document lens, the prohibition that saves the deck from its own best temptation.**
  **NO FRAME MAY DRAW SIXTEEN LOCATED MARKS.** The record holds the COUNT of pages carrying the
  word and does not hold their folios, and a run that places them is inventing data no reader can
  check. This kills the ground lens's slide 6 as pitched, which staked 16 lath at true page
  positions. Slide 6 is rebuilt to draw the five page span alone, unstaked and unbroken, with the
  count stated in type. The absence stays the brightest area in the frame, so the focal law is
  still satisfied by the argument rather than in spite of it.
- **From the document lens, the anti-gotcha lint list**, committed as an acceptance item on every
  frame. No slide anywhere may use hide, hidden, omit, avoid, quietly, fail to, refuse, silent,
  buried, scrub, conceal or dodge.
- **From the two vocabularies lens, one display string per frame** above 96px, and figures always
  in JetBrains Mono tabular and never in Fraunces. `FIELD_NOTES` 2026-08-12 records Fraunces
  rendering a 3 as a 5 at feed size, and this deck's figures run to nine zeros.

### Corrections applied to the treatment as pitched

- **`TXSDF` is spent on slide 1 and `TX3D` is not used at all.** The ground lens asked for both. A
  benched excavation is the one subject in this story that is a subtraction from a solid, so it
  earns the raymarcher. The projected mass on slide 3 does not need a software 3D renderer to say
  it casts no shadow, and the library's own record is that this renderer puts its subject in the
  bottom fifteen percent twice before anybody works out why. Drawn plates with no contact do the
  same job and cannot land in the wrong place.
- **The palette drops `locate_white`**, because slide 8 as pitched is gone. **`granite` is nearly
  spent too.** It marked the two words on slide 7 and that frame is rebuilt, so it survives only as
  the two identical ticks under the two attributions, one per document, marking neither out.
- **The cost history is not on one axis with the phase figure**, which both the ground lens and the
  two vocabularies lens independently insisted on. A phase authorization and a project total are
  different quantities and one axis would be a lie told with true numbers.
- **No frame draws a site boundary.** The record gives an acreage and a campus and no shape.

## Palette, as declared tokens

Source: the material world of a north Austin construction site on the Edwards limestone shelf at
eight in the morning in August. Weathered pale rock, crushed limestone base course, dark prairie
ground, live oak canopy at the end of summer, the morning sky over the escarpment, and the state's
own Capitol granite.

`limestone #E5DECC`, `cut_face #F4F0E4`, `roadbase #C3B291`, `blackland #211E1A`,
`liveoak #4D5842`, `austin_sky #C1D1D7`, `granite #9A3B2A`

**`granite` marks a word and never a quantity**, and it is the state's public red rather than the
university's. `flag_red` **stays unspent**, because no claim in this story carries a comment window
a reader can act on.

**The palette is craft and never evidence.** No frame names a soil, a rock or a geologic formation.
The record does not state the geology of this site, and a deck that captions one is publishing a
fact it did not fetch.

## The value arc, and the one inversion

    1  bright field, deepest void     the widest range in the deck, opened at once
    2  mid-bright, long dark masses   the range narrows, the dark runs horizontal
    3  bright, low contrast           the airiest frame, deliberately weightless
    4  pale with one dark band low    the range reopens at the bottom only
    5  bright, even, textured         the flattest range, a quiet frame before the turn
    6  brightest                      THE TURN. The lightest area in the deck is an absence
    7  mid, high local contrast       two seated blocks, same light, neither one ranked
    8  DARKEST                        THE INVERSION, the only frame not standing on limestone
    9  light above, dark mass below   the range reopens, closing where slide 1 started

**The turn is at 6 and the inversion is at 8, deliberately not the same frame.** The turn rescues
the middle where attention sags. The inversion lands late, so the image a reader stores survives
the swipe to the end.
