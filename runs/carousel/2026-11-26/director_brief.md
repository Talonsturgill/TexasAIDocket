# Director brief, November 26th, 2026, carousel no. 88 (BESPOKE + LOOK V2)

## The story (docket tx-2026-0263, claims in out/2026-11-26/claims.json, c1 to c19, the ONLY facts you may use)
Texas Tech's Institute for Materials, Manufacturing & Sustainment (IMMS) in Lubbock worked with Fort Worth based AVX
Defense Technologies under the Army's FleetSpace program, funded by the Army Aviation and Missile Command, to build the
FleetSpace User Interface, FSUI (c1). Texas Tech calls FSUI a 3D interactive, AI enabled decision tool for Army aviation
operation, maintenance and sustainment planning (c2). The team also built data fusion and flight operations algorithms and
a predictive maintenance model (c3) that use neural networks (c4). IN TESTING (always say so) the networks predicted
unplanned and 480 hour phase maintenance actions (c5), restored 37 percent of missing and corrupted flight data, and cut data
volume by up to 30 percent and data files by up to 73 percent (c6). A second tool, the Propulsion System Validator, PSV (c7),
tracks the remaining useful life of aircraft engines on its own and recommends maintenance actions tied to the aviation
technical manual (c8). Texas Tech says both have been provided to the Army (c9), with FleetSpace demonstrated at the Army's
annual Experimental Demonstration Gateway Event and PSV at GE Aerospace (c10). More than 18 students worked on it (c11) and
completed three capstone projects (c18). IMMS and AVX have partnered since 2019 (c12). Postdoc Henry Johnston says the models
let maintenance teams find weak components, schedule repairs ahead of failure and reduce downtime (c13), and that this will
increase fleet availability (c14, a forecast). Maintainers assess aircraft with computers and hands on inspections (c15).
Helicopter systems hold complex parts that need regular inspection (c16, background only, names no airframe). Co-PI Tommy Dang
says both tools give soldiers capability to manage their fleets (c17). Texas Tech frames it as a step toward cutting the
billions agencies spend maintaining aircraft (c19, no figure, no savings claimed).

THE TURN, if you want one: an aircraft's record is full of holes. In testing, 37 of every 100 missing or corrupted pieces of
flight data came back, and the files shrank. The AI's job is the paperwork of a machine, read early enough to fix a part
before it fails. People still inspect and decide (c13, c15).
