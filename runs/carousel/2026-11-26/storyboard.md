# Carousel, November 26th, 2026: before it fails

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

Texas Tech's Institute for Materials, Manufacturing & Sustainment in Lubbock worked with Fort Worth based AVX Defense Technologies under the Army's FleetSpace program, funded by the Army Aviation and Missile Command, to build the FleetSpace User Interface (c1), an AI enabled decision tool for Army aviation operation, maintenance and sustainment planning (c2), with data fusion algorithms and a predictive maintenance model (c3) on neural networks (c4). In testing, the networks predicted unplanned and 480 hour phase maintenance actions (c5), restored 37 percent of missing and corrupted flight data and cut data volume by up to 30 percent and files by up to 73 percent (c6). A second tool, the Propulsion System Validator (c7), tracks the remaining useful life of aircraft engines on its own and recommends maintenance tied to the technical manual (c8). Texas Tech says both have been provided to the Army (c9), with demonstrations at the Army's Experimental Demonstration Gateway Event and at GE Aerospace (c10). More than 18 students (c11) finished three capstones (c18). IMMS and AVX have partnered since 2019 (c12). Henry Johnston says the models let teams find weak components and schedule repairs ahead of failure (c13). Maintainers still inspect by hand (c15). The fact check carried 19 claims and rejected any service result, any aircraft type or unit, any adoption or fielding, any cost, any accuracy figure and any savings figure.

Docket item tx-2026-0263, Lubbock and Tarrant counties, defense-and-federal.
