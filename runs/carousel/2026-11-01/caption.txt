Why does an AI lab melt metal?

Several dozen elements make roughly a trillion possible mixes. That sounds like a job for software. Raymundo Arróyave of Texas A&M says no computational tool anywhere can predict how an alloy will behave.

A $2 million WoodNext Foundation grant will help build the Aggie AI Foundry. Computer controlled arms on an assembly line are to mix, melt and test new alloys. The university says existing self driving labs can't handle alloys at up to 3,000 degrees Celsius. Months of work cut to a week or less is the goal, not a result.

A separate NSF award has obligated $4.1 million of an estimated $24.9 million. It funds the open access Autonomous Robotic Metallurgist at Texas A&M.

The award record names researchers at non-R1 and EPSCoR institutions among its intended users. Who outside Texas A&M makes the first alloy there?
