Roku's founder is paying for a foundry.

The WoodNext Foundation of Anthony and Susan Wood is giving $2 million to Texas A&M's materials science department. The grant will create the Aggie AI Foundry.

Its computer controlled arms are meant to mix, melt and test new alloys. Raymundo Arróyave helped conceive it. He says no computational tool anywhere can predict how an alloy will behave. So the metal gets made to find out.

The university's goal is "from months to a week or less." It is a target and not a result.

NSF funds a second robotic alloy platform at the Texas A&M Engineering Experiment Station. It is open access, non-R1 and EPSCoR institutions included.

The slides follow one alloy from powder to a broken bar. Drawn to illustrate.

Neither announcement gives an opening date. When does the first alloy get melted?
