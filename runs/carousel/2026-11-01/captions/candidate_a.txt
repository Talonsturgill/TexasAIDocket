Why does an AI lab need a furnace?

Several dozen elements make roughly a trillion possible mixes. That sounds like a job for software. Raymundo Arróyave of Texas A&M says no computational tool anywhere in the world can predict how an alloy is going to behave.

So the metal still gets made. A $2 million WoodNext Foundation grant will help build the Aggie AI Foundry. Computer controlled arms on an assembly line will mix, melt and test new alloys. The university says existing self driving labs can't handle alloys at up to 3,000 degrees Celsius. Months of work cut to a week or less is the goal, not a result.

The NSF has obligated $4.1 million of an estimated $24.9 million for an open access robotic metallurgist at Texas A&M.
