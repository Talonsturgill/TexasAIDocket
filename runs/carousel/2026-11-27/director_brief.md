# Director brief, November 27th, 2026, carousel no. 89 (BESPOKE + LOOK V2)

## The story (docket tx-2026-0255, statewide, state-policy. Claims will be in out/2026-11-27/claims.json and are the ONLY facts you may use. Until then, use only what is below, which is all read from the primary documents.)

On November 18th Texas Comptroller Don Huffines announced proposed amendments to Rule 3.308 (34 TAC §3.308). They appeared in the
Texas Register on November 20th, 2026 (filed with the Secretary of State November 9th). Texas has taxed "data processing services"
since the Legislature taxed them in 1987. The proposal narrows that: a data processing service would be one where the provider
processes data the CUSTOMER supplies and returns it organized (a report, chart, form, database). Storage or manipulation of data
alone would not count, and neither would a customer using a provider's software or computing to process its own data. A new term,
"hosted technology service", includes cloud computing, artificial intelligence and quantum computing, and the rule says those are
not data processing and not taxable. Internet hosting too. It is a PROPOSAL, not adopted.

The comptroller's OWN fiscal note in the Register: losses to General Revenue Fund 0001 of $1,947,310,000 in fiscal year 2027,
$2,271,960,000 (2028), $2,655,080,000 (2029), $3,108,360,000 (2030), $3,646,140,000 (2031) and $4,286,040,000 (2032). Losses to
cities, counties and transit authorities of $507,450,000 (2027) rising to $1,116,890,000 (2032). The SAME note says the rule "will
have insignificant effect on this state's economy", and that one further effect "cannot be estimated". Comments go to Robin
Robinson, Associate Deputy Comptroller for Tax, by mail or email, and must arrive within 30 days of publication. Earliest
possible adoption: December 20th, 2026. Huffines is outgoing (the Tribune reports his successor objected), which the fact check
may or may not confirm.

THE TURN, if you want one: a tax nobody sees, on services nobody touches, and the state's own estimate of what it stops
collecting grows every year of the six it counts. A reader can still write in before December 20th.
