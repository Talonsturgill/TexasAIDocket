# Slide 07 concepts, THE SAME NOTE

1. THE LONG SHADOW (macro, txthree). A worm's eye macro on the fiscal note page lying at the foot of a stack of exactly 4,286 sheets, 428.6 mm tall and out of focus, with a coral key 8 degrees high behind it so the stack's violet shadow runs straight down the page and swallows half of the one dusk gold line. Only this beat has a note calling its own effect insignificant while that same note's largest number literally throws its shade over the sentence.

2. THE LOUPE (close, txthree studio). A jeweller's loupe stands on the highlighted line, magnifying a blur of greeked type, and in the loupe's curved glass the tall kraft stack is reflected small and bent. It says the finding needs a lens to see while the estimate is everywhere around it, but the reflection is tiny at feed size and the loupe reads as "scrutiny" on any story.

3. THE SCALE PAN (medium, studio). A brass letter scale with the single page on one pan and the 2032 stack crushing the other, the beam pinned against its stop. A balance is the oldest cliche for "this outweighs that", it reads as editorial sarcasm (the brief's named risk), and a scale is a verdict this frame should not draw.

PICK: 1. The shadow is a physical consequence of the figure (its length follows from 428.6 mm under an 8 degree key), so the picture argues with the headline without a word of comment, and no other sentence in the deck could own a quote lying in the shade of its own number.

Neighbours: frame 6 is a hearing dais with two piles at medium distance, frame 8 a lit card drawer. This frame is the deck's only macro, backlit, shallow depth of field.

Composition with type: the stack stands left of centre and towers into the violet sweep wall, so the right column of the upper half is a quiet out of focus violet field. The headline sits there in four lines, right column, with the dek under it. The lower third is the sharp page and the shadow crossing the gold line, kept clear of type.
