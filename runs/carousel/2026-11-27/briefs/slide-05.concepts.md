# Slide 05 concepts, THE FISCAL NOTE

1. **Six stacks on the conference table at dusk.** Six kraft wrapped stacks of paper stand in fiscal year order along an oak conference table, seen at table height, each holding exactly its year's sheets at one per $1,000,000, the last one rising past a steel rule while low window light rakes through mullions across them and throws long violet shadows over the grain. Only this beat counts six annual estimates that climb, and the paper the note is printed on is the unit, so the staircase of reams is the fiscal note itself.

2. **The archive shelf.** A tall violet steel archive shelving unit seen square on from a step back, six shelves, each holding one year's estimate as a row of reams laid flat, so the shelves fill fuller from top to bottom like a ledger filed by year. It answers "every year it counts" with filing, but a filled shelf reads as storage, not growth, and shelves in a grid read as a chart.

3. **The hand truck.** One hand truck at a mail room door, loaded with the fiscal 2032 stack alone at 4,286 sheets, with five smaller stacks waiting on the floor behind it in a line toward a window, seen from knee height. It makes the estimate feel like freight, but it leads with one year and pushes the climb into the background where the six counts can't be read at feed size.

**Pick: 1.** It is the only one where the six years read as one climb a stranger gets in a second, with whole reams countable by their rose bands and the rule saying "measured", and a table in a room keeps it from being a bar chart in costume. It shares no world with frame 4 (a plan view relief of a page) or frame 6 (a council dais).

**Composition.** The stacks rise left to right, so the upper left over the violet back wall is quiet: the headline sits there, top left, three lines. The dek goes low, across the dark front edge of the table and its shadowed apron, under the stacks' bases, so the stacks own the middle of the frame at full height.
