Popular isn't the same as good.

Researchers at Texas A&M, the University of Texas at Austin and Purdue kept training four open models on short, popular Twitter/X posts.

Llama3 8B Instruct took it hardest. Its reasoning score fell from 72.1 to 57.2 as the junk share rose from 0% to 100%. Popularity tracked the damage better than length did.

The models skipped steps. The paper's "No Thinking" label appeared in 84% of failures under junk training.

Instruction data at 4.8 times the junk tokens didn't fully undo the damage. The best repaired models still trailed the baseline by 17.3 points on reasoning.

The deck builds each score as a stair's height.

The junk shares were doses in a training mix, not a reading of the internet. ChatGPT and Gemini aren't open source and weren't tested. Would a closed model skip the same steps?
