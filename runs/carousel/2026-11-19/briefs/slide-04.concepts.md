# Slide 4 concepts, TODAY, BY HAND

1. **The rack of the kept.** A violet microcentrifuge rack of FIG.traps_floor milky tubes, raked three quarter and filling the frame's middle on a cobalt epoxy bench, every tube dark with its packed catch, a dead end jar and petri stacks behind it, and far down an apricot lab a technician bent over a desk under a cobalt evening window. Only this beat has a hundred identical kept jars of catch waiting for one person, which is "every trap keeps everything" and "sorted by hand" in one look.

2. **The sorting tray at dusk.** A white enamel tray on a lab stool heaped with an undifferentiated tangle of catch, forceps resting on its rim and one empty vial waiting beside it, seen at a low raking angle in honey lamplight. Only this beat is about the heap itself, the work of separating it, before any machine exists.

3. **The shelf of jars.** A floor to ceiling steel shelving wall of one door jar traps, each a sealed funnel jar with its dark sediment, receding down a corridor in one point perspective while a lone cart waits at the far end. Only this beat has the weight of every trap's whole catch stored and waiting.

**Pick: 1.** It is the only one where the figure is the picture (one tube per monitored trap, counted in code) and the delay reads as a person far away from a lot of kept catch. Concept 2 risks a fly beside forceps (a size the claims never give) and concept 3 turns the count into an uncountable wall. It also differs from both neighbours: frame 3 is a glass Y on a studio sweep, low and close, frame 5 an outdoor trap under sky. This is a lit interior, medium, raked from above.

**Type.** The headline sits bottom left over the near bench front, which the camera leaves in the bench's own cobalt shade, two lines, about 100 px. The dek follows it beneath at 28 px.
