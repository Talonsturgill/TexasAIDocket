# Slide 6 concepts, WHO PICKS THE SKY

1. **The planner's dividers on a carved map (TXBENCH plan relief).** South Texas carved in caliche one step above a dust Mexico and a navy Gulf, the Rio Grande cut as a slate channel, and a pair of brass map dividers standing on it with one point in Kerrville and the other in Edinburg, so the dividers' V only shows in its long violet cast across the land. Only this beat has a lab in one town planning a release from another, and dividers are the drawing tool of a plan, set to the measured span between the two places the claims name.

2. **A cobalt dawn over the Valley from the ground (txthree, v2wonder sky, worm's eye).** An empty sky filling four fifths of the frame above a low line of Valley brush, the honey sun low at left, the sky the thing being decided, with the type set inside it. Only "who picks the sky" asks the reader to look up at the place a model is choosing, though it risks a sky with nothing in it.

3. **The model as a cut stone forecast table (txthree studio, raked orthographic).** A stone plinth carrying a small carved relief of the Valley under a glass dome, studio sweep in cobalt and peach, read as an instrument that already exists and is already deciding. Only a second machine already at work could be drawn as a finished instrument on a stand, though a dome on a plinth risks reading as a museum piece rather than a plan.

**Pick: 1.** It is the one a stranger could not move to another headline, two named places joined by a planning instrument at their true span, and it changes medium from frame 5's ground level brush scene and frame 7's raked field of bales.

Type: the navy Gulf on the right is the one large dark quiet field in the picture, so the headline sits in a column over the open water below the upper coast, three lines, with the dek under it, and the coast is cropped so the water reaches from the coast to the right margin.
