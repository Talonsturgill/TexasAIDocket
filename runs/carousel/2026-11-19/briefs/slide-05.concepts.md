# Slide 05, THE ALLIES, three concepts

1. **The open mouth in the brush.** A weathered white one way bucket trap hangs by its wire bail from a twisting mesquite limb at eye height, shot wide and looking up into a cobalt golden hour sky, while a long loose ribbon of eighty bronze flies, one per million released a week, pours in from the left sky and bends down toward the trap's single funnel mouth before sweeping past it and out the right edge. Only this beat has the released allies flying straight at the one door that can't tell them from the enemy, so the friendly fire is the picture itself.

2. **From the release chute.** An aerial camera looking down out of the open belly of a release aircraft, eighty flies tumbling out and falling away toward brush country far below, where small white dots of bucket traps wait among the mesquite. The release is shown as a gift falling onto a field of traps that will swallow some of it, which only the friendly fire beat needs.

3. **One funnel, worm's eye.** A macro from just under the lid of the trap looking up past the dark funnel's lip to the sky, where the flies arrive as backlit specks converging on the hole. The single door seen from below makes the indiscriminate opening the subject.

**Pick: 1.** It carries the measured stream (FIG.flies_week_millions) at true fly scale with honest perspective, it names the friendly fire in one glance (the program's flies heading for the program's trap's one mouth), and it differs from frame 4 (an apricot interior) and frame 6 (a cool plan relief) in world, camera and medium. Concept 2 is an aerial that sits too close to frame 6's map view, and concept 3's macro fly against a bright sky repeats no. 54's black spider failure.

## Composition with the type
The trap hangs left of centre in the middle third, the limb entering from the left edge. The headline runs across the darkest part of the frame, the cobalt zenith at the top, in two lines over the full width. The dek sits in a right column under it in the sky, while the stream enters the left sky below the headline, falls to the trap's mouth and leaves the right edge under the dek, so the flies never cross a word. The bottom third is low caliche brush with long shadows and nothing on it but scrub.
