# Slide 03, THE SORT, three concepts

1. **The glass fork on a pedestal (studio, medium).** A clear borosilicate Y the height of a forearm stands on a tall honed caliche pedestal against a cobalt sweep, seen level with the fork, one fly paused at the fork, one held in the capped keep chamber behind its dusk gold band, three flies and a beetle lifting out of the open release branch into a honey pool of light. Only this beat has one way in and two ways out with the choice already made, keep on the cool side and free on the warm side.

2. **The fork in the caliche road (outdoor, wide).** A ranch road in brush country splits around a single mesquite, one fork ending at a closed gold gate, the other running open to a bright horizon with a loose cloud of flies drifting down it. It draws the decision as a place, but frames 5 and 8 already own brush, roads and fences, and a road says journey more than sort.

3. **Inside the fork (macro, glass interior).** The camera sits inside the glass at the branch point and looks down the two bores, one narrowing to a gold capped dark chamber with a single fly, the other opening to a blown out peach sky with flies leaving as silhouettes. It is the most intimate picture of the choice, but frame 1 is the macro at the door and a second glass macro would repeat its camera and scale.

**Pick: concept 1.** It is the only one where the sentence "keep the wild ones, free the rest" is the whole picture, one fly kept behind gold and the rest leaving, at a scale (tabletop, level camera, studio) neither neighbour uses.

**Composition with the type.** The glass stands right of centre, mouth near the top and foot on the pedestal at the bottom, so the stem leaves a tall quiet cobalt column on the left above the descending keep branch. The headline sits top left in three lines in that column, the dek directly under it in a narrow column ending above the keep chamber, the released flies take the upper right into the honey pool, and the pedestal bleeds off the bottom under the footer.
