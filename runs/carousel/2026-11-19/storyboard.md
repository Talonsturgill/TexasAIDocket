# Carousel, November 19th, 2026: one door or two, the screwworm trap that would sort at the entrance

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

UT Arlington is coordinating a $3.1 million USDA funded project (c1) against New World screwworm, declared eliminated from the United States in 1966 and now back, with more than 120 confirmed animal cases as of November 12th, most of them in Texas (c2). Female flies lay eggs in open wounds and untreated infestations can be fatal (c3). The center of the project is an AI smart trap meant to tell wild flies from sterile ones and females from males (c4), keeping wild females because they produce the larvae (c5, c9) and releasing sterile flies and other insects (c9). Today's traps catch everything and need sorting by hand (c7), and can interfere with the federal sterile fly program (c8). Detections could feed alerts and risk maps to APHIS (c10). A prototype is expected next spring (c6), the project runs through June 2028 (c12), one of 40 projects in roughly $105 million of USDA funding (c11). Producers and veterinarians would report cases with smartphone images (c14). USDA's Kerrville lab already uses AI models to pick where, when and how many sterile flies to release (c16 to c19), models that can't predict every scenario (c20). An APHIS page says the $8.5 million Edinburg facility deploys more than 80 million sterile flies a week over Texas and Mexico (c21, c22), from a male only strain (c23). USDA has monitored more than 100 screwworm specific traps since July 2025 (c24).

The fact check carried all fifteen UT Arlington quotes and nine USDA quotes, each found character for character on a page fetched today. It dropped fly size, appearance, irradiation and trap mechanism, because no primary page states them, and kept the Panama fly supply off the deck so no frame sets two flows against each other.

Docket item tx-2026-0252, statewide, research-and-science.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Base: ONE DOOR OR TWO. The news is one decision moved from a technician's bench to a trap's entrance, and that decision can be drawn as a glass passage that forks, without inventing a mechanism. Every frame shows how many exits the system has. Today it has one door and keeps everything. The planned trap has two. Grafted from THE RANGE: the forty projects are forty round hay bales in a pasture rather than machined billets (no. 74 on October 21st drew machined housings), and the months to June 2028 are fence posts beside a caliche road. Grafted from THE CLOCK: the trap is never drawn as a field device. It is a glass study in a studio, labelled drawn to illustrate the decision and not the device, and the close names next spring as the date to watch. Dropped: an aircraft and a release plume (no claim says aircraft), ear tags for the cases (a count of animals drawn as tags implies cattle), and a plaster maquette (glass carries the same honesty and reads as an idea).
