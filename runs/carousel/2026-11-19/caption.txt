Today's screwworm traps keep everything. The planned one would choose.

Screwworm is back, with more than 120 confirmed animal cases as of November 12th. Most are in Texas. Traps in the field now catch insects indiscriminately. People sort the catch by hand. Those traps can't tell wild flies from the sterile flies a federal program releases.

The trap UT Arlington is designing is meant to do that sorting itself. Its AI would keep wild flies, especially the females that lay the eggs. Sterile flies and other insects would go free. Producers and veterinarians would report cases with phone photos.

The trap isn't built yet. The $3.1 million USDA project runs through June 2028.

UTA associate professor Shouyi Wang expects a prototype by next spring. Will one be ready by then?
