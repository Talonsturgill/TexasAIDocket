# Storyboard, 2026-10-18
# "AI picks where Texas fights this fly"

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

New World screwworm is a fly that lays its eggs in open wounds, and its maggots feed on living tissue (c23, c24).
Texas confirmed its first case in a calf in Zavala County on June 3rd (c26). The calf recovered (c34). The Texas
Animal Health Commission now counts 43 premises in 17 counties with a confirmed detection (c27, c28), and keeps
movement restrictions in an infested zone reaching into parts of 12 named counties (c29).

USDA's research laboratory in Kerrville built predictive models that use AI to forecast where the fly will turn
up (c1, c6, c7). USDA uses them to choose where, when and how many sterile flies to release (c2), and they already
pick areas for surveillance, fly trapping and releases (c4). The dispersal facility in Edinburg deploys more than
80 million sterile flies a week over Texas and Mexico (c18, c19) and can disperse up to 100 million (c20). A
production plant being built in South Texas is projected at 300 million (c21). The technique helped eradicate
screwworm from the United States in 1966 (c25). USDA's drones flew over Texas more than 2,000 times since June and
observed more than 36,000 animals with no case found, KRGV reported, and officials said the drones are not
foolproof (c10, c11, c12). The models can't predict every scenario, especially people moving infested animals
(c5). The next step is the commission's: report a suspected case within 24 hours (c30, c31), and never move an
animal that shows signs (c9). The record carries it as tx-2026-0218.

**WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED.**
- **The drones' "no case" is never Texas's.** The commission counts 43 premises. Frame 7 says "no case among them".
- **Two drone tallies are never subtracted.** The July 31st page's 1,000 and 20,000 (c17) and KRGV's October
  figures cover different periods. Only the October pair is drawn.
- **No credit to Knipling or Bushland.** No fetched page names them except in the laboratory's name.
- **No Hidalgo County.** Edinburg is named as a city only.
- **The 17 counties are never drawn.** No claim names them. The map draws only c29's 12 named counties.
- **The models' output is not published**, so no frame draws a forecast surface or a model screen.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Three directors pitched THE TALLY (every count drawn as countable units at one stated scale), THE WATER POINT (one
South Texas ranch at last light, the stage pool as the model's choice) and ONE TRAILER WIDE (a scale ladder from a
fly on a thorn to a region, landing on a stock trailer no model sees).

- **THE COVER CHANGED REPRESENTATION AT THE AUDITION.** A macro fly on a wire was built and rendered twice, and both readings failed: backlit it read as a black spider, and looked down on it read as a toy over a mid tone ground. The studio rule says change the representation after one unsuccessful repair, so the cover became THE WATER POINT's ranch at last light, which the morning probe had proved.
- **From ONE TRAILER WIDE, the spine.** The camera moves by orders of magnitude, a water point (1), the region (2), the
  release (4), the supply on an apron (5), the herd from the air (7), a trailer at human scale (8, 9). Its Edinburg
  apron, 80 boxes in a 100 slot block with the 300 outline painted at the same pitch, is the evidence frame.
- **From THE TALLY, the measured units.** One box is a million flies a week (5), one animal is a thousand seen
  (7), one windmill is one premises (3), and the year 1966 carved in Hill Country limestone (6). The fly perches on
  the top strand of a barbed wire fence, and the fence returns at the trap (4) and the gate (9).
- **From THE WATER POINT, the world and the close.** The ranch at last light, the windmill as the unit of a
  premises, the map of c29's 12 counties as rims, and the shut gate with the commission's number.
- **Refused.** A wound or maggot image (gore, and no honest model). A 17 county map (no claim names them). A
  forecast heatmap or a model screen (no output is published). Cattle close up (the probe shows the kit cattle read
  as toys under six metres). Longhorns (costume). Cast trays for the fly counts (no. 53 was charged with bar charts
  on graph paper, and boxes on a real apron with a person beside them read as a place). Subtracting c17.
- **The accent.** Bluebonnet #4E5FA8, the one cool hue in a warm brush country palette. It means ONE thing, what
  the response sends into the country: the pins at Kerrville and Edinburg (2), the trap's lid (4), the tape band on
  the release boxes (5) and the drone's lamp (7). It is absent where the models are blind (8, 9).

## The world, and the laws that hold it

**LAST LIGHT OVER THE BRUSH.** The chassis is `assets/js/deck/2026-10-18-brush.js` and every frame loads it. It
declares `sky: lastLight` with the key at azimuth -58, elevation 8: the sun just down to the north west of the
subject, low, so every lit face is a north west face and every cast runs long to the south east. +x is east and +z
south. A material relief reads the same light from the top left of its frame.

The argument for this world: a rancher checks stock at the end of the day, and the brush goes dark around one lit
thing. The staged pool is the argument itself, light on the place the response is sent. lastLight carried no. 51 but
not this hero or this accent, and the last three worlds were nightSodium, floodlit and a darkfield bench.

1. **Start dark.** A near black sky, one warm seam low on the key's side, a pool where the subject stands.
2. **Type is light, on the dark field**, and every wash only darkens.
3. **Bluebonnet is the only accent** and means what the response sends. The kit windmill's blue tail vane is
   repainted weathered galvanized so it never reads as the accent.
4. Every standing thing gets `TXT.contact`, every spatial frame `TXT.weather`.
5. **Cattle stand at twelve metres or more**, or as silhouettes on the seam.
6. **Nothing drawn is a real ranch, a real aircraft or a real facility.** Every frame says drawn to illustrate.
