More than 36,000 animals. No case.

That is what USDA's drones saw in more than 2,000 flights over Texas since June, KRGV reported. It speaks only for those animals. The Texas Animal Health Commission counts 43 premises in 17 counties with a confirmed detection.

The aiming is known. USDA uses AI models to choose where, when and how many sterile flies to release. The models already pick areas for surveillance and trapping.

How well they aim is not. Kim Lohmeyer directs USDA's Kerrville laboratory and says the models can't predict every scenario, especially people moving infested animals. Officials told KRGV the drones are not foolproof.

The deck runs from a fly on a wire to a shut ranch gate.
