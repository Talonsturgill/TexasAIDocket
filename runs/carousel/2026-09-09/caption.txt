A commercial clinician-assisted tool estimated when patients would go home. Houston Methodist's case managers beat it, and by more the nearer discharge got.

The authors say that is when an accurate date counts for most in bed management.

At admission the two were level on error, though the case managers matched the exact date more often. At 24 hours the case managers put 79.5% of estimates within one day of the true date. The tool put 37.9%. Between the two readings the case managers got better. The tool got worse, from 1.59 days to 1.93.

The authors then argue against themselves. Case manager estimates were part of usual care and visible to care teams, so they may be partly self fulfilling. The AI estimates were not enabled automatically.

No baseline patient demographic data were available. Which patients did it read worst?
