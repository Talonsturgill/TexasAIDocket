Two ways to say when a patient goes home. A commercial tool wired into the health record. The hospital's own case managers.

At admission the two sat close together on error, though the case managers matched the exact date more often.

At 24 hours the columns come apart. The case managers put 79.5% of estimates within one day of the true date. The tool put 37.9%. Between the two readings the case managers got better. The tool got worse, from 1.59 days to 1.93.

The authors argue against their own result. Case manager estimates were part of usual care and visible to care teams, so they may be partly self fulfilling. The AI estimates were not enabled automatically.

The comparison carries no patient demographics at all. The authors state that as a limitation. How much of a comparison is missing when it names none of the patients it is about?
