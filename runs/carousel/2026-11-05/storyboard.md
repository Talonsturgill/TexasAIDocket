# Carousel no. 69, November 5th, 2026: the dock door

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

Applied Optoelectronics of Sugar Land filed its third quarter 2026 results with the SEC on November 5th (c1 to c6), and
its quarterly report the same day (c7 to c12, c14 to c20). It makes optical transceivers for internet and AI data
centers (c17). Its chief executive says it is scaling 800G and 1.6T manufacturing for demand for AI-related optical
technologies (c5) and that it recently signed a multi-year agreement with a Tier 1 hyperscale data center customer it
does not name (c6). Its chief financial officer says total equipment capacity is approaching 330,000 units a month (c2),
that equipment for over 500,000 pieces of 800G and 1.6 Tb products a month is expected by the end of 2026 (c3), and
capacity for over 1 million such transceivers a month by the end of 2027 (c4). The same officer says the 2027 US
expansion plan will take longer to complete "due to longer than expected time to obtain necessary electrical power
among other construction related issues" (c1). The quarterly report says the company makes its laser chips only in its
Sugar Land facility (c19), leases about 209,665 square feet on Gillingham Lane in Sugar Land used mainly for
manufacturing (c8, c9), owns about 388,133 square feet in Pearland (c10, c16), and has leased two Chimney Rock Road
buildings in Houston of about 356,186 and 737,621 square feet that are still to be built, with substantial completion
about 16 months after the August 31st leases (c11, c12). Fort Bend County's abatement agreement places the Sugar Land
headquarters in Fort Bend County (c13).

The fact check held the figures to their own words. c2 counts total equipment capacity across every product and site,
while c3 and c4 count 800G and 1.6 Tb pieces, so they are never drawn on one scale and no ratio between them is printed.
It also refused "Sugar Land is the one Texas site", because the report describes a Houston building used for light
manufacturing (c20). Nothing in the record says which building waits on power, which utility serves it or how many
megawatts it needs, so every building in the deck is drawn to illustrate and no frame names a site as the one waiting.

Docket item tx-2026-0241, admitted this run on the SEC filings.

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Base: DOCK DOOR (the waiting building lens). A new manufacturing bay is taped out for machines and lit only by the last
of the day through its open dock door, because the one thing it waits for is power. The figures go onto the same floor
as taped machine pads at one stated scale, one pad for 50,000 pieces a month, so 10 pads by the end of 2026 and 20 by
the end of 2027 (computed). The scale is never printed. Grafted from THE LIT RULE: the larger Chimney Rock lease staked
along one edge, one stake per month to substantial completion, the months already gone flagged. Grafted from all three
pitches: the four Texas properties as equal-area squares at one scale. Grafted from RIM LIGHT: the beat that the laser
chips come from Sugar Land alone, carried by the Gillingham Lane plant drawn at its stated floor area. Dropped: every
millimetre macro (the laser die, the wafer), because the engine's stage, pool and shadow bias are tuned for metres and
none of those frames was buildable with confidence in one day.
