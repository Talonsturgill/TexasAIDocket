# Slide 07, THE PURSE, three concepts

1. **The studio stacks (the planned picture).** Three stacks of machined brass plates on the butter win sweep, thirty, fifteen and ten, the tall one cut by the top edge and the teal rover beside them for scale. Three amounts at one scale become three heights a thumb compares, and the cut stack says more than.

2. **The purse on the pad at Disaster City.** The same three machined brass stacks standing outdoors on a sun bleached concrete pad under the clean win sky, low camera at plate height, the thirty plate stack running out of the top of the frame and the rover parked beside the ten as the yardstick, rubble and live oaks small on the horizon. The prize money stands where it was won, and the rover being taller than Data's stack and shorter than Systems' turns a dollar figure into something a reader measures against a machine.

3. **The weigh bridge.** A freight platform scale in a TEEX yard with the plates loaded as two piles on its deck, the dial replaced by nothing but the stacked mass, seen from a crouch. Weight as weight, but a scale reads one total, so the two wins can't be compared by eye, and a dial edges toward a gauge.

**Chosen, 2.** It is the only one that ties the money to this contest's ground and keeps the one scale comparison, and frame 8 is a cooler studio sweep and frame 6 a tent, so a studio here would repeat a neighbour's world while the open pad does not.

Type. The headline goes high in the sky across the left two thirds, where the short stacks leave clear air, and the dek follows in a column above the rover and the ten plate stack. The thirty plate stack owns the right edge from the floor out of the top.
