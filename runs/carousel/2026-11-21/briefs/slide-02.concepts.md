# Slide 02, 52 ACRES, three concepts

1. THE WHOLE TOWN FROM THE AIR. A raked aerial over one square plot of caliche whose side is computed from 52 acres, set in green Bermuda grass and ringed by live oaks, holding a parking frame with its floors pancaked at one end, three rubble piles, a row of broken shopfronts and a derailed train jackknifed off its track nearest the camera. Only this beat is about the size and the contents of the place itself, so only this beat gets the whole town in one frame with its edges measured.

2. THE DERAILMENT AT GROUND LEVEL. A low worm's eye beside a tank car lying on its side across bent rail, the collapsed frame and rubble piles behind it under a deep blue noon sky, a lath survey stake in the foreground. It says "built to fall down" through one staged wreck, but it shows one set and not a town, so the 52 acres has nothing to measure.

3. A CUTAWAY OF ONE BUILT RUIN. An exploded three quarter section of a three storey concrete frame on a white studio sweep, its slabs lifted apart on guide rods to show how it was built to be dropped. It speaks to "built to fall down" but leaves the town and the acreage out, and a studio object would rhyme with the purse and the rail frames later in the deck.

**Pick: 1.** Only the aerial makes the place physical as a measured whole, where the computed plot side is the picture's own edge and the staged ruins stand inside it. Neighbours are a close low rover on rubble (01) and a lit briefing room (03), so a high outdoor aerial differs from both in camera, scale and world.

## Composition with the type

The plot is turned about 40 degrees so it reads as a lit diamond, its near corner low and right of centre, its left and right corners bleeding off the frame and its far corner high under a thin band of sky and oaks. That leaves a grass triangle at the bottom left. The headline sits there in two lines, bottom left over the Bermuda grass, with the dek under it to the footer. The derailed train lies on the near half of the plot just above and right of the type, so the eye goes headline to wreck to town.
