# Slide 06, THE FIELD, three concepts

1. **The badge board.** One hundred laminated credentials on woven lanyards hang from steel hooks on a teal perforated pegboard in the competitors' tent, ten rows of ten, raked by a butter key so each casts its shadow on the board, and the hook row runs on past the right edge where a hundred and first badge is cut by the frame. A crowd is counted the way an event counts it, one credential per person, with no face anywhere, and the cut badge is the only honest way to draw "more than".

2. **The boot line.** A long tent-floor rack of work boots and trainers, pair after pair receding from a low camera at the tent door, dusty from the course, laces trailing. It says people were here without showing one, but a hundred pairs in perspective can't be counted at feed size and the low ground camera repeats frame 5's low lane.

3. **The helmet shelf.** A wall of a hundred hard hats in cubbies seen square on, each with a reflective strip, the last cubby cut by the edge. Countable and faceless, but helmets read as a construction site rather than a competition, and a square-on wall of cubbies is the flat elevation judges keep calling a chart.

**Pick: 1.** Only a credential is literally one per participant, so the count is the object's own meaning, and the medium (an orthographic interior close on a board) differs from frame 5's low night lane and frame 7's studio stacks.

Composition: the board fills the top 64 percent edge to edge, its ten rows starting under the kicker and ending on its aluminium foot rail; the type sits bottom left in two lines over a deep teal rubber tent floor, the dek three lines under it, clear of the claim ids.
