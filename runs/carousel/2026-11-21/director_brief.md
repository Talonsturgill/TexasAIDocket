# Director brief, November 21st, 2026, carousel no. 84 (BESPOKE + LOOK V2)

## The story (docket tx-2026-0254, claims in out/2026-11-21/claims.json, c1 to c31, the ONLY facts you may use)
The Texas A&M Engineering Extension Service, TEEX, staged the finals of DARPA's Triage Challenge at Disaster City in
College Station from November 5th to November 13th (c1). Disaster City is a 52 acre mock community with full scale
structures that can collapse, rubble piles and a derailed train (c11). Autonomous drones and ground robots worked
alongside first responders to triage simulated mass casualty incidents, spotting lifelike injuries and relaying
critical data to human medics (c2). The challenge seeks autonomous systems, drones and robots, that assess injuries
quickly, accurately and at scale (c8). Teams built algorithms, aerial and ground vehicles meant to work with the
human medics who give hands on care (c9). Systems must locate casualties, identify signs of injury and send the data
to medical teams (c10), and read vital signs from a distance (c18). The five competitions included a simulated tornado
ripping through a community market, smoke filled buildings, nighttime searches in near darkness and search and rescue
under rubble (c14, c4). On the Human Machine Teaming night course, a robot searched for casualties during a controlled
explosion while role players took cover (c27), a course built to simulate an attack on a forward operating base (c28).
Each team was assigned medics for that course (c16). Moulage artists made hyper realistic mock injuries (c15), and
actors playing civilians went through nearly a dozen timed preparation stations (c29). DARPA's command post checked
every mock casualty report against each patient's simulated injuries and vital signs (c13). More than 100 participants
competed for more than $3 million in prizes (c12). The top two reached a championship round for the $1.5 million grand
prize (c23). RoboScout won the Systems competition and $1.5 million. Disruptors won the Data competition and $1 million
(c3). A self funded two person team from Hawaii, DRONE, a father and his daughter, placed second in the first trials
(c24 to c26). DARPA's director said human machine teams outperform every approach (c5), then said that is not enough
(c6), and that some teams did well finding victims while others did well at triage or evacuation (c7). DARPA created the
challenge in 2023 (c21). DARPA's open TRIAGE-X topic, for autonomous systems that recognize chemical toxidromes and
deliver the antidote, is open until November 25th, 2026 (c19, c20). A casualty's life hinges on the speed and accuracy
of triage decisions (c31).
