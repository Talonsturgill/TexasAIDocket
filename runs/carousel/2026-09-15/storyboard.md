# Carousel no. 25 — 2026-09-15 — THE WORLD AND THE PAPER

**Story.** ERCOT put three emerging Large Load risks in front of its own Board of Directors with
a mitigation for each, and the three mitigations sit at three different stages. One rule is in
force, one reached the Board this month, and one is not written and has no number. The same page
says the dynamic models ERCOT uses to study these loads have proven inaccurate.

**The structural law, and it is the whole treatment.** THE WORLD AND THE PAPER NEVER SHARE A
FRAME. Frames 1, 3, 4, 6 and 9 are the physical world at standing or detail eye, with a light, a
horizon and shadows, and they carry no accent and no drawn rule anywhere. Frames 2, 5, 7 and 8
are paper, and every one of them carries the deck's one accent. The reader learns that code by
frame three without being told it, and by frame seven it is the argument: the rule is a drawing,
and ERCOT says the drawing is wrong about the machine.

**Register.** A gas plant on the ERCOT system at 06:20 at the end of night shift, and its yard at
first light. A place in ERCOT a Texan has driven past and this project has never drawn.

**Camera and light.** Standing eye 1.68 m on frames 1, 3, 6 and 9, each with its own horizon
and focal length because each looks at a different thing: f 820 with the horizon at 790 on
frame 1, f 840 with the horizon at 1042 on frame 3 where the camera looks at a wall, f 820
with the horizon at 560 on frame 6 where the cut IS the horizon, f 820 with the horizon at
556 on frame 9. Detail
scale with no vanishing point on frame 4. A copy stand looking down 38 degrees on frames 5 and 7.
No ground plane at all on frames 2 and 8, which are an elevation and a survey sheet. ONE LIGHT,
declared once and never changed: azimuth -68 degrees, elevation 6 degrees, a low raking bar
through clerestory glazing indoors and first light on the pad outdoors. Every shadow in the deck
falls from it. Frames 2 and 8 have no light because a drawing has none.

**Palette.** A turbine deck and its yard, and nothing else.
`deck_epoxy #101A12` oil-darkened green-black epoxy, the ground. `lagging #E4DCC6` aluminium
jacketing over turbine insulation under a low warm sun, the ink. `forging #5C6552` ground rotor
steel in shade. `enamel #2C4433` machinery enamel on pedestals and cabinets. `pad_caliche
#A08A5E` the plant pad. `first_light #C08A5A` the 06:20 sun. `bond #F4F1E2` the drawing sheet.
The grounds and inks sit near earlier decks' near-blacks and near-whites by nature, which is
stated rather than hidden; what carries this deck's distinction is the register and the accent.

**The screen ceiling, measured on 2026-09-15, and it is why the value arc reads low.**
On a dark ground TXINK lays light ink where the source is light, so a frame's median is bounded
by how much ink its screen can physically put down. Measured by printing a white field through
each of this deck's own screen configurations and reading the press, and written to
`screen_ceilings.json` rather than typed: the stipple at cell 5 with 6 dots per cell returns a
mean of 63.0 of 255, which is a ceiling of L* 26.7; the hatch at cells 6 to 8 returns 107.6 to
107.9, a ceiling of L* 45.5; the halftone at cell 6 returns 205.7, a ceiling of L* 82.7. So a
full-bleed
screened world frame on this ground cannot reach the forties whatever is drawn in it, and the
first cut of this arc asked frame 3, a stipple frame, for 40. The numbers in each dossier below
were re-derived from the press rather than from the wish. The paper frames are unaffected because
their sheet is painted after the press, unscreened, which is the same device frame 3's sky now
uses. WHAT THIS DOES NOT EXCUSE is a frame that came out dark because nothing was drawn in it.
Frame 3 was rebuilt for that reason on the same day, and the ceiling is the reason its number is
9 rather than 40, not the reason it was thin.

**Accent `#2F7D57`**, the drawing office checker's green pencil, the mark meaning a line has been
drawn and checked. Measured at 20.9 dE from the nearest hex in the last eight decks, against the
8.9 dE a layout blue would have sat from 2026-09-13's. It appears on frames 2, 5, 7 and 8 and
nowhere else, and never on a frame showing a real machine. It may fill a MARK and it may not fill
a THING: frame 7's dimensioned limit and frame 8's scale bar segments are marks the deck drew to
be read as marks, and a table row, a county, a machine or a plate behind a label are not. The
first cut of this sentence read "never as a fill" and three critics returned it, because frames 7
and 8 contradict it in the plan and frame 2 contradicted it in the render, where the two leader
chips were solid accent behind cream type. The chips are bond now and the rule says what it
meant. Flag red is UNSPENT
and that is deliberate: the presentation was for information only, no comment window exists, and
a red would be false urgency.

**Five laws this deck is held to, each from a different director and each a refusal.**

1. **NOTHING IS DRAWN BROKEN.** No crack, no spall, no bent shaft, no alarm colour. ERCOT says
   the variations `can induce` and `potentially result in` fatigue or damage and nowhere says it
   has happened. A drawn crack would turn a could into a has.
2. **NO ROW OF THE TABLE IS COLOURED.** Colouring one row ranks three risks, a ranking is a
   severity ramp, and a severity ramp is a verdict a presentation marked for information only
   does not make. This is the Grid Watch bar-never-a-dial rule applied to a table.
3. **NO MEASURED SERIES IS DRAWN ANYWHERE.** The presentation's chart is captioned an actual data
   center in ERCOT and credits a preprint this run did not fetch. Frame 7 draws the declared
   LIMIT as the geometry it is and leaves the rest of the field empty ruled paper.
4. **NO COUNTY IS LIT.** The record names no Texas place. Frame 8 draws all the counties at one
   value and says so, rather than lighting Brazos from a campus address nothing sourced.
5. **NO MACHINE IS NAMED.** No plant, no nameplate, no capacity, no company, no county on any
   machine the deck draws.
