Four optical water sensors at varying heights.

They belong to a solar flood warning node UT San Antonio described on September 28th. A TinyML model on its own microcontroller reads them and judges imminent flood risk with no central server.

The release puts the danger at low-water crossings in San Antonio. Parts for one prototype cost roughly $150 to $220.

Pull back and it is AI that runs off the grid on a solar charge. UT Arlington is building a Hill Country early-warning system. Its researchers found standard radar can underestimate storm totals by roughly 20% under some conditions.

The release reports 98.82% validation accuracy in initial assessments after training. It names no site and no test against a real flood.

The deck walks one drawn crossing at night.
