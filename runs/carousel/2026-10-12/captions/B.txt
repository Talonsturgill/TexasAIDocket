A low-water crossing in San Antonio.

UT San Antonio's September 28th release says flood risk there often climbs block by block before regional alerts sound.

So Chen Pan's team put the judgment at the water. Four optical water-level sensors at varying heights sit on one solar powered node. TinyML on its microcontroller judges imminent flood risk with no central server. Parts for one prototype run roughly $150 to $220.

That puts the proof in real water. The release reports 98.82% validation accuracy in initial assessments after training. It names no site, no city or county running it and no field test against a real flood.

The slides follow one drawn crossing from the sensor rail to a pickup turning back.

No false alarm or missed flood figure is public. How many of each would a real storm season at one crossing turn up?
