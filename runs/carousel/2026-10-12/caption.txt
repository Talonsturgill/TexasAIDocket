A low-water crossing in San Antonio.

UT San Antonio's September 28th release says flood risk there often climbs block by block before regional alerts sound.

Chen Pan's team built a prototype that judges that risk on the device itself. Four optical water-level sensors at varying heights sit on one solar powered node. TinyML on its microcontroller judges imminent flood risk with no central server. Parts for one prototype run roughly $150 to $220.

A real flood would be the test. The release reports 98.82% validation accuracy in initial assessments after training. It names no site, no city or county running it and no false alarm or missed event figure. The team aims the design at small coastal towns.

The release holds a validation score and a parts cost. Where is the record of the node in a real flood?
