# Storyboard, 2026-10-25
# "Asked how much water they use, 233 never answered."

## The story, and what the fact check did to it

The Texas Water Development Board's water use survey goes to operating facilities that use more than 10 million
gallons a year (c38, c40), and state law requires every recipient to return it (c47, c46). Data centers are named among
the industrial users who must (c50). Records the Texas Tribune obtained for 2020 through 2025 identify 334 data center
facilities, of which 101 submitted at least once and 233 never submitted a requested survey (c27, c28, c29). Skipping it
is a Class C misdemeanor with a fine of up to $500 (c30, c31). The board says it has no authority to prosecute (c32),
local prosecutors decide (c33, c34), and state officials don't know whether any penalty has been issued (c35). The 97
facilities that did complete last year's survey reported 1.4 billion gallons together (c45). A bigger fine needs the
Legislature, which convenes in January (c36, c37).

The turn. ERCOT's notice of September 14th (c1) opened a request for information on data centers (c2). Its Section C
carries the water board's questions on water sources and consumption (c13), which ERCOT developed with the PUC and the
board (c12) and issues on the board's behalf (c14), forwarding the answers to the board (c15). It reaches developers of
proposed data centers from 25 MW (c3, c4) and Batch Zero loads not yet energized at 60 kV or above (c6, c7). Each
response was filed by the developer's own transmission or distribution provider (c21) with a notarized attestation
(c20), due 5 p.m. on October 12th (c19). Any failure to respond is reported to the PUC and the Office of the Governor
(c23), and ERCOT expects to publish a report by December 10th (c26).

The fact check moved four things, and every surface obeys them.
1. **Never "jointly".** ERCOT is the issuer. It coordinated Section C with the PUC and the board (c12) and the board's
   Executive Administrator authorized it (c14).
2. **Never "the same questions".** The notice says only that Section C seeks water sources and consumption (c13).
   The copy says the water board's questions.
3. **The two routes reach different facilities.** The survey reaches plants already operating (c40). The request
   reaches proposed projects and loads not yet energized (c4, c7). No surface implies the 233 are now covered.
4. **Nothing about the request cites the Tribune**, which never mentions it, and the December 10th report is an
   expected date, not a report that exists (c26).

## Why this treatment, and what was grafted

Director B's return plate is the spine. A gray cast iron plate with one threaded bore for each facility in the
Tribune's records, 334 of them. A brass hex pipe plug seals the 101 that returned a survey at least once and the 233
stay open. Under a low key a plug catches light and a bore swallows it, so the count reads as light before a word is
read, and an open bore is the honest drawing of an answer nobody has. It is one object at one scale, seen from nine
distances, which is the sibling's way: one industrial thing, close and large.

Grafted from Director C: the fact that the two routes reach different facilities, which became the deck's frame 7 and
its central caution. C's ten million gallon tank, drawn beside a data hall at true scale, so the survey's threshold has
a size, and C's 140 tanks in plan for the 1.4 billion gallons last year's answers reported. C's substation close crop
for the turn, the copper bus bar as the MW scale and the notarized attestation in relief. Grafted from Director A: the
stated alternation of image led and type led pages (planned and not built, see the rotation note), and the copy's
cautions on the fine (up to, never issued). Refused from A: the water meter as the hero, which cost three new kit models
for a deck whose data needs one, and the five bank notes, which read as a bribe. Refused from B: the concrete cube,
replaced by C's tank because a tank is what holds water. Refused from C: the pigeonhole case, two days after no. 58's
card drawer, and the courthouse, because no Texas place is in the claims and an unnamed courthouse asserts one.

## The world, and the laws that hold it

**LASTLIGHT, A STAGED WORLD, ON A WORKING YARD.** The chassis is `assets/js/deck/2026-10-25-returnplate.js` and every
frame loads it. It declares `sky: lastLight` with the key at azimuth -62, elevation 13: the last light low in the south
west, so on a camera at +z looking -z the key comes from the front left, every plug, flange and bushing has one lit edge
on its left, and every cast runs long to the right and away. A material relief reads the same light from the upper left
of its frame and its casts run down and to the right. +x is east and +z is south.

The argument for this world. The deck is about an answer that exists for some and not for others, and a staged pool puts
light only where something stands in it. A brass plug rises into the low key and an empty bore can't catch any, so the
light carries the count. Every recent deck stood in a building at night or on an apron. This one stands outdoors on
caliche, a casting on a yard, a tank in a field, a transformer against the seam.

1. **Start dark.** Near black sky, a warm seam on the horizon, caliche lit only where the pool lands.
2. **Type is light, on the dark field**, and every wash only darkens.
3. **Dust #C9B393, the brass of a returned survey, is the only accent** and means one thing: an answer returned. It
   appears on the brass plugs on frames 1, 2 and 9, and on frame 8's notary seal, struck in brass because a notarized
   response is an answer returned.
4. Every standing thing gets `TXT.contact`, every spatial frame `TXT.weather`, every frame `TXDECK.finish`.
5. **No logo, no wordmark, no likeness, no person.** The data hall is unbadged, the tank carries no name band, the
   substation no sign. The full length kit person reads as a mannequin, so nobody is in frame and the copy carries the
   prosecutors and the notary.
6. **No reflective water surface anywhere.** Water is shown as a volume held, never as a mirror.
