## The story (docket item tx-2026-0249, Bexar County, health-and-education)
UT Health San Antonio announced on November 5th, 2026 a one year proof of concept pilot, funded by a $200,000 grant from
the San Antonio Medical Foundation. Three leads: Rocio Norman (speech language pathologist, 18 years with veterans), Vivian
Ta-Johnson (psychology, natural language processing) and Alicia Swan (director of rehabilitation research, South Texas
Veterans Health Care System). Veterans with mild traumatic brain injury often keep their vocabulary and grammar but struggle
to find words, organise thoughts and keep up in fast or noisy conversation, even when standard tests show no clear
impairment. Most adult language tests were built for aphasia after a stroke or for severe injury. The study takes recordings
already collected at the San Antonio VA, where veterans described how they were injured and an important life event, at
several points during treatment. After transcription, natural language processing (a form of AI) is planned to measure
four named features: coherence, emotional tone, organization and level of detail. Today that analysis takes slow
transcription and coding, often by several reviewers. The tool is meant to support clinicians, not replace them. The first
question is whether an automated method can objectively find communication differences at all, and how they relate to
four named symptoms (anxiety, post-traumatic stress, depression, sleep difficulties). Norman hopes speech could become a
biomarker. If the pilot finds something meaningful, the team plans to seek larger federal grants. NO RESULTS EXIST YET.
