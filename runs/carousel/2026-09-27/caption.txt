Frisco is in this case two ways.

The complaint and the Justice Department's impact statement place Pinnacle Property Management Services there. Appendix A of the complaint lists Frisco as a market where it says aligned pricing harmed or is likely to harm renters.

Pinnacle licensed RealPage's AI Revenue Management. The complaint says it recommends a new price for every floor plan every night.

The proposed judgment isn't a ban on RealPage. What it cuts off is the data. Starting 180 days after the court enters its stipulation order, Pinnacle can't use pricing software fed by third-party nonpublic data or pooled across owners. It binds one landlord.

Comments go to the Justice Department's Antitrust Division within 60 days of the September 18th notice.

Should rivals' data be the line for every pricing algorithm in Texas, or only for one Frisco landlord?
