# Frame 3 concepts, "January brought $12 million for a clean room"

1. **The section cut.** A clean room sliced open like an architect's section model, seen straight on in a long lens: twelve fan filter units in one row across the ceiling, each cut to show its fan and pleated filter, pour columns of filtered air straight down through the work bay, through a perforated raised floor and into the sub-fab below. Only this beat asks the reader to learn what a clean room is, and the sealed fall of filtered air from ceiling to floor is the one thing that makes a room clean, so the twelve filters carry the exact figure as the room's own anatomy.

2. **The yellow bay.** A one point perspective from inside a lithography bay under amber safelight, the aisle running away under a ceiling of glowing filter panels, white tool faces and a perforated floor turned gold. It says "clean room" instantly, but the twelve could only be counted as ceiling panels shrinking into the distance, and the count gets lost in the perspective.

3. **The gowning room.** Twelve white bunny suits hanging in a row on pegs in a gowning room under hard white light, boots and hoods on the shelf above. It is the human face of a clean room and wholly unlike a bench, but the suits say "clean room staff" more than "a room built for semiconductor work", and a row of hanging objects is the old count-the-objects frame again.

**Pick: 1, the section cut.** It is the only one that shows how a clean room works, its count is the room's real structure (one filter per bay, one row), and as a cutaway diagram in a long lens it shares no world, camera or medium with a map of the state before it or a two halves frame after it.

**Composition with the type.** The building fills the frame edge to edge, roof near the top under the kicker, the twelve filters and the bay across the upper middle. The headline and dek sit bottom left inside the sub-fab, the dim navy level under the floor where the returning air goes, so the art makes its own quiet for the type and nothing is laid over the bay. Headline two lines, dek three.

**Dek's picture sentence, rewritten to describe this picture:** "Twelve ceiling filters, one for each million, drawn to illustrate." No new number, date, name or claim.
