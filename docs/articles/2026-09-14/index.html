The story

17 finished. None approved to energize.

Seventeen large loads have finished every process the grid operator runs and hold no approval to switch on. The reason is a questionnaire.

The half you can read names nobody.

Batch Zero Conditional Classification issued to Transmission and/or Distribution Service Providers
the Interconnecting Large Load Entity (ILLE) passing verification, the ILLE curing deficiencies in dynamic model data, or the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) granting ERCOT a good cause exception
Failure to satisfy any applicable condition may disqualify the Large Load from Batch Zero.

From c23 c24 c25. Each provider was told how its own loads were classified. This notice carries no name.

Count them. That is 6,608 megawatts.

A large load is 75 MW or greater. These seventeen have finished every process the grid operator runs and hold no approval to energize.

Each party finds a notary.

All information submitted will be treated as confidential to the extent permitted by law.

From c5 c6. A response is not complete until all three have filed a notarized attestation.

Ten business days, and it locks.

Three roles. Three different sets of questions.

The load entity, the distribution provider and the transmission provider each answer the sections their own role is given. Which sections a project sees depends on the classification it was handed.

Dickens County, because they said so.

The campus this record can name sits in the panhandle region of West Texas. The record knows that because the company published it, and not because any list did.

Filed one way. Classified another.
