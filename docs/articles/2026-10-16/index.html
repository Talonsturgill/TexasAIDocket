Anyone can ride Waymo to the San Antonio airport now. The flood fix is still listed in development

Waymo opened its San Antonio robotaxis to anyone with its app, the airport included. Its federal flood recall still lists the remedy as under development.

By Texas AI Docket October 16th, 2026

Read the story ↓

Waymo said that from October 14th, 2026 anyone with its app in San Antonio, San Diego and Tampa can hail a fully autonomous ride. San Antonio International Airport is included. The city's Director of Airports called the airport service a significant milestone in a statement Waymo published. The director said locals catching an early flight now have a new way to get to and from the airport.

The same week, the remedy field on the federal recall record for Waymo's flood defect still said the remedy is under development. The service opened to everyone while the permanent fix for driving into standing water was still listed as unfinished.

From invitation to anyone

On February 24th, 2026 Waymo said it was taking its first public riders in Dallas, Houston, San Antonio and Orlando. It invited them from the tens of thousands of people across those four cities who had downloaded its app. It said it would open the service to everyone later in the year.

Texas Public Radio reported that the San Antonio service began in a limited area of 60 square miles.

The creek and the recall

Texas Public Radio reported that a Waymo vehicle was swept into Salado Creek by floodwater during heavy rain on April 20th, 2026. The car had no one inside and nobody was hurt. Earlier that April another Waymo vehicle was stranded in floodwaters near McCullough Avenue and Contour Drive, the station reported.

NHTSA's recall record says the software may let a vehicle slow down and then drive into standing water on higher speed roads. It says entering a flooded road can cause a loss of vehicle control and raises the risk of a crash or injury. Texas Public Radio reported that the recall notice said the vehicle detected the flooded road and kept going at a reduced speed.

The recall covers certain 5th and 6th generation Waymo automated driving systems, and the record lists 3791 potentially affected units. Waymo LLC is the manufacturer on the campaign.

The pause, and what changed in the meantime

Texas Public Radio reported in May that Waymo had been operating in San Antonio that week without serving riders. The station called the flood pause the company's longest in the city to date, a period in which it reviewed its flood monitoring procedures and safety protocols. Waymo had said it would reexamine how its vehicles respond to flood prone areas and severe weather alerts before restarting service. On May 10th the station reported that Waymo was expected to resume service in San Antonio that week.

As an interim step Waymo narrowed its operations with tighter weather limits and updated its vehicle maps. It told NHTSA that every affected vehicle had received that interim update by April 20th, 2026.

What the record doesn't say yet

The record's remedy field, read on the reporting date, still describes the remedy as under development. It does not say when a permanent fix will arrive or what it will change about how the car behaves at the edge of standing water.

Texas Public Radio wrote that the rollout has raised broader questions about how self driving vehicles deal with emergency responders in severe weather.
