ExxonMobil has three automated drilling rigs in the Permian and plans more

ExxonMobil says three of its Permian drilling rigs now run with robotic pipe handling. It plans automated rigs on about half its Permian fleet by 2028.

By Texas AI Docket November 6th, 2026

Read the story ↓

ExxonMobil chief executive Darren Woods told investors on the company's third quarter earnings call that it has three automated drilling rigs in operation in the Permian. He said the company expects to expand them to about 25%% of its Permian fleet next year and to approximately half by 2028.

The company says mechanical arms and robotic systems can now do much of the heavy drill pipe handling. That work once put workers on the rig floor close to moving machinery. Bart Cahir, its senior vice president of unconventionals, told Reuters that about a third of significant injuries in well drilling happen on the rig floor.

What the machines do

Reuters reported that robotic arms now position columns of drill pipe over two stories tall and connect them to a drill string, work that crews would usually help with. It put the steel pipes at roughly 2,000 pounds. On one automated rig in Midland a gate with a Red Zone sign surrounds the drilling floor.

Reuters described a contractor in rural west Texas running the robotic machinery from a small office on the rig with controls on a screen. Employees on the rig work with ExxonMobil's central operations team in Houston to set the robot's precise movements.

How fast the plan is moving

In August Reuters reported that ExxonMobil operated more than 30 drilling rigs in the Permian, two of them automated. Its first automated rig came from the drilling contractor Helmerich & Payne. That rig drilled two miles horizontally in a little over six days, the third fastest drilling time in the company's history.

On October 7th, 2026 Helmerich & Payne said it expects to deploy seven more of its FlexRobotics systems for ExxonMobil over the next 12 months, for nine in operation. The ExxonMobil newsroom puts the goal as automating more than half of its unconventional Permian rigs by year end 2028. It calls automated rigs one of more than 50 technologies it is deploying in the Permian.

What the company has not shown

Woods said the rigs are significantly improving safety and delivering some of the strongest drilling performance in the fleet, and gave no figure for either. The company says it believes automation could eliminate roughly one-third of high-consequence drilling incidents. That is a stated potential. The call and the newsroom post print no incident count from the automated rigs.

ExxonMobil expects to keep similar staffing levels on automated rigs. The call and the newsroom post don't say how many people work on an automated rig today or how the work on the floor is divided now. The plan for 2028 is a company expectation and not a measured result.
