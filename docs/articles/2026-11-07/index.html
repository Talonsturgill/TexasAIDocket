Dallas AI cameras on brush trucks flag concerns, not violations

Cameras on Dallas sanitation trucks scanned 10,000 properties. A city memo counts 57 citations, issued only after an in-person inspection.

By Texas AI Docket November 7th, 2026

Read the story ↓

Dallas put cameras made by City Detect on its bulk trash trucks, and they scan homes and buildings for potential code violations. The city's own briefing to its Finance Committee says the cameras face sideways and capture images from the public right of way.

A city memo dated September 8th, 2026 says the cameras scanned 10,000 properties. It counts 57 citations at 34 properties.

How a flag becomes a citation

The software assigns blight scores that help Code Compliance decide which properties to review first. The memo says the citations came after camera detections were reviewed and confirmed by code officers. It says a citation is issued only after a code officer inspects the property in person and notifies the owner.

The city's briefing to its Finance Committee said the system is neither a staff replacement nor automated ticketing and that a person stays in the loop. It said faces and license plates are blurred.

Notices, cases and citations

The city sent courtesy notices to approximately 3,700 property owners after potential violations were detected. City officials say the notices are not citations or formal warnings and require no action, though they warn that a continuing violation could bring enforcement. Code Compliance also opened about 300 cases where officials believe an in-person inspection should come soon.

By the city manager's memo, 39 of the citations were issued in Council Districts 4, 8 and 7 in southern Dallas. Council member Chad West has raised concerns that the technology could put a disproportionate financial burden on neighborhoods with fewer resources.

The money

The three-year contract is estimated at $2,556,000, subject to annual City Council appropriations. It costs $852,000 a year, and the city may end it on 10 days' written notice. West proposed removing the program's funding and withdrew the request after the city manager's office agreed to brief the council.
