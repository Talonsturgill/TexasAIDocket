League City Proposition A, the November 3rd vote on Flock cameras

League City votes Tuesday on its Flock license plate cameras. The ballot calls the question nonbinding, yet the council has already directed removal if voters say no.

By Texas AI Docket October 31st, 2026

Read the story ↓

League City voters decide Proposition A in a special election on November 3rd, 2026. It asks whether the police department keeps using license plate reader cameras at fixed locations on public roadways. The Texas Tribune reported that the city's voters will be the first in Texas to decide whether to remove Flock cameras from city streets.

Harris County's sample ballot frames the question as a nonbinding statement of voter preference. The council put that word in itself, amending its motion before it ordered the election on August 11th, 2026. On October 13th, 2026 the council passed a resolution that directs removal of the fixed cameras if a majority rejects their continued use.

What the city leased

A League City resolution authorized a lease of 42 automated license plate reader cameras from Flock Safety, over two years at a cost not to exceed $233,400. The council approved it on its consent agenda. On December 12th, 2023 it approved an updated and restated agreement with Flock Group for 70 cameras, capped at $899,600.

The Tribune reported that the proposition asks about 55 Flock cameras along public roads. The lease names the cameras the city rents. The proposition asks about the cameras on public roads. The two numbers measure different things.

Who can search the data

The Tribune reported that League City lets more than 400 agencies access its Flock camera data. All of them are in Texas except the FBI, it reported from the city's transparency portal. Mayor Nick Long said only six officers have access to the city's system, and the Tribune reported that two officers must work together to scan the data.

The Tribune described the devices as AI-powered cameras that compile vehicle information into a database. It reported that the city has kept using them in the run-up to Election Day.

If the answer is no

Long said at the council meeting on October 13th, 2026 that no second vote is needed. He told the council the city will start bagging the cameras 72 hours after the vote is certified, the Tribune reported. Houston Public Media quoted him calling the resolution a commitment to take the cameras down within 72 hours.

Harris County's vote centers take voters from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Election Day. Kyle and Laredo plan to put the same question to their own voters in May, the Tribune reported.

What the record does not show

The Census Bureau lists League City in Galveston County as well as in Harris County. Galveston County's polling hours were not part of the record read here. The resolution sets what follows a no. It does not say when the count will be certified, so the day the cameras would start coming down is not yet known.
