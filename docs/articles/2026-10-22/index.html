Trained on short, popular posts, models skipped steps

Researchers at Texas A&M, UT Austin and Purdue trained four language models on doses of short, popular posts. Reasoning scores fell as the dose rose, and repair did not bring them back.

By Texas AI Docket October 22nd, 2026

Read the story ↓

Texas A&M Engineering News published the release on October 19th, 2026. The paper behind it lists its affiliations as Texas A&M University, the University of Texas at Austin and Purdue University. The arXiv listing marks it as published at COLM 2026. It calls itself a pilot study on Twitter/X.

The junk and control sets came from 1 million public Twitter/X posts. Junk meant posts under 30 tokens with popularity over 500. Control meant posts over 100 tokens with no popularity at all. Popularity is the total of a post's likes, retweets, replies and quotes. Each model was trained further on a mix of the two, then instruction tuned again.

What the doses did

The four models were Llama3 8B Instruct, Qwen2.5 7B Instruct, Qwen2.5 0.5B Instruct and Qwen3 4B Thinking 2507. All four showed some dose response. Llama3 8B Instruct was the most sensitive and Qwen3 4B the least.

On Llama3 8B Instruct, ARC Challenge with chain of thought fell from 57.2 to 83.7 as the junk share rose from none to all. The same model before any junk or control training scored 77.2. On a variable tracking test the fall was not steady. Half junk scored 59.4. The 20%% junk mix scored 60.7, with 22.4 at all junk.

The paper names thought skipping as the main reasoning failure, with models cutting their reasoning short or leaving it out. Its No Thinking category turned up in over 70% of failures across all cases and in 84% under the engagement measure's junk.

What repair did

The repair tests started from Llama3 8B Instruct after all junk training. Instruction tuning was scaled from 5k to 50k examples, the whole Alpaca set. That data came to 4.8 times the junk tokens and still did not fully undo the damage.

The best repaired models still trailed the baseline by 17.3 points on ARC Challenge with chain of thought. The paper says the damage persisted after tuning at the scales it tested. Shuo Xing, a lead author, said recovery stayed very hard even with higher quality data.

What the study can't say

The authors say the findings are specific to Twitter/X junk and the models they studied, and that other platforms remain an open question. The junk shares were doses in a training mix, not an estimate of how much of the internet is junk. The release says larger models such as ChatGPT or Gemini, which are not open source, still need testing.

Zhengzhong Tu said companies and researchers focus too much on model architectures and training resources, and that he wants more emphasis on data.
