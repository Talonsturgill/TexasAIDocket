Agreed to count AI fire detections, SPS counted cameras

Xcel Energy's Texas utility agreed to report AI camera fire detections under its approved plan. Its annual report answered that line with a count of cameras.

By Texas AI Docket September 28th, 2026

Read the story ↓

Southwestern Public Service Company operates as Xcel Energy, the Tribune reports. Its service area covers most of the Texas Panhandle, about 33,000 square miles by the utility's own study. Its resiliency plan called for AI cameras in its Tier 2 and Tier 3 wildfire risk areas.

The Public Utility Commission approved the utility's resiliency plan on July 10th, 2025. The order lists the number of artificial intelligence camera fire detections among the evaluation metrics for that plan. SPS will include the agreed evaluation metrics in its annual resiliency plan reports under the same order.

What the report put on that line

SPS filed its annual resiliency plan report on May 1st, 2026. Under the detections metric the report gives 33 cameras installed as the actual figure and 34 for 2026 as the projection. The line reports a count of cameras. It gives no count of detections.

The report states its own limits. SPS says the partial reporting year means it can't yet compare each measure's actual effectiveness with its projected effectiveness. Other lines on the same page do carry counts. For wildfire ignitions associated with overhead power lines the report gives a single actual figure of 24. It gives 26 as the actual total of wildfires reported by the National Interagency Fire Center in counties SPS serves.

What the cameras do

The plan application describes the system in plain terms. Each AI camera location carries two physical cameras that rotate to detect smoke. Data from the cameras is verified and forwarded by a human operator. SPS named Pano AI as its partner and said the system's fire detections are shared with SPS and first responders. SPS said it planned to install a total of 68 AI cameras in Tier 2 and Tier 3 wildfire risk areas.

What it costs

SPS budgeted $0 in 2025 capital for the AI camera project because the project was scheduled to start in 2026. It reports approximately $2M of actual capital spending on it in 2025 once the order was approved. For the current year SPS anticipates $10,537,400 of AI camera capital costs against $6,423,554 in the plan, which it calls a 64.04% change. It points in part to added system capabilities and expanded camera coverage in high-risk areas.

What the state asks of every utility

The rule behind these plans reaches far past one company. It applies to every electric utility, municipally owned utility and electric cooperative that owns a transmission or distribution facility in Texas. The staff blueprint for writing a plan treats cameras as optional. Its Situational Awareness section is marked Suggested and lists cameras or other monitoring technology as a tool. The PUCT said utilities are not required to use the blueprint. Pano AI had asked the commission to add a minimum requirement that plans describe their use of situational awareness solutions.

Few plans had arrived when lawmakers asked. Only eight of 160 utilities operating in fire-prone areas of Texas had complied with the wildfire mitigation plan law, the Tribune reports from a House State Affairs hearing.

What the record can't show

The report doesn't say whether the cameras detected a fire. It doesn't say they detected none either. A missing count isn't a count of zero. SPS says its 2027 annual report will carry a full year of data, and under the order that report carries the same line. The Tribune reports the company's interim president told lawmakers the company has deployed 97 wildfire detection cameras across its service territory. That count covers more than Texas.

Both documents are public on the PUCT Interchange. The order sits in Docket 57463 and the report in Project 57941. Under the state rule the deadline to intervene in a wildfire mitigation plan case is 30 calendar days from the date service of notice is complete.
