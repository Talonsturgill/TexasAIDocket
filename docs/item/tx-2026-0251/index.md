# UT Dallas researchers use satellites and machine learning to map air pollution against cancer in every Texas county

A team in UT Dallas's School of Economic, Political and Policy Sciences studied all 254 Texas counties. It paired CDC data on cancer prevalence with satellite readings of eight air pollutants taken in 2022 and used a machine learning method to analyze how the two line up. Counties with more pollution tended to have more cancer, and sulfur dioxide was the strongest environmental predictor. The east of the state had the worst air and the west the best. The authors say the result is an association between places, not proof that pollution causes cancer in any one person. They also say satellite readings may be less precise than ground monitors. The paper appeared online March 5th in Environmental Monitoring and Assessment, and UT Dallas announced it on November 6th, 2026.
