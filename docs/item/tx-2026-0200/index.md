# State education staff helped Alpha School's AI platform into pilots in Houston, Aldine and Fort Davis

Emails obtained by The Texas Tribune and ProPublica show Texas Education Agency staff helped connect Alpha School's AI learning platform with public school districts. Education Commissioner Mike Morath and his staff visited Alpha campuses, helped with a one page explainer for districts and connected Alpha associates with district leaders. The newsrooms report that at least 10 districts were approached and at least three launched pilots. Those three are Houston ISD, Fort Davis ISD in West Texas and Aldine ISD near Houston, and all three say they will use the software for supplemental learning. Fort Davis will use it in its K-8 classrooms for students who need help in reading and math. Aldine will deploy it to roughly 300 students at four middle schools who need support in math. Houston ISD says it plans to use the tool with students who have strong test scores and is not paying Alpha. It asked the attorney general to withhold the contract from the newsrooms. TEA says it has no formal partnership with Alpha and left the decision to the districts. The State Board of Education had voted down the Alpha leaders' bid for a Texas charter that would use the same technology. The board's own minutes from June 2025 record a veto of one charter proposed on a two hour learning model, Valenta Academy in Bastrop. Neither the minutes nor the reporting names Alpha as its applicant.
