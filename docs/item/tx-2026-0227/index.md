# UTEP and El Paso Water test whether a NASA backed AI model can map flooded streets from satellite images after a storm

The University of Texas at El Paso announced on October 8th, 2026 that a team led by Yong Je Kim has received nearly $500,000 from NASA. The money is for a two year project. It will combine satellite imagery, Earth observation data and artificial intelligence to map flooded areas of El Paso after storms. The team will use Prithvi-EO, an AI foundation model that reads the context around an area rather than single pixels. In El Paso wet pavement, building shadows and swimming pools can look like floodwater in a satellite image. Sentinel-1 radar fills in when clouds block the optical view. El Paso Water is the research partner and intended user, and the plan is a dashboard of flood maps, confidence information and infrastructure overlays. The team says the tool is not meant to replace crews on the ground. The project is expected to finish by June 2028, and no result has been published.
