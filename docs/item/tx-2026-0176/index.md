# The National Science Foundation funds a Texas A&M project to build AI digital twins of the water systems that fail first in a freeze

The National Science Foundation made a standard grant to the Texas A&M Engineering Experiment Station in College Station. The work is AI enabled digital twins of freshwater infrastructure under cascading hazards. The award record carries an award date of September 9th, 2026 and a start date of September 15th, 2026. It expires on August 31st, 2029. Sudipta Chowdhury is the principal investigator. The abstract describes water infrastructure that is getting harder to run. It names aging assets and deferred maintenance. It names climate driven hazards and power outages and water quality variability. A disruption that starts in one component can propagate through the connected networks. The abstract says current utility practice leans on monitoring systems and historical records and reactive emergency protocols. Those give limited ability to anticipate how a local anomaly escalates. The award sits in the foundation's Division of Civil, Mechanical, and Manufacturing Innovation.
