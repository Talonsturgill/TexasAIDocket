# Homeland Security tested facial biometric capture on pedestrians walking out of the country at the Progreso International Bridge

The Department of Homeland Security Science and Technology Directorate brought industry partners to the Progreso International Bridge in Hidalgo County to demonstrate facial biometric image capture for U.S. Customs and Border Protection. Customs and Border Protection asked for the work in October 2025. Six companies were invited to the Maryland Test Facility in early 2026 for basic capability demonstrations. Three solutions were selected for further data collection. Two came from NEC National Security Systems and one was a joint solution from Paravision and AiFi. A replica of the pedestrian exit environment was built at the Maryland Test Facility first. The partners then arrived at Progreso in May. Cameras were mounted on poles at different heights. Testing ran after dark as well as in daylight with external lighting configurations. The partners used artificial intelligence to construct a three dimensional digital environment of the image capture zone. They also used it to observe pedestrian movement and to analyze whether facial images were suitable for matching in the Traveler Verification Service. Signs in English and Spanish told pedestrians that United States citizens could opt out. An opt-out lane was provided. The biometric entry and exit mandate applies to travelers who are not United States citizens. The account published on September 1st, 2026 records what the agencies and the vendors said about the result. It publishes no accuracy figure and no count of images captured. It publishes no retention period and no deployment decision.
