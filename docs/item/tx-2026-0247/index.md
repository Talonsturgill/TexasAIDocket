# The state's chip fund gives Texas Tech over $19 million for an AI computing cluster in Lubbock that is meant to speed up semiconductor research

Texas Tech University said on November 13th, 2026 that its Whitacre College of Engineering received an additional award of over $19 million from the Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund. The money will help the university establish a next-generation computing cluster in Lubbock. The project will integrate artificial intelligence with high-fidelity simulation data and experimental results. The university says this should shorten development cycles for new semiconductor devices and materials. Planned work includes AI-driven materials discovery and AI-enabled epitaxial growth of ultrawide-bandgap semiconductors. Engineering dean Roland Faller says Texas Tech will be one of the first universities with access to Nvidia's newest generation of chips to power the cluster. The cluster is not built yet and the announcement gives no install date. The fund is run by the Texas CHIPS Office in the Office of the Governor. It gave the same college $12 million in January toward a clean room, which the university will match for a $24 million project. In May it gave roughly $4.5 million for wide and ultrawide bandgap research.
