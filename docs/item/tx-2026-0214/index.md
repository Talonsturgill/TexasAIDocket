# Federal trucking regulators let driverless trucks warn traffic with a cab beacon instead of triangles

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration granted Aurora and other carriers running Level 4 automated trucks a limited five year exemption. It covers the rules on placing warning triangles and flares around a stopped truck. A carrier that notifies the agency first may instead use warning beacons mounted on the cab. The agency found the beacons likely as safe as the triangles a driver would set out. The exemption runs from October 7th, 2026 to October 7th, 2031. The Texas Trucking Association is listed among supporters, and the Owner-Operator Independent Drivers Association among opponents. It applies on every Texas road a driverless truck uses.

## Evidence

Every fact above rests on one of these. The words are the source's own.

### The agency granted Aurora and other carriers running Level 4 automated trucks a limited five year exemption from the warning device rules.

> FMCSA announces its decision to grant a limited five-year exemption to Aurora Operations, Inc. (Aurora) and other motor carriers operating Level 4 automated driving system (ADS)-equipped commercial motor vehicles (CMVs), with prior notice to FMCSA, from compliance with the warning device placement requirements, the steady-burning lamp requirements, and the requirements for the types and number of warning devices.

Source (primary_official): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/pdf/2026-20734.pdf

### A carrier may use warning beacons mounted on the cab in place of traditional warning devices.

> This exemption permits motor carriers to use cab-mounted warning beacons in lieu of traditional warning devices on Level 4 ADS-equipped CMVs.

Source (primary_official): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/pdf/2026-20734.pdf

### The agency found the exemption likely as safe as the rule it replaces.

> The Agency has determined that granting the exemption would likely achieve a level of safety equivalent to or greater than the level of safety provided by the regulation.

Source (primary_official): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/pdf/2026-20734.pdf

### The exemption runs five years.

> The exemption is effective October 7, 2026 and expires October 7, 2031.

Source (primary_official): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/pdf/2026-20734.pdf

### The agency received 402 comments, and the Texas Trucking Association is listed among supporters.

> FMCSA received a total of 402 comments in response to Aurora’s application. Commenters who support the exemption include the Montana Trucking Association; the Nevada Trucking Association; the Texas Trucking Association;

Source (primary_official): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/pdf/2026-20734.pdf

### The owner operators' association said Aurora's studies do not account for the range of weather, road, lighting and traffic conditions trucks meet.

> OOIDA expresses concern that the two studies submitted with Aurora’s application do not account for the varying weather, road, lighting, and traffic conditions that CMVs encounter.

Source (primary_official): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/pdf/2026-20734.pdf
