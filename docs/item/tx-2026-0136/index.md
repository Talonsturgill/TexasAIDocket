# Federal investigators opened a defect evaluation into a driverless system after crashes in Dallas and Austin

The Office of Defects Investigation at the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened a preliminary evaluation into the Avride automated driving system. The resume gives the date it was opened as the sixth of May, 2026. It was prompted by crash reports filed under the agency's standing general order. The resume says the crashes happened in Dallas and in Austin. The failure report summary gives a crash total and a single alleged minor injury. Investigators reviewed video of every crash. The video shows vehicles changing lanes into other vehicles and failing to slow for what was ahead of them. A person sat in the driver's seat during every one of those crashes while the system drove. The office also records its understanding that the same vehicles carry paying passengers on a ride-hailing platform in Dallas. The document makes no finding of defect. Its strongest statement is that the system's performance may indicate inappropriate assertiveness and insufficient competence. The agency's own investigation status pages would not answer a request this run, so the record can't say what state the evaluation is in today. What it can say is that it was opened and that the document reaches no conclusion.
