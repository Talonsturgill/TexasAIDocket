# USDA's Kerrville laboratory builds AI models that pick where, when and how many sterile flies to release against New World screwworm

The USDA Agricultural Research Service says it is managing New World screwworm with predictive models. It says the models use advanced analytic tools and artificial intelligence to read ecological, environmental and historical information. USDA uses what the models show to choose the locations, timing and number of sterile flies to release. The models also guide where aircraft, fly traps and surveillance go. Kim Lohmeyer directs the agency's Knipling-Bushland Livestock Insects Research Laboratory in Kerrville and says the models already select areas for surveillance, trapping and sterile fly releases. Lohmeyer also says the models can't predict every scenario, especially people moving infested animals. KRGV reports USDA saying its drones have flown across Texas more than 2,000 times since June. The flights observed more than 36,000 animals and found no screwworm cases. USDA officials told the station the drones are not foolproof and use artificial intelligence to inspect animal wounds. Neither source gives an accuracy figure for the models or the drones. The Texas Animal Health Commission counts 43 premises in 17 Texas counties with a confirmed detection, the first in a Zavala County calf on June 3rd. The drones' count of no cases covers only what the flights observed. An agency page last modified July 31st says USDA's dispersal facility in Edinburg deploys more than 80 million sterile flies a week over Texas and Mexico. The commission keeps movement restrictions in place across an established infested zone.
