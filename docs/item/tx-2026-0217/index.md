# An MD Anderson AI model reads where T cells gather in tumor tissue, and points at mucin as a wall that may keep them out

Researchers at the University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center and collaborating Texas institutions published an AI model called SPACER in Nature Biomedical Engineering. It reads spatial maps of gene activity in intact tissue and looks at groups of neighboring cells to infer why immune cells gather in some regions and avoid others. The team ran it on 37 datasets and reports it was four to 21 times more effective than existing approaches on its benchmark tasks. Its leading finding is that mucins, sugar coated proteins many cancers overproduce, may form a physical barrier that keeps T cells away from tumors. In melanoma and gastric cancer datasets, tumors with more mucin went with poorer response to checkpoint drugs. The finding is an association in existing data. The researchers describe it as an idea to test in the lab, and the release reports no use of the tool in patient care.

### MD Anderson and collaborating Texas institutions built an AI model meant to explain why immune cells gather in some places in the body and avoid others.

> Researchers at The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center and collaborating institutions in Texas developed an artificial intelligence (AI) model that helps explain how immune cells find their way to specific locations in the body and why they avoid others.

### The model, called SPACER, reads spatial transcriptomics, which measures gene activity in intact tissue and records where each gene is active.

### It examines groups of neighboring cells rather than treating each cell alone.

### The team ran it on 37 datasets and reports it beat existing approaches by four to 21 times on its benchmark tasks.
