# National Science Foundation awards Texas A and M a national user facility where robots and artificial intelligence design and test new metal alloys

The National Science Foundation obligated a cooperative agreement to the Texas A and M Engineering Experiment Station for the Autonomous Robotic Metallurgist Materials Innovation Platform. The university calls it a six year, 24.9 million dollar grant. It runs from September 1st, 2026 to August 31st, 2032. The agency describes it as an open access national user facility that designs and makes and tests new alloys and learns from them. It does that by integrating robotics and artificial intelligence and physics based simulation and automated experimentation. The performance site of record is College Station and the principal investigator of record is Raymundo Arroyave.
